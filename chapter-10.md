# Chapter 10 — Same Building

Ha-eun woke up earlier than she expected.

For a few seconds, she didn't recognize the ceiling.

Then she remembered.

The apartment.

Her new apartment.

She stared at the ceiling.

"I'm still here."

A small smile appeared on her face.

She sat up and looked around.

There were still bags everywhere. She hadn't unpacked much the night before. She had been too tired.

She got out of bed and started sorting through her things.

There wasn't much.

A few clothes.

Some books.

A couple of small personal items.

She placed everything wherever it seemed to fit.

The apartment still looked empty, but it was starting to feel like hers.

After about twenty minutes, she looked around.

"Good enough."

She grabbed her phone.

She was already running late.

"Wait."

She checked the time.

Her eyes widened.

"I'm late!"

She rushed toward the door, then stopped.

She looked down at herself.

She was still wearing an oversized shirt.

"...Right."

Five minutes later, she ran out of the apartment properly dressed.

She locked the door and hurried toward the elevator.

She pressed the button.

The doors opened.

She stepped inside.

Then froze.

Someone was already there.

Ha-Kim.

He was standing in the corner in his usual black clothes, glasses on, his expression completely calm.

They stared at each other.

Ha-eun blinked.

Ha-Kim blinked.

Neither said anything.

The elevator doors started closing.

Ha-eun quickly stepped inside.

She looked at him.

Then at the floor.

Then back at him.

"...You."

"Yes."

"You live here."

"Yes."

She stared at him.

"Since when?"

"A while."

She narrowed her eyes.

"You really do live here."

"Yes."

She looked around the elevator, then at him again.

"You're the neighbor."

"Apparently."

"How did I not know?"

"You moved in yesterday."

"Right."

Silence.

The elevator began moving.

Ha-eun looked at the floor numbers.

Then at Ha-Kim.

Then back at the floor numbers.

She couldn't stop thinking about it.

The apartment.

The price.

The advertisement.

And now this.

She slowly looked at him.

"Did you know I was moving here?"

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"No."

She stared at him.

He stared back.

"Okay."

She looked away.

"I'm watching you."

"I noticed."

The elevator reached the first floor.

The doors opened.

Ha-eun walked out quickly.

Ha-Kim followed.

She stopped outside the building.

He walked past her.

"Have a good day."

She stared at his back.

"Wait."

He stopped.

"You're not going to explain anything?"

"About what?"

"This!"

She pointed at the building.

Ha-Kim looked at it.

"It's a building."

"I know it's a building!"

He waited.

She lowered her hand.

"Never mind."

He nodded.

"Okay."

Then he walked away.

Ha-eun stood there.

"...Why is talking to him so difficult?"

---

By the time they reached Haetae Cyber, Ha-eun had almost convinced herself it wasn't a big deal.

They were coworkers.

Now they were neighbors.

That was all.

It wasn't strange.

It wasn't—

She walked into the office.

Ji-woo waved.

"Morning!"

"Morning."

"You look tired."

"I discovered something terrible."

Ji-woo leaned closer.

"What?"

Ha-eun whispered.

"Ha-Kim lives in my building."

Ji-woo stared at her.

Then slowly smiled.

"Your trainer?"

"Yes."

"The quiet guy?"

"Yes."

"The guy who barely talks?"

"Yes."

Ji-woo covered her mouth.

"That's actually hilarious."

"It's not."

"It is."

"It really isn't."

Ji-woo smiled.

"Do you live on the same floor?"

Ha-eun paused.

"...Yes."

Ji-woo's eyes widened.

"No way."

"Yes."

She started laughing.

"Stop."

"I can't."

"It's not funny."

"It absolutely is."

Ha-eun crossed her arms.

"I don't see what's funny."

Ji-woo pointed toward the hallway.

"Because you're going to see him every day."

Ha-eun's expression slowly changed.

She hadn't thought about that.

"Oh no."

Ji-woo laughed harder.

---

At lunch, Ha-eun was still thinking about it.

She sat across from Ha-Kim.

Neither of them spoke.

She looked at him.

He ate quietly.

She looked at him again.

He looked up.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You've looked at me five times."

"You counted?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"You keep looking at me."

She looked away.

"I was thinking."

"About?"

"The building."

Ha-Kim continued eating.

"It's a building."

"I know."

Silence.

Then Ha-eun sighed.

"This is weird."

"What is?"

"You living there."

"You live there too."

"Exactly."

He looked at her.

"I don't see the problem."

"Of course you don't."

"Why?"

"Because you're weird."

He nodded.

"Fair."

She almost smiled.

Then she noticed something.

"Wait."

"What?"

"You said fair."

"Yes."

"You're admitting you're weird?"

"I've been called worse."

Ha-eun laughed quietly.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

For a second, he smiled too.

Then he went back to eating.

---

That evening, Ha-eun returned home after work.

She entered the elevator.

Nobody was inside.

"Finally."

The doors started closing.

A hand stopped them.

Ha-eun looked up.

Ha-Kim stepped inside.

She stared at him.

He stared at her.

Neither moved.

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Of course."

The doors closed.

The elevator started moving.

Ha-eun looked at the floor numbers.

Ha-Kim stood beside her.

Then the elevator stopped.

A woman entered.

She looked at both of them.

Then at the floor numbers.

Then back at them.

"Neighbors?"

Ha-eun nodded.

"Yes."

Ha-Kim nodded too.

"Yes."

The woman smiled.

"You two work together?"

Ha-eun answered immediately.

"Yes."

Ha-Kim answered at the same time.

"No."

Ha-eun turned toward him.

"What?"

He looked at her.

"You work at the company."

"You work there too!"

"Yes."

"So we're coworkers!"

He thought for a second.

"Technically."

The woman looked confused.

Ha-eun sighed.

"Don't mind him."

The elevator reached their floor.

Ha-eun stepped out.

Ha-Kim followed.

They walked in opposite directions.

Then Ha-eun stopped.

She looked at him.

"Wait."

He stopped.

"Yeah?"

"Good night."

He looked slightly surprised.

Then nodded.

"Good night."

She smiled.

Then entered her apartment.

Ha-Kim entered his.

For a few seconds, both stood on opposite sides of the wall.

Ha-eun looked around her apartment.

Ha-Kim looked around his.

Neither said anything.

Then Ha-eun quietly laughed.

"This is going to be annoying."

Across the wall, Ha-Kim sat down.

He looked toward the wall.

A small smile appeared on his face.

"Probably."

---

The next morning, Ha-eun opened her door.

At the exact same time, Ha-Kim opened his.

They both stopped.

They stared at each other.

Ha-eun sighed.

"Again?"

Ha-Kim nodded.

"Again."

She locked her door.

He locked his.

They walked toward the elevator together.

Neither spoke.

Then Ha-eun looked at him.

"You know, we're probably going to keep doing this."

"Yes."

"Every morning."

"Probably."

She smiled.

"That's going to be annoying."

Ha-Kim pressed the elevator button.

"Probably."

The elevator doors opened.

They stepped inside together.

For some reason, neither of them minded anymore.
