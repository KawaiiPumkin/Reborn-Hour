# Chapter 17 — You Deserve It

Ha-eun had started noticing something.

Min-seo kept showing up.

Not every day.
Not enough for anyone else to care.
But enough for Ha-eun to notice.

Sometimes she appeared near Haetae after work.
Sometimes Ha-Kim's phone lit up with her name.
Once, Ha-eun saw her waiting near the entrance while she was leaving with Ji-woo.

Min-seo noticed Ha-Kim almost immediately.

“Ha-Kim.”

He stopped.

Ha-eun stopped too.

Min-seo walked toward them.

“You're leaving?”

“Yes.”

“I was hoping we could talk.”

Ha-Kim glanced at Ha-eun.

She immediately smiled.

“It's okay. I was going home anyway.”

“I didn't say you had to leave.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun looked at Ji-woo.

“Come on.”

Ji-woo looked between all three of them.

She clearly wanted to ask something.

Ha-eun grabbed her arm.

“Come on.”

“Okay, okay.”

They walked away.

Ha-eun didn't look back.

At least, not immediately.

When they reached the corner, she did.

Min-seo was standing beside Ha-Kim.

Talking.

Ha-Kim was listening.

Ha-eun quickly faced forward again.

Ji-woo looked at her.

“Who was that?”

“Someone Ha-Kim knows.”

“Friend?”

“I don't know.”

Ji-woo looked behind them.

Then at Ha-eun.

“Oh.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Why did you say it like that?”

“Like what?”

“Like you know something.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“I don't.”

“You're annoying.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun kept walking.

She tried not to think about it.

She failed.

───

That night, Ha-eun lay in bed staring at the ceiling.

Her apartment was quiet.

Too quiet.

Usually around this time, she would be across the hall.

Sometimes eating.
Sometimes talking.
Sometimes doing absolutely nothing while Ha-Kim worked.

Tonight she had gone straight home.

She turned onto her side.

Then onto the other.

Then onto her back again.

“Why do I care?”

No answer.

She pulled the blanket higher.

Min-seo had known him for years.

That shouldn't matter.

Ha-Kim had a life before meeting her.

Obviously.

Ha-eun knew that.

So why did seeing Min-seo beside him bother her?

She closed her eyes.

Immediately, she remembered Ha-Kim.

Not Min-seo.

Ha-Kim.

Cooking while she sat at his table.
Telling her not to apologize every five seconds.
Noticing when she hadn't eaten.
Giving her his jacket in the rain.
Letting her sit in his apartment even when neither of them had anything to say.
The tiny smile he sometimes made because of her.
His messy hair in the morning.
His dark green eyes behind his glasses.
His voice when he said her name.

Ha-eun's eyes opened.

Her face slowly became warm.

“No.”

She rolled over.

A few seconds passed.

Then she sat up.

“No way.”

Her heart was beating faster.

She covered her face.

“Oh my God.”

It suddenly felt painfully obvious.

Why she kept finding reasons to knock on his door.
Why eating with him felt better than eating alone.
Why she noticed when he looked tired.
Why his fever had worried her so much.
Why she liked making him smile.
Why Min-seo bothered her.

Ha-eun slowly lowered her hands.

“I like him.”

The apartment remained silent.

She stared ahead.

“I actually like him.”

Saying it twice somehow made it worse.

She fell backward onto the pillow and pulled the blanket over her face.

“This is bad.”

But underneath the blanket...

She was smiling.

───

The smile didn't last forever.

Because another thought came.

Min-seo.

Ha-eun lowered the blanket.

She remembered the woman standing beside Ha-Kim.

Confident.
Beautiful.
Comfortable around him.
Someone who knew him before Ha-eun even knew he existed.

Then Ha-eun looked around her own apartment.

Her eyes moved toward the clothes hanging near the corner.

Nothing expensive.
Nothing special.

She thought about university.
About conversations at work she sometimes struggled to follow.
About the parts of her childhood she hoped nobody would ever ask about.
About everything she still hadn't told Ha-Kim.

The happiness in her chest became quieter.

“What am I doing?”

She hugged the blanket.

Someone like Min-seo made sense beside him.

Ha-eun wasn't sure she did.

───

The next few days only made it harder.

Min-seo didn't disappear.

She wasn't rude to Ha-eun.
She didn't insult her.
She didn't even do anything Ha-eun could properly be angry about.

She simply kept trying to be part of Ha-Kim's life again.

And somehow, that was worse.

One afternoon, Ha-eun was leaving the break room when she saw Min-seo near the lobby.

Again.

Ha-Kim stood nearby.

Min-seo said something.

Ha-Kim answered.

She smiled.

Ha-eun slowed down.

Then continued walking.

She hated the feeling in her chest.

It wasn't anger.

It wasn't even really about Min-seo.

It was fear.

Because now Ha-eun knew what she wanted.

And suddenly, she could lose something she had never even had.

───

Ha-Kim noticed.

Of course he did.

Ha-eun still spoke to him.
Still smiled.
Still came over.
But less.

She hadn't appeared at his door for two nights.

On the third evening, Ha-Kim knocked on hers.

Ha-eun opened the door.

She froze.

“Oh.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“Oh?”

“I wasn't expecting you.”

“I noticed.”

She stepped aside.

“Come in.”

Ha-Kim entered.

It still felt strange seeing him in her apartment instead of the other way around.

Ha-eun closed the door.

“Do you want something to drink?”

“No.”

“Food?”

“No.”

“Then why are you here?”

“You haven't come over.”

Ha-eun stopped.

“What?”

“You haven't come over.”

“I heard you.”

“Then why did you ask?”

She stared at him.

“Why are you like this?”

“You've asked me that before.”

Ha-eun almost smiled.

Almost.

Ha-Kim noticed.

He sat down.

Ha-eun remained standing for a moment before sitting across from him.

Silence.

Then Ha-Kim spoke.

“What's wrong?”

“Nothing.”

“You're bad at lying.”

“You say that a lot.”

“Because you lie badly a lot.”

She frowned.

“I'm fine.”

Ha-Kim simply looked at her.

Ha-eun lasted about five seconds.

Then she looked away.

“Stop.”

“I'm not doing anything.”

“You're looking at me.”

“Yes.”

“Stop.”

“No.”

She grabbed a cushion and held it against herself.

“You're annoying.”

“I know.”

Another silence.

Ha-eun stared at the cushion.

Then quietly asked,

“Do you still like Min-seo?”

Ha-Kim's expression changed slightly.

There it was.

He finally understood.

Ha-eun immediately regretted asking.

“Forget it.”

“No.”

“Ha-Kim.”

“You asked.”

“I shouldn't have.”

He was quiet for a moment.

Then answered.

“I care about her.”

Ha-eun's fingers tightened around the cushion.

“She was important to me.”

Ha-eun nodded.

“Okay.”

“But that isn't what you asked.”

She looked at him.

Ha-Kim continued.

“No.”

Her eyes stayed on him.

“No?”

“I don't want to be with her.”

Ha-eun didn't know what to say.

Ha-Kim watched her.

“Is that why you've been avoiding me?”

“I wasn't avoiding you.”

“You live across the hall.”

“That doesn't mean I have to visit you every day.”

“You almost do.”

Her face warmed.

“That is not the point.”

“Then what's the point?”

Ha-eun opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

She looked away again.

“I don't know.”

Ha-Kim waited.

That somehow made it worse.

He wasn't forcing her.

He wasn't guessing for her.

He was simply waiting.

Ha-eun swallowed.

“I think…”

She stopped.

Her heart was beating ridiculously hard.

“I think I got jealous.”

Silence.

Ha-eun wanted the floor to open.

“Don't look at me.”

“You told me to stop doing that earlier.”

“Yes.”

“I said no.”

“Ha-Kim.”

The corner of his mouth moved.

She threw the cushion at him.

He caught it.

“Don't smile!”

“I'm not.”

“You are!”

“Barely.”

“That still counts.”

He placed the cushion beside him.

Ha-eun covered her face.

“This is embarrassing.”

“Why?”

“Because it is.”

“It doesn't have to be.”

She lowered her hands.

“Easy for you to say.”

Ha-Kim became quiet again.

Ha-eun looked at him.

Then she finally said what she had been trying not to admit.

“I like you.”

There.

She said it.

Her stomach dropped immediately.

Ha-eun stared at the floor.

“I didn't know before.”

Ha-Kim didn't move.

“I mean... maybe I did. I just didn't realize it.”

Her words started coming faster.

“I kept thinking I just liked being around you because you're nice to me, and you cook, and I don't hate being quiet around you, and then Min-seo showed up and I started getting annoyed for no reason and then I realized there was actually a reason and—”

“Ha-eun.”

She stopped.

Ha-Kim looked directly at her.

“I like you too.”

Her mind went completely blank.

“What?”

“I like you.”

She stared.

Ha-Kim's expression remained calm.

But his ears were slightly red.

Ha-eun noticed immediately.

Her eyes widened.

“Your ears are red.”

He adjusted his glasses.

“That's irrelevant.”

“No, it's not.”

“It is.”

“You're embarrassed.”

“So are you.”

“That's different.”

“How?”

“I don't know.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Ha-eun smiled.

A small, nervous smile.

But the insecurity didn't disappear completely.

She looked down again.

“Why?”

Ha-Kim frowned slightly.

“Why what?”

“Why me?”

He didn't answer immediately.

Ha-eun rubbed her thumb against her fingers.

“You could probably be with someone much better.”

“Better?”

“You know what I mean.”

“No.”

She sighed.

“Someone like Min-seo.”

Ha-Kim's expression became more serious.

“Don't compare yourself to her.”

“I'm not trying to.”

“You are.”

Ha-eun looked down.

“She's beautiful.”

“So are you.”

Ha-eun froze.

Her head slowly lifted.

Ha-Kim seemed completely serious.

Her face went red.

“You can't just say that.”

“Why?”

“Because…”

She had no answer.

Ha-Kim continued.

“You don't need to be her.”

Ha-eun stayed quiet.

“You don't need a degree.”

Her eyes softened.

“You don't need expensive clothes.”

She looked down at her shirt.

“You don't need to know everything.”

He paused.

“And you don't need to be perfect.”

Ha-eun's eyes began to sting slightly.

She hated that.

She quickly blinked.

Ha-Kim leaned forward.

“I like being around you.”

She looked at him.

“I like when you come over.”

Her lips parted slightly.

“I like eating with you.”

A tiny smile appeared on her face.

“I like hearing you complain.”

The smile disappeared.

“Hey.”

“You complain a lot.”

“This was supposed to be nice.”

“It is.”

She tried not to smile again.

Failed.

Ha-Kim's voice became quieter.

“And when you're not there…”

He looked away for the first time.

“The apartment feels too quiet.”

Ha-eun stopped smiling.

Not because she was upset.

Because that one hurt somewhere much deeper.

The apartment that had once been completely silent.
The apartment where Ha-Kim had lived alone.

Somehow, she had become part of it.

Ha-eun looked down.

“You really mean that?”

“Yes.”

She swallowed.

“But someone like me…”

“Ha-eun.”

She looked at him.

“You deserve to be wanted.”

Her eyes widened slightly.

“You deserve good things without having to prove you're good enough for them.”

Ha-eun didn't speak.

For years, almost everything in her life had felt temporary.

Food.
Money.
Homes.
Safety.
People.

She had learned not to expect too much.

Expecting things made losing them worse.

But Ha-Kim was sitting in front of her now.

Not asking about where she came from.
Not asking her to become someone better first.

Just choosing her.

Ha-eun wiped quickly beneath one eye.

“I'm not crying.”

“I didn't say you were.”

“Good.”

She took a breath.

Then looked at him.

“So…”

“So?”

Her cheeks became warm again.

“What are we?”

Ha-Kim stared at her.

Ha-eun immediately regretted the question.

“I mean, we both said we like each other, so…”

She looked away.

“Does that mean…”

Ha-Kim stood.

Ha-eun looked up at him.

He held out his hand.

“Will you date me?”

She stared at his hand.

Then at him.

“You're asking like it's a work contract.”

“I don't know how else to ask.”

A laugh escaped her.

Ha-Kim waited.

Ha-eun placed her hand in his.

“Yes.”

His fingers closed gently around hers.

Her heart jumped.

She looked at their hands.

Then back at him.

“I'm your girlfriend now?”

“Yes.”

She smiled.

A huge, genuine smile she couldn't hide.

“And you're my boyfriend?”

“That is generally how it works.”

“Don't ruin it.”

“I wasn't.”

“You were.”

───

A little later, Ha-eun was back where she usually ended up.

Ha-Kim's apartment.

Except now everything felt different.

Nothing had actually changed.

Same couch.
Same table.
Same kitchen.
Same quiet man standing in front of the stove.

But Ha-eun sat at the table staring at him like she had never been there before.

Ha-Kim looked over his shoulder.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You're staring again.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“I can stare now.”

“Why?”

“I'm your girlfriend.”

“That doesn't explain anything.”

“It explains everything.”

He turned back to the stove.

Ha-eun rested her chin in her hands.

“My boyfriend is cooking for me.”

Ha-Kim stopped moving for half a second.

Ha-eun noticed.

Her smile grew.

“You heard that.”

“Yes.”

“Boyfriend.”

“Ha-eun.”

“Boyfriend.”

He turned toward her.

“Do you want food?”

“Yes.”

“Then stop.”

She covered her mouth to hide her smile.

“Okay.”

A few seconds passed.

“Boyfriend.”

Ha-Kim sighed.

───

Dinner disappeared faster than either of them expected.

Afterward, neither seemed ready to end the night.

Ha-eun sat beside Ha-Kim on the couch.

Closer than usual.

Their shoulders touched.

Outside the large window, Seoul had gone dark.

Buildings glowed in the distance.

The apartment lights were off except for a small lamp near the kitchen.

Moonlight came through the window, stretching softly across the floor.

Ha-eun looked down.

Their hands rested close together on the couch.

Almost touching.

She moved one finger slightly.

Her little finger touched his.

Ha-Kim looked down.

Ha-eun immediately looked toward the window.

He moved his hand.

For one horrible second, she thought he was moving it away.

Instead, his fingers slipped between hers.

Ha-eun's breath caught.

She looked down.

Then at him.

Ha-Kim was already looking at her.

Neither spoke.

Ha-eun suddenly became aware of everything.

How close they were.
His hand around hers.
The quiet apartment.
The moonlight catching the side of his face.
His dark green eyes behind his glasses.

Her heart started beating faster.

Ha-Kim's gaze moved briefly toward her lips.

Then back to her eyes.

Ha-eun noticed.

Her face became hot.

Neither moved.

Then Ha-eun leaned closer.

Only a little.

Ha-Kim stopped.

He gave her enough time to move away.

She didn't.

His free hand rose slowly.

He touched the side of her face.

Ha-eun's eyes softened.

Their foreheads almost touched.

“Ha-eun.”

“Hm?”

He hesitated.

“Can I?”

Her heart nearly stopped.

She nodded.

“Yes.”

Ha-Kim leaned closer.

Their lips met.

Softly.

Ha-eun's fingers tightened around his hand.

For a few seconds, nothing else existed.

No Min-seo.
No work.
No past.
No questions about tomorrow.

Just warmth.

Then they slowly pulled apart.

Ha-eun stared at him.

Ha-Kim stared back.

Silence.

Her entire face turned red.

She immediately hid against his shoulder.

Ha-Kim froze.

“Don't look at me.”

“I'm not.”

“You were.”

“You were looking at me too.”

“That's different.”

“How?”

“I don't know.”

Ha-Kim's shoulders moved slightly.

Ha-eun lifted her head.

“Did you just laugh?”

“No.”

“You did.”

“No.”

She stared at him suspiciously.

Then she noticed something.

His ears were red again.

Ha-eun smiled.

“You're embarrassed too.”

Ha-Kim looked away.

“No.”

“You're terrible at lying.”

He looked back at her.

“You've been spending too much time with me.”

“Maybe.”

She rested her head against his shoulder again.

For a while, neither spoke.

Ha-eun listened to his breathing.

She could feel his thumb moving slowly against the back of her hand.

A small smile stayed on her face.

Then she whispered,

“Ha-Kim.”

“Hm?”

“You can do it again.”

His hand stopped.

Ha-eun immediately regretted having a mouth.

She buried her face against his shoulder.

“Never mind.”

Ha-Kim gently lifted his hand to her cheek.

Ha-eun looked at him.

“Are you sure?”

She hesitated only because she was embarrassed.

Then nodded.

This time, she moved first.

The second kiss lasted a little longer.

Still gentle.
Still nervous.

But neither pulled away as quickly.

When it ended, Ha-eun stayed close.

Their foreheads rested against each other.

She smiled.

“So…”

Ha-Kim waited.

“Tomorrow too?”

He understood.

Just like always.

“Tomorrow.”

Ha-eun's smile grew.

Outside, Seoul continued moving beneath the moonlight.

Inside, the apartment was quiet.

But for once, neither of them thought the silence felt empty.
