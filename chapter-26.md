Chapter 26 — The Black Figure
Rain tapped softly against the apartment windows.
It wasn't heavy.
Just enough to make the city outside look slightly blurred.
Ha-eun was asleep beside him.
One arm rested across his chest, her fingers loosely curled into his shirt.
Ha-Kim was awake.
He stared at the ceiling.
He hadn't been sleeping well lately.
Not because of work.
Not because of Ha-eun.
If anything, life had become quieter since they started living together.
Too quiet sometimes.
He could still remember what the apartment had looked like before she moved in.
Dark curtains.
Empty walls.
A couch he barely used.
A kitchen that looked untouched most days.
Now there was a blanket thrown over the back of the couch.
A small plant near the window.
Her slippers beside his.
A ceramic cat sitting on one of the shelves.
There were things everywhere.
Small things.
Normal things.
He liked them more than he admitted.
Ha-eun shifted beside him.
"Mmm..."
Ha-Kim looked down.
She opened one eye.
"You're awake."
"Apparently."
"What time is it?"
He checked his phone.
"Six forty."
She immediately closed her eye again.
"Too early."
"You asked."
"I didn't ask."
"You literally did."
She pulled the blanket higher.
"Go away."
"This is my bed."
"Then I'll steal it."
Ha-Kim looked at her for a moment.
"You already stole half of it."
"I need more."
"You don't."
She opened her eyes again.
"You're annoying in the morning."
"I'm like this all day."
"I know."
A tiny smile appeared on her face.
Then she closed her eyes again.
Ha-Kim watched her for another moment before turning away.
For a few seconds, everything was normal.
Warm.
Quiet.
Safe.
Then—
something changed.
His vision went black.
Ha-Kim's body froze.
The bedroom disappeared.
───
Rain.
Heavy rain.
Not the soft sound from the window.
A storm.
Water poured across an empty street.
Streetlights flickered through the darkness.
Ha-Kim stood somewhere he couldn't recognize.
He looked down.
There was no ground beneath him.
No body.
No hands.
Nothing.
Only darkness.
Then he heard footsteps.
Slow.
Heavy.
Coming closer.
Ha-Kim turned.
A figure stood at the end of the street.
Completely black.
Not wearing black.
Not standing in a shadow.
Black.
Like the shape itself had been cut out of the world.
Ha-Kim stared.
The figure didn't move.
Then something appeared behind it.
Another shape.
Smaller.
Ha-Kim's breathing stopped.
He knew that silhouette.
Small frame.
Shoulder-length hair.
The way she stood.
The way she tilted her head.
"No..."
The smaller figure took a step backward.
Ha-Kim tried to move.
Nothing happened.
He couldn't reach her.
Couldn't speak.
The black figure stepped forward.
One step.
Then another.
The rain became louder.
Ha-Kim's heart began pounding.
"Ha-eun."
The smaller figure looked up.
He couldn't see her face.
But he knew.
It was her.
The black figure raised one arm.
Ha-Kim felt something inside him twist.
"No!"
His voice finally came out.
The figure stopped.
For one second, everything went silent.
Then he heard a voice.
Distorted.
Distant.
Almost human.
"...too late."
Ha-Kim stared.
The black figure moved.
Ha-eun disappeared behind it.
A sharp metallic sound cut through the rain.
Then—
nothing.
The world went completely black.
───
Ha-Kim woke violently.
He shot upright.
His breathing was ragged.
His chest hurt.
For several seconds, he didn't know where he was.
His hands were shaking.
The room slowly came back.
The bed.
The curtains.
The faint morning light.
Ha-eun.
She was sitting beside him now.
Her hair was messy.
Her eyes were wide.
"Ha-Kim?"
He stared at her.
She reached toward him.
"Hey."
He flinched before he could stop himself.
Ha-eun froze.
"What's wrong?"
He looked at her hand.
Then her face.
She was alive.
She was right there.
His breathing slowly began to settle.
"Nothing."
Her eyebrows pulled together.
"That didn't look like nothing."
"I'm fine."
"You were shaking."
He looked away.
"I had a bad dream."
Ha-eun studied him.
"A really bad one?"
He didn't answer.
She moved closer.
"Do you want to tell me?"
"No."
The answer came too quickly.
Ha-eun noticed.
But she didn't push.
Instead, she placed her hand over his.
His fingers were still cold.
"You don't have to."
Ha-Kim looked at their hands.
"Thank you."
She gave him a small smile.
"You're welcome."
For a while, neither spoke.
Then Ha-eun leaned against his shoulder.
"Was I in it?"
Ha-Kim went still.
He turned toward her.
"Why would you ask that?"
"You keep looking at me."
He hadn't noticed.
She smiled faintly.
"You're doing it again."
Ha-Kim looked away.
"I just..."
He stopped.
There was no way to explain it.
He couldn't tell her that he'd watched a black figure stand over her.
Couldn't tell her that he'd heard a voice say he was too late.
Couldn't tell her that, for the first time, the strange visions felt less like meaningless images and more like something waiting for them.
So he said the only thing he could.
"Just making sure you're here."
Ha-eun blinked.
Then her expression softened.
"I'm here."
She squeezed his hand.
"See?"
He looked at her.
She was smiling.
Small.
Sleepy.
Completely alive.
Ha-Kim nodded.
"Yeah."
───
He didn't go to work immediately.
He sat at the kitchen table with a cup of coffee in front of him.
He hadn't touched it.
Ha-eun was making breakfast.
The smell of toasted bread filled the apartment.
"You've been staring at that coffee for ten minutes."
"I know."
"Are you going to drink it?"
"Eventually."
"It'll be cold."
"I'll drink it cold."
She turned around.
"Why?"
"I don't know."
Ha-eun stared at him.
"You really are weird today."
He looked at her.
"Today?"
She smiled.
"Fair."
She placed a plate in front of him.
"Eat."
He looked down.
Toast.
Eggs.
A few pieces of fruit.
Nothing special.
But he suddenly felt his chest tighten.
He had seen her die.
At least—
he thought he had.
He didn't know if it was the future.
He didn't know when it would happen.
He didn't even know if the vision followed the rules of his ability.
But the fear remained.
Ha-eun sat across from him.
"You're doing it again."
"What?"
"Looking at me."
He lowered his eyes.
"Sorry."
She tilted her head.
"You've said that three times this morning."
"Have I?"
"Yes."
She picked up a piece of toast.
"You're worrying me."
Ha-Kim was quiet.
Then he said:
"I'll be fine."
Ha-eun looked at him.
"I wasn't asking about you."
He paused.
She pointed at him with the toast.
"You're allowed to tell me when something is wrong."
"I know."
"You don't have to solve everything alone."
He didn't answer.
She lowered the toast.
"Ha-Kim."
He finally looked at her.
"I'm not asking you to tell me everything."
Her voice softened.
"Just don't disappear into your head."
For a moment, he couldn't speak.
Then he nodded.
"I'll try."
"Good."
She smiled.
"Now eat."
He picked up his fork.
"You're bossy."
"You're slow."
"That's not an answer."
"It wasn't supposed to be."
He almost smiled.
Almost.
───
Later that morning, Ha-Kim stood alone in the bathroom.
He stared at himself in the mirror.
Dark green eyes.
Glasses.
Messy hair.
Same face.
Nothing looked different.
He splashed water onto his face.
Then looked up again.
He closed his eyes.
He didn't activate his ability.
He didn't want to.
Not yet.
The normal visions had always been clear.
An hour.
A countdown.
Events unfolding like a film.
He knew how they worked.
The strange visions were different.
No countdown.
No control.
No certainty.
And now—
a black figure.
Ha-eun.
A voice.
Too late.
He opened his eyes.
His reflection stared back.
"What are you?"
The room stayed silent.
He knew better than to expect an answer.
Still, the question remained.
What were these visions?
Were they showing the future?
A warning?
A possibility?
Something else entirely?
He didn't know.
But he knew one thing now.
The visions weren't meaningless.
The black figure hadn't felt like a dream.
It had felt like something real.
Something that existed somewhere beyond what he could see.
And if Ha-eun was connected to it...
Then whatever was coming wasn't only his problem anymore.
───
Ha-Kim returned to the kitchen.
Ha-eun was putting dishes away.
She looked over.
"Better?"
"A little."
"Good."
She smiled.
Then walked past him.
Their shoulders brushed.
Nothing happened.
No vision.
No darkness.
No warning.
Just Ha-eun.
Just the apartment.
Just another morning.
Ha-Kim turned and watched her for a moment.
She opened a cabinet.
"Why are you staring again?"
He looked away.
"Nothing."
She narrowed her eyes.
"You're terrible at lying."
"I know."
She smiled.
"At least you know."
Ha-Kim stood there quietly.
Outside, the rain continued falling.
He looked toward the window.
For a moment, he remembered the black figure.
The empty street.
The rain.
Her silhouette.
And those two words.
Too late.
His fingers slowly curled into a fist.
Whatever that vision was—
he would find out.
Before it happened.
Before anyone died.
Before he became too late.
