# Chapter 25 — The Past Doesn’t Deserve Your Death
Morning sunlight slipped through the curtains.
Ha-eun was still asleep.
Ha-Kim stood in the kitchen, waiting for the kettle to boil.
The apartment was quiet except for the soft hum of the refrigerator and the faint sound of traffic outside.
He glanced toward the bedroom.
Nothing.
He poured hot water into two cups.
One coffee.
One tea.
Ha-eun preferred tea in the morning.
He had learned that without ever really thinking about it.
The kettle clicked off.
He carried both cups to the table.
A few minutes later, he heard slow footsteps.
Ha-eun appeared in the doorway, hair slightly messy, eyes half-open.
She stopped when she saw the cups.
“You made mine?”
“Yes.”
She sat down.
“Thank you.”
Her voice was still sleepy.
She wrapped both hands around the cup and took a small sip.
Then she sighed.
“Warm.”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“That's usually what happens when something is hot.”
She frowned at him.
“I wasn't talking about the temperature.”
“I know.”
She stared at him for a second.
Then smiled.
“You're annoying in the morning.”
“You're barely awake.”
“That doesn't make you less annoying.”
He took a sip of coffee.
Ha-eun leaned against the table.
For a while, neither spoke.
It was comfortable.
That was something Ha-Kim had become strangely accustomed to.
Silence used to mean being alone.
Now it could mean someone sitting across from him, drinking tea with messy hair and sleepy eyes.
Ha-eun reached across the table and quietly took a piece of toast from his plate.
He watched her.
“You have your own.”
“I know.”
“Then why take mine?”
“Yours tastes better.”
“They're the same.”
“No.”
He didn't argue.
She ate it.
Then another piece.
Ha-Kim let her.
For a few minutes, the morning remained ordinary.
Until his head suddenly hurt.
He stopped moving.
The pain wasn't sharp at first.
Just pressure.
A familiar pressure behind his eyes.
His fingers tightened slightly around the cup.
Ha-eun noticed.
“Headache?”
He didn't answer immediately.
The pain grew.
He set the cup down.
“I'm fine.”
She frowned.
“You always say that.”
Before he could respond, the room disappeared.
───
Darkness.
Ha-Kim stood in the middle of a street.
Rain hit the pavement.
Hard.
He looked around.
He didn't recognize the place.
Buildings rose on both sides, their signs blurred by rain.
A red light flickered somewhere ahead.
His vision moved.
Not by choice.
It pulled him forward.
Thirty-eight minutes.
The number appeared in the corner of his vision.
Ha-Kim's expression changed.
Thirty-eight minutes meant the future had already started.
He saw Min-seo.
She was walking quickly.
Alone.
Her phone was pressed against her ear.
“Hello?”
No answer.
She lowered the phone.
Looked around.
Something was wrong.
Ha-Kim tried to move closer.
He couldn't.
He was watching.
The same way he always did.
A black vehicle turned into the street.
Min-seo stopped.
The vehicle slowed beside her.
The passenger door opened.
She stepped back.
A man got out.
Another appeared behind him.
Min-seo turned.
She started walking away.
Faster.
The men followed.
Ha-Kim's chest tightened.
He knew what was coming.
Min-seo suddenly ran.
One of the men grabbed her arm.
She struggled.
Her phone fell onto the pavement.
The screen cracked.
Ha-Kim heard her shout.
The vision shifted.
Thirty-one minutes.
She was being dragged toward an alley.
Twenty-seven.
She hit the wall.
Twenty-three.
One of the men raised something in his hand.
Ha-Kim's vision shook.
His breathing became heavier.
He tried to activate the ability again.
He couldn't.
This was already the future.
He had to watch.
Nineteen minutes.
Min-seo fought back.
She managed to break free for a moment.
She ran toward the street.
A car passed.
She stumbled.
The men were behind her.
Twelve minutes.
Ha-Kim saw blood on her forehead.
Nine.
A metal door.
Six.
A hand around her wrist.
Three.
Min-seo looked toward the street.
Her eyes widened.
One.
The vision stopped.
Ha-Kim opened his eyes.
He was back in the apartment.
His hand was gripping the edge of the table.
His breathing was uneven.
Ha-eun was standing beside him.
“Ha-Kim?”
He blinked.
The room felt too bright.
“Thirty-eight minutes.”
“What?”
He stood.
Too quickly.
His vision blurred.
He caught himself against the chair.
Ha-eun immediately reached for his arm.
“What happened?”
“Min-seo.”
Her expression changed.
“What about her?”
“She dies.”
The words came out quietly.
Ha-eun stared at him.
“What?”
“In thirty-eight minutes.”
He closed his eyes.
The vision remained clear in his mind.
Rain.
Red light.
Her phone on the ground.
The alley.
He opened his eyes again.
“I need to go.”
Ha-eun held his arm tighter.
“You're barely standing.”
“I can still—”
“No.”
Her voice was firmer than usual.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“You don't have to do everything yourself.”
He didn't answer.
Because she was right.
He could go.
He could reach Min-seo himself.
But changing the future had a price.
And this wasn't a small change.
He had no idea what saving her would cost.
The headache behind his eyes deepened.
His vision blurred again.
Ha-eun noticed.
“You can't go like this.”
“I know.”
He looked toward the window.
Thirty-eight minutes.
Then thirty-seven.
His mind worked quickly.
Police would take too long.
Calling Min-seo directly might make her suspicious.
And if she didn't answer...
He needed someone close enough.
Someone capable.
Someone he could send without explaining everything.
His eyes settled on his phone.
“Ji-hoon.”
───
Han Ji-hoon was halfway through reviewing a security report when his phone rang.
He looked at the screen.
Ha-Kim.
Ji-hoon answered.
“Yes?”
“I need you to handle something.”
Ji-hoon straightened.
“What kind of something?”
“There is a security issue at a location.”
Ha-Kim gave him an address.
Ji-hoon typed it down.
“What's the issue?”
“Possible unauthorized activity.”
“Do we have confirmation?”
“No.”
Ji-hoon paused.
That wasn't unusual.
The rest was.
Ha-Kim gave him a specific description of the area.
A specific building.
A specific entrance.
Even a specific time window.
Ji-hoon slowly lowered his pen.
“How did you get this information?”
“I received a report.”
“From who?”
“Internal monitoring.”
Ji-hoon stared at the phone.
There was no internal monitoring system that should have produced information this specific.
At least, not this quickly.
Still, he didn't question it.
Not yet.
“Understood.”
“Go now.”
“I'm on my way.”
The call ended.
Ji-hoon grabbed his jacket.
As he walked toward the elevator, he looked at the address again.
Something about this felt wrong.
Not dangerous.
Just... strange.
───
Thirty minutes later, Ji-hoon arrived.
Rain had started.
He stepped out of his car and looked across the street.
The location matched.
He checked his phone.
No additional information.
No suspect names.
No confirmed threat.
Only the instructions.
He entered the building.
The lobby was almost empty.
Ji-hoon looked around.
Then he noticed someone leaving through a side entrance.
A man.
Dark jacket.
Moving quickly.
Ji-hoon followed.
Outside, he saw another man near the alley.
And then he heard shouting.
He moved immediately.
“Hey!”
The men turned.
One grabbed Min-seo.
Ji-hoon crossed the distance quickly.
The first man swung.
Ji-hoon blocked it.
The impact pushed him back half a step.
He responded immediately, striking the man's arm and forcing him away.
The second man grabbed Min-seo again.
She struggled.
Ji-hoon stepped between them.
“Let her go.”
The man hesitated.
Then ran.
Ji-hoon chased him for several steps before stopping.
He wasn't there to pursue them.
He turned back.
Min-seo was against the wall, breathing hard.
Her forehead was bleeding.
She looked at him in confusion.
“Who are you?”
“Haetae Cyber.”
She blinked.
“What?”
“Security.”
Ji-hoon pulled out his phone.
“Are you injured anywhere else?”
“I... don't know.”
He looked her over quickly.
“Can you walk?”
She nodded.
He called emergency services.
While waiting, Min-seo kept staring at him.
Then her expression changed.
“Did Ha-Kim send you?”
Ji-hoon looked at her.
That was enough of an answer.
She already knew.
───
Ha-Kim sat on the edge of the bed.
He hadn't moved much since Ji-hoon left.
Ha-eun sat beside him.
She didn't ask him to explain everything.
She knew enough.
He had seen something.
He had changed it.
Now he had to wait.
That was sometimes the hardest part.
Ha-Kim looked at the clock.
Twenty minutes had passed.
His headache remained.
His eyes felt heavy.
He tried to focus on the room.
The curtains.
The table.
The cup Ha-eun had left beside him.
Everything looked slightly blurred.
Ha-eun noticed.
She moved closer.
“Your eyes.”
“It's fine.”
“Stop saying that.”
He looked at her.
She reached up and gently touched his cheek.
“You don't have to pretend with me.”
His expression softened.
“I know.”
Another minute passed.
His phone rang.
Ji-hoon.
Ha-Kim answered immediately.
“Report.”
“She's safe.”
Ha-Kim closed his eyes.
His shoulders lowered slightly.
“Any serious injuries?”
“No. A cut on her forehead. Nothing life-threatening.”
“Good.”
Ji-hoon paused.
Then added,
“You gave me very specific instructions.”
Ha-Kim said nothing.
“I'll send the report.”
“Do that.”
The call ended.
Ha-eun watched him.
“She survived.”
“Yes.”
She smiled faintly.
“Then that's enough.”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
She didn't ask whether Min-seo would think about him.
She didn't ask why he cared.
She didn't ask whether he still had feelings.
She simply sat beside him.
Ha-Kim reached for her hand.
She held it.
For the first time since the vision began, his breathing settled.
───
Two days passed.
Min-seo didn't contact him.
Ha-Kim didn't contact her.
Life continued.
Work.
Meals.
Sleep.
The apartment.
Ha-eun didn't mention Min-seo unless he did.
She seemed to understand that he needed time.
On the third evening, Ha-Kim came home to find Ha-eun sitting on the couch.
She looked up.
“Someone came earlier.”
“Who?”
“Min-seo.”
Ha-Kim stopped.
“She didn't come inside.”
Ha-eun watched his face.
“She said she wanted to talk to you.”
Ha-Kim was quiet.
Then nodded.
“I'll go.”
Ha-eun reached for his hand before he left.
“You don't have to stay long.”
“I know.”
“And Ha-Kim?”
He looked back.
“You don't owe her anything.”
His eyes softened.
“I know.”
───
Min-seo was waiting outside.
The evening air was cold.
She stood with both hands inside her coat pockets.
When Ha-Kim approached, she looked at him.
For several seconds, neither spoke.
Finally, she said,
“You saved me.”
Ha-Kim stopped a few steps away.
“Yes.”
“You knew.”
“Yes.”
“How?”
He didn't answer.
She looked down.
“I thought I was going to die.”
Ha-Kim remained silent.
“I couldn't move.”
Her voice shook slightly.
“I kept thinking about everything.”
She swallowed.
“About my parents. My work. My apartment. People I haven't talked to in years.”
Then she looked at him.
“And you.”
Ha-Kim's expression didn't change.
“You sent someone.”
“Ji-hoon.”
“You could have come yourself.”
“I couldn't.”
“Why?”
He looked away for a moment.
“My condition wouldn't have allowed it.”
She frowned.
“What condition?”
“It's not important.”
She studied him.
“You still don't tell me anything.”
“Some things haven't changed.”
A small, sad smile appeared on her face.
“No.”
She looked down again.
“I kept thinking about what you said before.”
Ha-Kim waited.
“That we shouldn't go back.”
“Yes.”
“I didn't understand it.”
She took a breath.
“I thought if I tried hard enough, maybe you would change your mind.”
He didn't respond.
“But then you saved me.”
She looked up.
“Why?”
Ha-Kim's eyes met hers.
“Because you were going to die.”
“That isn't enough.”
“It is for me.”
She stared at him.
“I don't understand.”
“You don't have to.”
He paused.
“I don't want you back, Min-seo.”
Her eyes lowered.
“I know.”
“I don't want to restart our relationship.”
“I know.”
“I don't want us to pretend the past didn't happen.”
She looked at him again.
“Then why?”
Ha-Kim took a slow breath.
“Because I don't want you dead either.”
Silence.
Min-seo's eyes widened slightly.
Ha-Kim continued.
“I don't hate you.”
She looked away.
“I don't regret everything we had.”
His voice remained calm.
“We were happy once.”
A tear slipped down her cheek.
“I remember.”
“So do I.”
He looked toward the street.
“But that doesn't mean we belong together now.”
Min-seo wiped her cheek.
“You really moved on.”
“Yes.”
She gave a small, broken laugh.
“I thought I'd hate hearing that.”
“You don't?”
“I do.”
He almost smiled.
“That's okay.”
She looked at him.
“Why are you being kind to me?”
“I'm not trying to be kind.”
“Then what are you trying to do?”
“Tell you the truth.”
She was quiet.
Ha-Kim looked at her directly.
“You have a life ahead of you.”
She didn't answer.
“You have work. People you haven't met yet. Places you haven't seen.”
Her lips trembled.
“Don't throw that away because of something that ended seven years ago.”
She looked down.
“I don't know how to stop thinking about it.”
“You don't have to stop.”
She looked up.
“You can remember.”
He paused.
“Just don't live there.”
The words stayed between them.
Min-seo slowly nodded.
Ha-Kim continued.
“The past happened.”
His voice was quiet.
“We can't change it.”
For a moment, his mind drifted back.
The first time he met her.
The first time she made him laugh.
The first time he realized he was comfortable around someone.
The arguments.
The silence.
The breakup.
Seven years.
All of it.
He didn't need to erase any of it.
“I don't regret loving you,” he said.
Min-seo's eyes filled again.
“But I don't belong there anymore.”
She covered her mouth.
A few tears fell.
Ha-Kim stood quietly until she finished crying.
He didn't hug her.
He didn't promise friendship.
He didn't tell her everything would be fine.
Some endings didn't need another promise.
Eventually, Min-seo took a breath.
“Then what am I supposed to do?”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“Live.”
She laughed quietly through her tears.
“That sounds simple.”
“It isn't.”
“No.”
“But you can.”
She nodded.
He stepped back.
“One more thing.”
She looked at him.
“The people who hurt you don't get to decide how your life ends.”
Her expression changed.
“And neither does your past.”
He looked at her for a moment.
“The past doesn't deserve your death.”
Min-seo lowered her head.
For a long time, she said nothing.
Then she whispered,
“Goodbye, Ha-Kim.”
He looked at her.
“Goodbye.”
She walked away.
Ha-Kim stayed where he was.
He watched her disappear into the crowd.
Then he turned around.
And went home.
───
The apartment lights were on.
Ha-eun was sitting on the couch, waiting.
She looked at him when he entered.
“How did it go?”
Ha-Kim took off his shoes.
“It's over.”
She nodded.
She didn't ask for details.
Instead, she moved over and made space beside her.
He sat.
For a while, neither spoke.
Then Ha-eun quietly reached for his hand.
Their fingers intertwined.
Ha-Kim looked down at them.
Something felt different.
Not dramatic.
Not like some weight had suddenly vanished.
Just quieter.
The past was still there.
He could remember it.
He could remember the good parts.
The bad parts.
The person he used to be.
The person Min-seo used to be.
But he didn't need to return to any of it.
Ha-eun rested her head against his shoulder.
“Do you feel better?”
Ha-Kim thought about it.
“A little.”
She smiled.
“That's enough.”
He looked at her.
Then at the room around them.
Their apartment.
Her things.
His things.
The little ceramic cat sitting on the shelf.
Two cups in the kitchen.
A blanket folded over the couch.
A life that existed now.
Ha-Kim didn't activate his ability.
He didn't look one hour ahead.
He didn't need to know what tomorrow would bring.
For once, tomorrow could simply arrive.
And he would meet it when it did.
