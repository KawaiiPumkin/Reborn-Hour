# The Quiet Employee

6:20 AM.

The alarm on Ha-Kim's phone rang for the third time.

He opened his eyes and stared at the ceiling for a few seconds.

He reached for his phone and turned the alarm off.

Silence.

Ha-Kim slowly sat up on his bed.

His apartment was quiet. There was nothing unusual about it. No loud family, no one asking him where he was going, no one waiting for him.

Just silence.

He preferred it that way.

He got out of bed, walked to the bathroom and washed his face. After brushing his teeth, he took a quick shower and changed into his usual work clothes.

A black shirt.

Dark pants.

Glasses.

His off black hair was slightly messy, but he didn't bother fixing it much.

He looked at himself in the mirror.

He stared for a moment.

Then he looked away.

"Whatever."

He grabbed his bag and left his apartment.

The morning streets of Seoul were already busy.

Cars everywhere.

People rushing to work.

Students walking around while staring at their phones.

Ha-Kim walked through all of it without saying a word.

He put his earphones in, but he didn't play anything.

He just liked having them in.

It gave people less reason to talk to him.

After taking the bus, he arrived at Haetae Cyber.

The building was already crowded.

Ha-Kim walked through the entrance.

"Morning."

A coworker said it casually.

Ha-Kim looked at him.

"Morning."

That was all.

The coworker looked slightly surprised before walking away.

Another employee whispered to his friend.

"He actually answered."

His friend laughed quietly.

Ha-Kim heard them.

He ignored them.

He walked to his desk and put his bag down.

His desk wasn't especially messy.

It wasn't perfectly clean either.

A laptop.

A few documents.

A cup of coffee from yesterday that he forgot to throw away.

He sat down and turned on his computer.

Within minutes, he was already working.

He didn't talk to anyone.

He didn't need to.

Ha-Kim was one of those employees people didn't really understand.

He wasn't rude.

He didn't insult anyone.

He didn't cause problems.

He simply didn't talk.

If someone asked him something, he answered.

If nobody talked to him, he could go the entire day without saying more than a few sentences.

Some coworkers thought he was arrogant.

Others thought he hated everyone.

A few simply found him creepy.

Ha-Kim didn't care.

He was halfway through checking a security report when someone stopped beside his desk.

"Ha-Kim."

He looked up.

It was his manager.

"Yes?"

"Come with me for a second."

Ha-Kim sighed quietly.

He saved his work and stood up.

They walked toward the manager's office.

Ha-Kim sat down.

His manager remained standing.

"I have something for you."

Ha-Kim looked at him.

"What?"

"We have a new employee starting today."

Ha-Kim waited.

His manager continued.

"She's going to be trained by you."

Ha-Kim stared at him.

"...Me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because you're one of the few people here who can actually teach her."

Ha-Kim leaned back slightly.

"I don't want to."

His manager sighed.

"I know."

"Then why are you telling me?"

"Because you're doing it anyway."

Ha-Kim stared at him for a few seconds.

His manager smiled slightly.

"Her name is Choi Ha-eun. She doesn't have much experience, so you'll have to start from the basics."

Ha-Kim's expression didn't change.

"How long?"

"Until she can work properly on her own."

Ha-Kim looked away.

Great.

Just great.

He preferred working alone.

Now he had to train someone.

"Fine."

His manager nodded.

"Good. She's probably arriving soon."

Ha-Kim stood up.

"Anything else?"

"No."

He walked out.

His manager watched him leave.

"What a cheerful guy."

Ha-Kim returned to his desk.

He sat down and continued working as if nothing happened.

A few minutes later, his phone vibrated.

A message.

He looked at the screen.

It was from an encrypted internal system.

CEO communication channel active.

Ha-Kim looked around.

Nobody was paying attention.

He opened the message.

There was a request regarding an upcoming company meeting.

He answered through the secure system.

A few seconds later, another message appeared.

CEO: Confirmed.

Ha-Kim closed the system.

Nobody around him knew.

To everyone here, he was just another employee.

The CEO was someone they had never seen.

Every important meeting with the CEO happened through a large screen in the conference room.

The voice was distorted.

Nobody knew what he looked like.

Nobody knew where he worked from.

Even the employees who had been there for years had never seen his face.

Ha-Kim preferred it that way.

He went back to work.

About thirty minutes later, the office doors opened.

A nervous girl walked inside.

Ha-eun.

She looked around the huge office.

There were computers everywhere.

Employees walking around.

People talking about projects she barely understood.

She held her bag tightly.

"Okay..."

She took a deep breath.

"Don't mess this up."

She looked around again.

Then she spotted her manager.

"Excuse me?"

The manager looked at her.

"You're Choi Ha-eun?"

"Yes."

"Good. Your trainer is over there."

He pointed toward Ha-Kim.

Ha-eun followed his finger.

She froze.

A tall man was sitting at his desk.

Black clothes.

Glasses.

Messy dark hair.

His expression looked completely emotionless.

He was staring at his monitor.

He looked like someone who would rather be anywhere else.

Ha-eun swallowed.

"That's my trainer?"

"Yes."

She slowly walked toward him.

Ha-Kim noticed her approaching before she even reached the desk.

He didn't look up immediately.

He already knew she was there.

She stopped beside his desk.

"Excuse me."

Ha-Kim looked up.

Their eyes met.

Ha-eun immediately became nervous.

"Hi."

"Hi."

Silence.

Ha-eun waited.

Ha-Kim waited too.

Neither of them said anything.

Ha-eun smiled awkwardly.

"I'm Choi Ha-eun."

"I know."

She blinked.

"Oh."

Silence again.

Ha-Kim looked back at his computer.

Ha-eun stood there.

"...So."

He looked up again.

"So?"

"Are you my trainer?"

"Apparently."

Ha-eun wasn't sure if that was supposed to be a joke.

He didn't look like he was joking.

"Okay."

Ha-Kim pointed toward the empty chair beside him.

"Sit."

She sat down.

He opened a document on his computer.

"What do you know about cybersecurity?"

Ha-eun stared at him.

"...Not much."

"How much?"

She thought about it.

"I know computers."

Ha-Kim waited.

"And?"

"That's basically it."

He stared at her.

Ha-eun looked down.

"I told them I was inexperienced."

"I know."

She looked at him.

"Then why did they accept me?"

Ha-Kim paused.

He looked at her for a moment.

"Ask them."

Ha-eun lowered her head.

"Right."

Ha-Kim noticed something.

She wasn't just inexperienced.

She was scared.

Every time she answered, she looked down.

Every time he asked a question, she looked like she expected him to tell her she was useless.

He had seen that before.

People who already expected themselves to fail usually stopped trying before they even started.

Ha-Kim turned his monitor toward her.

"Look."

Ha-eun leaned closer.

"This is the system you'll be working with."

He began explaining.

His voice was calm.

Short.

Direct.

No unnecessary words.

Ha-eun tried to follow.

After a few minutes, her eyes became confused.

Ha-Kim noticed.

"You don't understand."

Ha-eun looked embarrassed.

"Sorry."

"Don't apologize."

She looked at him.

"Then what should I say?"

"Nothing."

Ha-eun stared at him.

For the first time, Ha-Kim noticed her almost smiling.

It disappeared quickly.

He continued explaining.

Ten minutes later, Ha-eun accidentally clicked something.

The screen changed.

She froze.

"Uh..."

Ha-Kim looked at the screen.

Ha-eun slowly moved her hand away from the mouse.

"I broke it."

Ha-Kim stared at the screen.

Then at her.

"You didn't."

"Oh."

"That button just opens another menu."

"Oh."

She looked relieved.

Ha-Kim turned back to the computer.

"You're fine."

Ha-eun nodded.

"Okay."

A few desks away, two coworkers looked over.

One whispered.

"Is he actually training someone?"

"I thought he hated people."

"He probably does."

Ha-Kim heard them.

He ignored them.

Ha-eun heard them too.

She looked at Ha-Kim.

He didn't react at all.

She quietly wondered how someone could be so calm.

After another hour, Ha-Kim gave her a simple task.

"Try this."

Ha-eun looked at the screen.

"What if I get it wrong?"

"Then you'll know what you did wrong."

She looked at him.

"...That's actually good advice."

Ha-Kim shrugged.

"It's obvious."

Ha-eun smiled slightly.

"You're weird."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She immediately realized what she said.

"I mean..."

He turned back toward the monitor.

"You're not the first person to say that."

Ha-eun quietly laughed.

It was the first time that morning she felt comfortable.

Ha-Kim didn't smile.

But for a second, his expression softened.

Then it disappeared.

He looked at her work again.

"You made a mistake here."

Ha-eun immediately became nervous.

"Where?"

He pointed at the screen.

"Here."

"Oh..."

She fixed it.

"Like this?"

"Yes."

She smiled.

"I got it."

Ha-Kim nodded.

"Good."

That single word made Ha-eun smile even more.

She didn't know it yet, but this quiet, gloomy employee would become one of the most important people in her life.

And Ha-Kim had no idea how much this new employee was about to change his.

For now, though, he only had one thought.

Training someone was going to be annoying.
