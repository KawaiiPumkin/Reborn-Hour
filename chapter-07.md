# Two Broken People

It was raining that evening.

Not heavily.

Just enough to cover the windows with small drops.

Most of the employees had already left.

The office was quieter than usual.

Ha-eun sat at her desk, staring at the screen.

She had finished her work twenty minutes ago.

She just didn't want to go home yet.

She looked outside.

The sky was dark.

Cars moved slowly through the wet streets below.

She sighed.

“Great.”

She checked her phone.

No messages.

Nothing.

She put it back down.

Then noticed someone was still working.

Ha-Kim.

Of course.

He was sitting at his desk with his glasses slightly lower than usual.

His screen reflected faintly in the lenses.

Ha-eun looked at him.

Then at the rain.

Then back at him.

“Do you ever go home?”

Ha-Kim looked up.

“Yes.”

“When?”

“When I'm finished.”

“You've been finished for like an hour.”

He looked at his screen.

“No, I haven't.”

Ha-eun leaned back in her chair.

“You're lying.”

“Probably.”

She smiled.

For a few seconds, neither spoke.

The rain tapped quietly against the glass.

───

Ha-eun stood up.

“I'm getting something from the vending machine.”

Ha-Kim nodded.

“Okay.”

She walked away.

A minute later, she came back with two drinks.

She placed one on his desk.

He looked at it.

“What's this?”

“Coffee.”

“I can see that.”

“Then why did you ask?”

“I didn't.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“You're welcome.”

Ha-Kim looked at the coffee.

Then at her.

“Thanks.”

She sat down.

He opened it.

They drank quietly.

Neither seemed in a hurry to leave.

───

After a few minutes, Ha-eun spoke.

“Do you like working here?”

Ha-Kim looked at the rain.

“It's fine.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It is.”

She sighed.

“You say everything like that.”

“Like what?”

“Like you don't care.”

He looked at her.

“I care.”

“About what?”

He thought for a moment.

“Some things.”

Ha-eun nodded slowly.

“That's fair.”

She looked down at her drink.

“I don't think I care about many things either.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She smiled slightly.

“Not anymore, at least.”

He didn't ask what she meant.

For some reason, she appreciated that.

───

The rain became a little heavier.

Ha-eun watched the drops slide down the window.

“When I was younger, I thought things would get better.”

Ha-Kim stayed quiet.

“I don't know why.”

She smiled faintly.

“I guess that's what kids do.”

She looked at him.

“Believe things will be okay.”

Ha-Kim's eyes lowered slightly.

“Yeah.”

“You don't believe that?”

He looked back at the window.

“I used to.”

Ha-eun didn't respond.

Something about the way he said it felt different.

Not sad.

Not angry.

Just...

Tired.

She looked at him again.

“You had a difficult life too?”

Ha-Kim was quiet.

For a moment, he didn't answer.

“Yeah.”

That was all.

Ha-eun waited.

He didn't continue.

So she didn't ask.

“Me too.”

He looked at her.

She gave a small smile.

“I don't really talk about it.”

“Okay.”

“Not because I don't trust you.”

“I know.”

“I just don't like talking about it.”

“I understand.”

Ha-eun looked at him.

“You really do?”

He nodded.

“Yeah.”

She looked down.

For some reason, that answer felt more comforting than she expected.

───

A few minutes passed.

Ha-eun slowly turned the coffee cup between her hands.

“I used to think being alone meant nobody wanted you around.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She continued.

“Now I think sometimes people are just... alone.”

He didn't interrupt.

“Even when other people are around.”

Ha-Kim's expression barely changed.

But his eyes did.

Just slightly.

“You're right.”

Ha-eun looked at him.

“You've felt that?”

He looked down at his coffee.

“Sometimes.”

She smiled.

“Sometimes?”

“More than sometimes.”

She didn't laugh.

She just nodded.

“I know that feeling.”

───

The office was almost completely silent now.

Only the rain.

The air conditioner.

And the faint sound of Ha-Kim's keyboard.

Ha-eun looked around.

Everyone had gone home.

It was just them.

She didn't feel uncomfortable.

That surprised her.

Usually, silence made her nervous.

But with him...

It didn't.

She leaned back.

“You know something?”

“What?”

“You're not as scary as I thought.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“That's unfortunate.”

She smiled.

“See? That.”

“What?”

“You're actually funny sometimes.”

“I'm not trying to be.”

“That's why it's funny.”

He looked away.

She smiled.

A few seconds later, Ha-Kim quietly laughed.

Ha-eun looked at him.

He stopped.

“What?”

“You laughed.”

“No.”

“You did.”

“I didn't.”

“You definitely did.”

He looked back at his screen.

“Go home.”

She laughed.

“Okay.”

───

They packed their things.

Ha-eun put her bag over her shoulder.

Ha-Kim shut down his computer.

They walked toward the elevator together.

The hallway was almost empty.

Their footsteps echoed quietly.

Ha-eun looked at him.

“Can I ask you something?”

“You just did.”

She smiled.

“Why are you always alone?”

He pressed the elevator button.

He didn't answer immediately.

The doors opened.

They stepped inside.

He looked at the floor numbers.

“I got used to it.”

Ha-eun looked down.

“That's not a good thing.”

“No.”

“Then why?”

He thought about it.

“It's easier.”

The elevator continued downward.

Ha-eun understood.

She didn't know exactly what happened to him.

But she understood that answer.

“Yeah.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She smiled faintly.

“Sometimes being alone is easier.”

He nodded.

“But it's not always better.”

The elevator reached the first floor.

The doors opened.

Neither moved immediately.

Then Ha-eun stepped out.

Ha-Kim followed.

───

They reached the entrance.

The rain was still falling.

Ha-eun stopped.

“I didn't bring an umbrella.”

Ha-Kim looked outside.

“Neither did I.”

She stared at him.

“You live nearby?”

“Yeah.”

“How nearby?”

He pointed down the street.

“About ten minutes.”

Ha-eun looked at the rain.

“My place is farther.”

He thought for a moment.

Then took off his jacket.

Ha-eun looked at him.

“What are you doing?”

“Take it.”

“No.”

“You'll get wet.”

“So will you.”

“I don't care.”

She stared at him.

“You're weird.”

“So I've heard.”

She took the jacket.

“Thank you.”

He nodded.

They stepped outside.

───

The rain was cold.

Ha-eun held the jacket over her head as she walked.

Ha-Kim walked beside her.

For a while, neither said anything.

Then Ha-eun spoke.

“Your jacket smells like coffee.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“What?”

She laughed quietly.

“Nothing.”

He shook his head.

She smiled.

They kept walking.

Neither noticed how natural it felt.

───

After a few minutes, they reached the point where they had to separate.

Ha-eun held out his jacket.

“Here.”

He took it.

“Thanks.”

She looked at him.

“No, thank you.”

“For what?”

She thought about it.

“For listening.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“You didn't tell me much.”

“I know.”

“You don't have to.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“I know.”

She turned around.

“Goodnight, Ha-Kim.”

“Goodnight.”

She walked away.

Ha-Kim watched her for a few seconds.

Then turned in the opposite direction.

He walked slowly.

The rain continued falling.

He didn't mind it.

───

Ha-eun reached her apartment.

She unlocked the door.

The room was dark.

She turned on the light.

The same small room.

The same old walls.

The same bed.

She put her bag down.

Then looked at herself in the mirror.

For some reason, she was smiling.

She touched her cheek.

“Why am I smiling?”

She didn't know.

She changed clothes and sat on her bed.

Her eyes slowly moved toward the window.

She thought about the conversation.

She hadn't told him everything.

Not even close.

But somehow...

She felt understood.

That was new.

───

Ha-Kim returned home.

He placed his keys on the table.

The apartment was silent.

He took off his glasses.

Rubbed his eyes.

Then stopped.

He thought about Ha-eun.

Her questions.

Her small smiles.

The way she laughed.

The way she understood silence without trying to fill it.

He had spent years convincing himself that being alone was easier.

Most of the time, it was.

But lately...

He didn't mind having someone around.

He walked toward the window.

Seoul was still glowing beneath the rain.

He stared outside.

Then smiled.

Just slightly.

“She's strange.”

He said it quietly.

But this time...

He didn't mean it as an insult.

───

The next morning, Ha-eun arrived at work.

She walked past Ha-Kim's desk.

“Morning.”

He looked up.

“Morning.”

She smiled.

He smiled back.

Neither of them said anything else.

But something had changed.

Neither knew exactly what it was yet.

They only knew that being around each other felt a little easier than before.

And sometimes...

That was how things started.
