# Chapter 13 — The First Shadow

The next morning, Ha-eun woke up earlier than usual.

For a few seconds, she stared at the ceiling.

Then she remembered.

Ha-Kim was sick.

She sat up.

“Oh.”

She looked toward the small kitchen.

Yesterday, she had told him she would cook.

She had meant it.

Ha-eun got out of bed and opened the refrigerator.

She stared inside.

Vegetables.

Eggs.

Some meat.

A few containers.

She picked up a carrot.

“Soup.”

She nodded to herself.

“Easy.”

---

At Haetae Cyber, the morning passed normally.

Ha-eun sat at her desk, working through a security report.

Ji-woo leaned over from the desk beside her.

“You're distracted.”

“I’m not.”

“You've been staring at the same line for two minutes.”

Ha-eun looked down.

She had, in fact, been staring at the same line.

“I was thinking.”

“About?”

“Nothing.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“Ha-Kim?”

Ha-eun's face immediately warmed.

“No.”

“You answered too quickly.”

“I just—”

Ji-woo laughed.

“I knew it.”

Ha-eun lowered her voice.

“He's sick.”

Ji-woo's expression changed.

“Sick?”

“Just a fever.”

“And you're worried?”

“A little.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“You're going to check on him after work, aren't you?”

Ha-eun looked away.

“I told him I'd cook.”

Ji-woo stared.

“You're cooking for him?”

“Yes.”

“Wow.”

“Why does everyone react like that?”

“Because you're cooking for Ha-Kim.”

“He cooked for me first.”

“That makes it different?”

“Yes.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“Sure.”

Ha-eun went back to her screen.

A few seconds later, she quietly asked,

“What kind of soup is good when someone has a fever?”

Ji-woo laughed.

“Okay. You're definitely worried.”

“Just answer.”

---

After lunch, the office became quieter.

Ha-Kim was at his desk.

He looked better than yesterday.

At least from a distance.

His fever had dropped slightly in the morning, but the strange pressure behind his eyes remained.

He ignored it.

There were reports to finish.

A meeting to attend.

Messages from the CEO channel.

Normal things.

He preferred normal things.

Ha-eun occasionally looked toward him from her desk.

He noticed.

He pretended not to.

At one point, their eyes met.

She pointed at him.

He frowned.

She mouthed:

Are you okay?

He nodded.

She narrowed her eyes.

Then returned to work.

Ha-Kim almost smiled.

---

After work, Ha-eun went home first.

She changed into something comfortable, tied her hair back, and started cooking.

The kitchen quickly filled with the smell of vegetables.

She chopped carrots.

Potatoes.

Onions.

A few other vegetables.

Nothing complicated.

She tasted the soup.

“Too bland.”

She added a little salt.

Tasted it again.

“Better.”

She smiled.

Then the doorbell rang.

Ha-eun wiped her hands and opened the door.

Ji-woo stood there holding a small bag.

“Surprise.”

Ha-eun blinked.

“Ji-woo?”

“I wanted to see your apartment.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“Come in.”

Ji-woo stepped inside.

She looked around.

The apartment was small.

Simple.

But comfortable.

A small blanket rested on the couch.

A few cheap plants sat near the window.

The place didn't have much, but it felt lived in.

Ji-woo smiled.

“You made it cute.”

Ha-eun looked around.

“Really?”

“Yeah.”

“I like it.”

Ji-woo handed her the bag.

“I brought snacks.”

Ha-eun looked inside.

Her eyes immediately brightened.

“Chocolate?”

“And chips.”

“You're the best.”

Ji-woo laughed.

“You're really easy to make happy.”

Ha-eun nodded seriously.

“I know.”

They sat on the couch.

Ha-eun opened the chocolate.

Ji-woo looked toward the kitchen.

“Are you cooking?”

“Yeah.”

“For yourself?”

Ha-eun hesitated.

“...For Ha-Kim.”

Ji-woo slowly turned toward her.

“You're going to his apartment?”

“Yes.”

“To cook?”

“Yes.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“You really care about him.”

Ha-eun looked down at the chocolate.

“I just don't want him to be sick alone.”

Ji-woo's expression softened.

“That sounds like caring.”

Ha-eun didn't answer.

She took another piece of chocolate.

---

A little later, Ha-eun packed the soup into a container.

Ji-woo watched her.

“You're really serious about this.”

“He said he didn't need help.”

“That sounds like him.”

“So I'm going anyway.”

Ji-woo laughed.

“Good luck.”

Ha-eun picked up the container.

“I'll see you tomorrow.”

“Tell Ha-Kim I said hi.”

“I will.”

Ha-eun left.

Ji-woo watched the door close.

Then smiled.

“Those two.”

---

Ha-eun walked across the hallway.

She knocked.

Nothing.

She knocked again.

“Ha-Kim?”

The door opened slowly.

Ha-Kim stood there.

His hair was slightly messy.

His glasses were crooked.

He looked tired.

Ha-eun frowned.

“You look worse.”

“Good evening.”

“You look terrible.”

“I appreciate your concern.”

She held up the container.

“I made soup.”

Ha-Kim looked at it.

“You didn't have to.”

“I know.”

She walked inside.

Ha-Kim stepped aside.

She went straight to the kitchen.

He followed.

“You came all the way here just to cook?”

“Yes.”

“You could have stayed home.”

“You're sick.”

“I'm better.”

“You don't look better.”

He sighed.

Ha-eun opened the container.

The smell filled the kitchen.

Ha-Kim looked at it.

“You made vegetable soup.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because you're sick.”

“I know that.”

“Then why are you asking?”

Ha-Kim stopped.

“Fair.”

She poured the soup into a bowl.

“Sit.”

“I can stand.”

“Sit.”

He sat.

Ha-eun placed the bowl in front of him.

“Eat.”

He picked up the spoon.

Took one bite.

Ha-eun watched him carefully.

“Well?”

“It's good.”

Her face immediately brightened.

“Really?”

“Yes.”

She smiled.

“Good.”

He took another bite.

She sat across from him.

“You like it?”

“Yes.”

“Really?”

“You've asked three times.”

“Because I'm happy.”

Ha-Kim paused.

Then continued eating.

Ha-eun looked ridiculously pleased.

For her, it didn't take much.

Good food.

Someone enjoying what she made.

A quiet evening.

That was enough.

---

After he finished, Ha-eun took the bowl.

“I'll wash it.”

“You don't have to.”

“I cooked it.”

“You don't have to wash it.”

“You're sick.”

“That doesn't explain anything.”

“It does to me.”

She took the bowl into the kitchen.

Ha-Kim watched her.

“You're stubborn.”

“So are you.”

She turned on the water.

For a moment, the apartment was quiet.

Then Ha-Kim coughed.

Ha-eun immediately turned around.

“You're still sick.”

“It's one cough.”

“You need to rest.”

“I rested all morning.”

“Then rest again.”

She walked over.

“Come here.”

“What?”

She touched his forehead.

Ha-Kim froze.

“You're still warm.”

“It's not that bad.”

“You're lying.”

“I don't lie.”

“You do when you say you're fine.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She looked back.

Neither moved.

Her hand remained against his forehead.

Then Ha-eun leaned closer, trying to feel his temperature better.

Ha-Kim turned slightly at the same time.

Their faces suddenly ended up much closer than either expected.

Both stopped.

Ha-eun's eyes widened.

Ha-Kim didn't move.

For a second, neither of them seemed to breathe.

Then Ha-eun quickly pulled her hand away.

“You're still warm.”

Ha-Kim adjusted his glasses.

“...I noticed.”

She looked toward the kitchen.

“I should wash the bowl.”

“You already washed it.”

She looked down.

The bowl was sitting beside the sink.

“Oh.”

Ha-Kim looked away.

“Good work.”

“Shut up.”

He almost smiled.

---

Later, Ha-eun sat on the couch while Ha-Kim rested.

Neither talked much.

The television was off.

No music played.

Just the quiet sound of the city outside.

Ha-eun eventually looked at him.

“You should sleep.”

“I will.”

“You promise?”

“Yes.”

She nodded.

“Good.”

She stood.

“I'll go home.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“Okay.”

She reached the door.

Then turned around.

“Eat something tomorrow.”

“I will.”

“And don't skip medicine.”

“I won't.”

“And if you still have a fever—”

“I'll tell you.”

She smiled.

“Good.”

She opened the door.

“Good night.”

“Good night.”

She left.

The door closed.

Ha-Kim sat quietly for a moment.

Then his smile disappeared.

The pressure behind his eyes had returned.

Stronger.

He stood.

One step.

Another.

Then—

Pain.

A violent headache exploded through his skull.

Ha-Kim grabbed the wall.

His breathing stopped.

His vision blurred.

“No...”

The apartment disappeared.

---

Darkness.

Ha-Kim stood somewhere he didn't recognize.

There was no street.

No room.

No sky.

Nothing.

He looked down.

He could see himself.

But only barely.

Like his body was fading into the darkness.

Then something appeared in the distance.

A person.

A shadow.

No face.

No details.

Just a human shape.

Ha-Kim turned.

Another shadow appeared.

Then another.

Then dozens.

They stood everywhere.

Some were close.

Others were impossibly far away.

Ha-Kim tried to move.

Nothing happened.

He looked at the time.

There was no clock.

He tried to activate his ability.

Nothing.

His breathing became heavier.

This wasn't right.

His ability always showed him the next hour.

Always.

But this—

He couldn't even tell where he was.

A shadow suddenly moved.

Ha-Kim looked toward it.

The figure was smaller than the others.

Something about it felt familiar.

He stared.

“...Ha-eun?”

The figure didn't answer.

The darkness shifted.

For a moment, the world changed.

Rain.

A street.

Lights.

Someone running.

Someone falling.

A hand reaching forward.

Then the image vanished.

The small shadow remained.

Behind it stood something enormous.

Black.

Still.

Impossible to understand.

Ha-Kim stared at it.

“What are you?”

The figure moved.

The darkness shook.

The shadows began disappearing.

Ha-Kim reached toward Ha-eun.

“Wait!”

She turned.

For a fraction of a second, he thought he saw her face.

Then—

Everything shattered.

---

Ha-Kim woke on the floor.

He gasped.

His hand was stretched toward nothing.

Sweat covered his face.

His heart was pounding.

He stayed there for several seconds.

Then slowly sat up.

The apartment was exactly as it had been.

Quiet.

Normal.

He looked toward the door.

Ha-eun was gone.

He pressed his fingers against his forehead.

The pain was still there.

But the headache wasn't what disturbed him.

He knew his ability.

He knew what the future felt like.

He knew its limits.

One hour.

One hour was all he had ever seen.

But this vision had been different.

There was no hour.

No clear future.

No certainty.

Only shadows.

And something far beyond them.

Ha-Kim looked toward the window.

Seoul's lights stretched across the darkness.

His reflection stared back at him.

For the first time in years, he felt something he hadn't expected.

Fear.

Not because he had seen someone die.

Not because he had seen a disaster.

But because he had seen something his ability was never supposed to show him.

He whispered:

“...How far ahead was that?”

There was no answer.

Only the city outside.

And somewhere beyond what he could see, a future waited that even Ha-Kim couldn't understand.
