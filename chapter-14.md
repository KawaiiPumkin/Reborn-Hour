# Chapter 14 — Someone From Before

The days after Ha-Kim’s fever slowly returned to normal.

At least, on the outside.

His temperature went back down.
The headaches became less frequent.
And he never told Ha-eun about what he had seen.

The shadows.
The rain.
That strange shape standing somewhere beyond them.

He still didn't understand it himself.

Telling her would only make her worry.

So he said nothing.

And life continued.

───

Ha-eun's training was almost over.

She had stopped needing Ha-Kim beside her every few minutes.
She knew where everything was now.
She understood most of the systems.

She still made mistakes, but instead of immediately turning toward him in panic, she usually stared at the screen until she figured them out herself.

Ha-Kim noticed.

He didn't say anything.

Of course.

Ha-eun looked over at him.

"Are you staring at my screen?"

"No."

"You are."

"I'm working."

"Your screen is over there."

Ha-Kim slowly looked back at his own monitor.

Ha-eun smiled.

"Thought so."

A few seconds passed.

Then he spoke.

"You fixed it."

She looked back at her screen.

"The mistake?"

"Yes."

"By myself."

"I noticed."

There was something strangely satisfying about hearing that.

Ha-eun tried not to smile too much.

"Maybe I'm becoming good at this."

"Don't get ahead of yourself."

Her smile disappeared.

"Wow."

Ha-Kim's mouth moved slightly.

Ha-eun narrowed her eyes.

"Did you just smile?"

"No."

"You did."

"No."

She leaned a little closer.

"You definitely did."

"Do your work."

"You're changing the subject."

"Ha-eun."

"Fine."

She turned back toward her computer.

Still smiling.

Ha-Kim glanced at her once more.

Then returned to his work.

───

Later that afternoon, the manager stopped beside Ha-eun's desk.

"Choi Ha-eun."

She immediately straightened.

"Yes?"

"Come with me."

Her confidence disappeared instantly.

She looked at Ha-Kim.

He didn't even look worried.

"It's fine."

"You don't know that."

"I do."

"How?"

"Go."

Ha-eun stared at him suspiciously before following the manager.

Twenty minutes later, she returned.

Ha-Kim was typing when she stopped beside his desk.

He looked up.

Ha-eun was holding a document against her chest.

Her expression was blank.

"What happened?"

She slowly lowered the paper.

"I passed."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"My training."

Her voice became a little louder.

"I passed."

A smile slowly spread across her face.

"I'm actually an employee now."

Ha-Kim looked at the document.

Then back at her.

"Good job."

That was it.

Two words.

But Ha-eun's smile immediately became brighter.

"Thanks."

She looked down at the paper again.

For someone else, maybe it was just a job.

For her, it wasn't.

Not long ago, she had been standing outside that building wondering whether security would even let her inside.

She had barely known what she was doing.

She had been terrified of touching the wrong thing.
Terrified of looking stupid.
Terrified they would realize she didn't belong there.

Now she had passed.

She had actually done it.

Ha-eun looked at Ha-Kim again.

"You really think I did good?"

He raised an eyebrow.

"I just said it."

"I know. Say it again."

"No."

"Why?"

"Once is enough."

She smiled.

"You're so difficult."

Then something occurred to her.

Her smile slowly faded.

"Wait."

"What?"

"You're not my trainer anymore."

Ha-Kim stopped.

Ha-eun looked at the paper.

"My training is finished."

"Yes."

"So..."

She looked at him.

"You don't have to deal with me anymore."

There was a short silence.

For months, there had always been a reason.

Training.
Work.
Questions.
Mistakes.

Something that gave them an excuse to sit beside each other.

An excuse to leave together.

An excuse for Ha-eun to find him whenever she didn't understand something.

Now that excuse was gone.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"You live across the hall."

Ha-eun blinked.

Then smiled.

"Right."

"And you keep showing up at my apartment."

Her smile disappeared.

"Keep showing up?"

"Yes."

"That makes me sound annoying."

"You are."

She stared at him.

Ha-Kim looked back at his monitor.

But she saw the tiny smile on his face.

"You're lucky I passed today."

───

When work ended, Ha-eun packed her things.

She looked toward Ha-Kim.

He was already putting on his jacket.

For a second, neither said anything.

They didn't have to leave together anymore.

Technically, they never had.

Ha-eun adjusted the strap of her bag.

"I'm going home."

Ha-Kim nodded.

"Okay."

She stood there.

"...Okay."

He looked at her.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She started walking.

Ha-Kim grabbed his bag.

A few seconds later, he caught up beside her.

Ha-eun looked at him.

"I thought you were staying."

"I never said that."

She smiled.

They left together.

───

Outside, Seoul was already beginning to glow under the evening lights.

Ha-eun walked beside him.

She was unusually quiet.

Ha-Kim noticed.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

"You've been staring at the ground for five minutes."

"I'm thinking."

"About?"

"My job."

He waited.

Ha-eun looked up at the buildings around them.

"I didn't think I'd actually make it."

"You almost didn't."

She looked at him.

"Can you let me have one happy moment?"

"I'm being honest."

"You're terrible at celebrations."

"I don't celebrate."

"I can tell."

She lightly bumped her shoulder against his arm.

Ha-Kim looked down at her.

She was smiling.

There was something different about her now.

Maybe confidence.
Maybe comfort.

When they first met, she barely looked him in the eyes.

Now she walked beside him, complained to him, entered his apartment without panicking, and sometimes ordered him to eat when he was sick.

Her dark-cyan streaks moved slightly in the evening wind.

She pushed some hair away from her face.

Ha-Kim caught himself watching.

She really was beautiful.

Not in some distant, perfect way.

It was the little things.

The way her eyes changed when she was genuinely happy.
The way she tried to hide her smile when he complimented her.
The way she worried about people without making a big deal about it.
The way his quiet apartment somehow felt warmer whenever she was inside it.

Ha-eun suddenly looked up.

Their eyes met.

Ha-Kim immediately looked forward.

She blinked.

Then she looked at him properly.

His hair was slightly messy from the wind.

His glasses sat low enough that she could see his dark green eyes more clearly.

She had noticed them before.

But for some reason, she kept looking this time.

He was...

Actually really handsome.

Ha-eun quickly looked away.

Why was she thinking about that?

"You okay?"

"Yes."

"Your face is red."

"It's cold."

"It's not."

"Can you stop noticing things?"

"No."

She pulled her sleeves over her hands.

Ha-Kim looked at her suspiciously.

She refused to look back.

───

When they reached their building, Ha-eun stepped into the elevator with him.

She pressed their floor.

Then she looked at him.

"What are you eating tonight?"

"I don't know."

"That's a bad answer."

"It's an answer."

"You were sick two days ago."

"I'm not sick anymore."

"You still need to eat."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"Why do you care so much?"

The question came out more directly than he intended.

Ha-eun went quiet.

For a moment, she didn't know what to say.

Then she looked down.

"Because you took care of me."

Ha-Kim didn't respond.

She continued quietly.

"When I didn't know anything at work."

"When I messed up."

"When I didn't have anywhere to stay..."

She paused.

"You always helped without making me feel stupid."

The elevator continued upward.

Ha-eun smiled slightly.

"So let me worry about you sometimes."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

Something warm settled quietly in his chest.

The elevator doors opened.

Ha-eun stepped out.

Then turned around.

"I'm coming over later."

"Why?"

"Food."

"I have food."

"Good. Cook it."

The doors started closing.

Ha-Kim stared at her.

She smiled.

"Congratulations dinner."

"It's your congratulations."

"Exactly. You cook."

The doors closed.

Ha-Kim stood inside the elevator alone.

"...How does that make sense?"

───

Thirty minutes later, someone knocked.

Ha-Kim already knew who it was.

He opened the door.

Ha-eun stood there.

Empty-handed.

He looked at her hands.

"You brought nothing."

"I'm the guest."

"You invited yourself."

"Are you going to let me in?"

Ha-Kim moved aside.

She smiled and walked past him.

She took off her shoes without hesitation.

Then went straight toward the kitchen.

Ha-Kim closed the door.

"You've become very comfortable here."

Ha-eun looked back at him.

"Is that bad?"

He paused.

"No."

Her expression softened.

"Good."

She sat at the table while he started cooking.

For a while, she watched him quietly.

Ha-Kim noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're staring."

She immediately looked away.

"No, I'm not."

"You are."

"I'm waiting for food."

"With your eyes?"

"How else am I supposed to wait?"

Ha-Kim shook his head.

Ha-eun smiled to herself.

A few minutes later, he placed a plate in front of her.

She looked down.

Then up at him.

"You made this that fast?"

"It's simple."

She took a bite.

Her eyes immediately brightened.

Ha-Kim noticed.

"Good?"

She nodded quickly.

"Really good."

He sat across from her.

Ha-eun kept eating.

For a moment, neither spoke.

They didn't need to.

The apartment was quiet.

But it wasn't the same silence Ha-Kim had lived with before.

This one had the sound of plates.

Someone sitting across from him.

Someone occasionally making a small happy sound because the food tasted good.

He looked at Ha-eun.

She caught him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You keep doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Looking at me."

Ha-Kim picked up his glass.

"So do you."

Ha-eun froze.

"I do not."

"You do."

She looked down at her plate.

"Eat your food."

He almost smiled.

"That's my line."

───

The following morning felt normal.

Almost too normal.

Ha-Kim arrived at work.

Ha-eun was already at her desk.

Not beside his anymore.

Her own desk.

Her own work.

Her own responsibilities.

She looked up and noticed him.

"Morning."

"Morning."

That was all.

But she smiled.

And Ha-Kim realized something.

Training was over.

There was no reason anymore.

No assignment.
No responsibility.
No manager telling him to help her.

Yet she had still come to his apartment last night.

And he had still cooked for her.

He sat down.

For some reason, that thought stayed with him all morning.

───

By evening, Ha-Kim left the building alone.

Ha-eun had stayed behind with Ji-woo to finish something.

He walked down the steps outside Haetae Cyber.

His phone vibrated.

A CEO notification.

He glanced at it.

Nothing urgent.

He put the phone away.

The street was crowded.

People passed in every direction.

Ha-Kim adjusted his glasses and started walking.

Then he heard it.

"Ha-Kim."

He stopped.

Not because someone knew his name.

Because he knew the voice.

For several seconds, he didn't turn around.

The sounds of the street seemed quieter.

His expression changed.

Not fear.
Not anger.

Something harder to read.

Something Ha-eun had never seen on his face.

Slowly, Ha-Kim turned.

A woman stood several meters away.

Long dark-brown hair.
Glasses.
A familiar face.

One he hadn't seen in a long time.

Lee Min-seo.

Neither moved.

Min-seo stared at him.

Ha-Kim's hand slowly tightened inside his pocket.

All the warmth from the night before suddenly felt very far away.

Min-seo took one step closer.

Her eyes never left his.

"Ha-Kim."

And just like that...

Someone from before had returned.
