# A Strange Advertisement

Ha-eun walked down the street with two bags in her hands.

She had no idea where she was going.

Her apartment was gone.

She had no money for another one.

And she definitely didn't have a plan.

She stopped at a crosswalk and looked at her phone.

There were several rental apps open.

She checked one.

Too expensive.

Another.

Too far.

Another.

The room looked like it hadn't been cleaned since the beginning of time.

She closed it.

“Great.”

She looked up at the traffic light.

Cars passed in front of her.

People walked around her like everything was normal.

For them, it probably was.

For her, everything had suddenly become complicated again.

She looked down at the bags in her hands.

“I need somewhere to sleep.”

She sighed.

Then her phone buzzed.

For a second, she thought it was another message from the people she didn't want to hear from.

It wasn't.

Just an advertisement.

She ignored it.

Then she started walking again.

She didn't notice the person who had left the building several minutes after her.

Ha-Kim kept a reasonable distance behind her.

He wasn't following her.

At least, that was what he told himself.

He was just...

Walking in the same direction.

He watched as Ha-eun stopped at another rental building and looked at the advertisement board outside.

She checked the prices.

Her shoulders dropped.

Then she walked away.

Ha-Kim quietly sighed.

“This is ridiculous.”

He continued walking.

A few minutes later, Ha-eun sat down on a bench.

She placed her bags beside her and opened her phone again.

She searched.

And searched.

And searched.

Then she opened her contacts.

Ji-woo.

Her thumb hovered over the name.

She could call her.

Maybe Ji-woo would let her stay for a few days.

But that would mean explaining why.

And then there would be questions.

Questions she wasn't ready to answer.

She closed the contact list.

Her thumb moved lower.

Ha-Kim.

She stared at his name.

“No.”

She locked the phone.

“Definitely not.”

Across the street, Ha-Kim watched her lock her phone.

He couldn't hear what she had said.

But he had a pretty good idea.

He waited until she stood up again.

Then he crossed the street.

Unfortunately, Ha-eun suddenly turned around.

Ha-Kim stopped.

She stared at him.

He stared back.

There was a long silence.

“What are you doing?”

“Walking.”

She looked behind him.

Then in front of him.

Then back at him.

“You've been behind me for like twenty minutes.”

“Coincidence.”

“Twenty minutes?”

“Yes.”

“That is a very long coincidence.”

Ha-Kim looked at the bags in her hands.

“You have bags.”

She looked down.

“Yes.”

“I noticed.”

“Thank you for the information.”

He nodded.

“You're welcome.”

Ha-eun stared at him.

“Why are you like this?”

“I don't know.”

For some reason, that made her smile a little.

She quickly looked away.

Then her smile disappeared.

Ha-Kim noticed.

“You didn't go home.”

She stayed quiet.

“Ha-eun.”

She looked at him.

“Did you lose your apartment?”

Her fingers tightened around the bag handles.

For a moment, she considered telling him.

She almost did.

Instead, she looked away.

“The landlord is selling it.”

Ha-Kim said nothing.

She waited.

Nothing.

“You don't believe me, do you?”

“I didn't say that.”

“You're making that face.”

“What face?”

“That face.”

“I always look like this.”

She looked at him for a second.

“That's true.”

Ha-Kim ignored that.

“If the landlord is selling it, where are you staying tonight?”

She hesitated.

“I'll figure something out.”

“You don't have somewhere.”

“I said I'll figure it out.”

He looked at her bags again.

She was trying very hard to sound normal.

It wasn't working.

“Okay.”

Ha-eun blinked.

“Okay?”

“Okay.”

He turned around.

She stared at him.

“That’s it?”

“You said you'll figure it out.”

“That's all you're going to say?”

“What do you want me to say?”

“I don't know!”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“You're strange.”

She frowned.

“I'm the strange one?”

“Yes.”

“I'm not the one secretly following people.”

“I wasn't following you.”

“You were literally following me.”

“I was walking.”

“Behind me.”

“Yes.”

She opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

“Forget it.”

Ha-Kim almost smiled.

Almost.

Instead, he looked down the street.

“Find somewhere safe.”

“I will.”

“Don't stay outside.”

“I won't.”

He walked away.

Ha-eun watched him leave.

Then she looked at her bags.

“...What a weird guy.”

───

That evening, Ha-Kim sat at his desk.

His apartment was quiet.

His laptop was open.

Several rental listings were on the screen.

He had already checked the prices in the area.

Most were too expensive.

Some were too far from Haetae.

Others were in poor condition.

He opened another file.

One of the apartment buildings he owned was only a short distance from the company.

There was an empty studio available.

Small.

Clean.

Furnished.

Affordable.

Perfect.

He leaned back in his chair.

There was only one problem.

He couldn't simply give it to her.

If he did, she'd ask questions.

She'd refuse.

Or worse, she'd realize he had been paying attention to her problems.

Ha-eun didn't want pity.

He knew that much.

So he needed another way.

Ha-Kim opened the rental listing system.

He made the apartment available.

Adjusted the advertisement.

Nothing suspicious.

Nothing special.

Just another rental listing.

Affordable price.

Good location.

Simple description.

He looked at it once.

Then closed the laptop.

“Good enough.”

He didn't think about it again.

At least, he tried not to.

───

The next morning, Ha-eun arrived at Haetae Cyber earlier than usual.

She looked tired.

She had barely slept.

Her phone was full of rental listings.

She sat at her desk and started searching again.

Ji-woo walked past.

“Morning.”

“Morning.”

Ji-woo stopped.

“You look dead.”

“Thanks.”

“You're welcome.”

Ha-eun gave her a tired look.

Ji-woo smiled.

“Did you sleep?”

“A little.”

“How little?”

Ha-eun thought about it.

“Enough to technically call it sleeping.”

Ji-woo laughed.

Ha-eun smiled slightly.

Then her phone buzzed.

A new rental listing had appeared.

She opened it.

Her eyes slowly widened.

It was a studio apartment.

The price was...

Actually affordable.

She checked the location.

Close to work.

She checked the pictures.

Small.

Clean.

Furnished.

She scrolled down.

No ridiculous conditions.

No hidden fees listed.

She stared at the screen.

“...What's wrong with it?”

Ji-woo looked over.

“With what?”

“This.”

She showed her the phone.

Ji-woo read the listing.

“That looks pretty good.”

“That's the problem.”

“What?”

“It's too good.”

Ji-woo laughed.

“Maybe you're just lucky.”

Ha-eun looked at the screen again.

“Lucky?”

The word felt strange.

She couldn't remember the last time she had felt lucky.

Still, she saved the listing.

Then she immediately started worrying.

“What if it's a scam?”

“Then don't send money before checking it.”

“Right.”

“You've got a brain.”

“Sometimes.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“Good luck.”

Ha-eun looked at the listing again.

She didn't know it yet.

But someone had already made sure she had a chance.

───

During lunch, Ha-eun was still looking at the apartment.

She had already read the advertisement five times.

Ha-Kim noticed.

“You're looking for a place.”

She nearly dropped her phone.

“How did you know?”

“You've been staring at the same screen for ten minutes.”

“Oh.”

He looked back at his computer.

“Check the contract carefully.”

“That’s your advice?”

“Yes.”

“That's it?”

“Yes.”

She stared at him.

“You really aren't good at comforting people.”

“I wasn't trying to comfort you.”

“Obviously.”

She looked back at her phone.

Then paused.

“Do you know anything about rental scams?”

“Yes.”

Ha-eun looked at him.

“How?”

“I know things.”

“That explains absolutely nothing.”

He continued typing.

She narrowed her eyes.

“Are you secretly some kind of real estate expert?”

“No.”

“Then why do you sound like one?”

“Because you're asking bad questions.”

She frowned.

“That's rude.”

“You asked.”

She looked at him for another second.

Then quietly smiled.

“Fair.”

───

That afternoon, Ha-eun went to see the apartment.

She stood outside the building and looked up.

It wasn't new.

It wasn't fancy.

But it looked clean.

More importantly, it was quiet.

She met the landlord and looked around the studio.

A small kitchen.

A bathroom.

A bed.

A desk.

A window.

Everything she needed.

Nothing she didn't.

She walked to the window.

The city stretched out below her.

For the first time in days, her chest didn't feel so tight.

She could afford it.

Barely.

But she could.

She looked around again.

“This is actually...”

She smiled.

“Nice.”

The landlord handed her the paperwork.

She read through everything carefully.

Then signed.

Just like that, she had somewhere to live again.

She walked outside with the keys in her hand.

She stared at them.

“I got lucky.”

She smiled to herself.

For once, she wanted to believe it.

───

That evening, Ha-eun stood outside the apartment with her bags.

She looked at the keys in her hand.

She still couldn't believe it.

After everything that had happened over the past few days, she actually had somewhere to stay.

She unlocked the door and stepped inside.

The apartment was small.

A little old.

But clean.

There was a bed, a small kitchen, a desk, and enough space for her things.

It wasn't much.

But it was hers.

Ha-eun slowly put her bags down.

She walked around the room, checking everything again as if the apartment might disappear if she stopped looking at it.

The kitchen worked.

The lights worked.

The water worked.

There was even a small window facing the city.

She stood there for a moment.

Then she smiled.

“I actually got lucky.”

She sat on the edge of the bed.

For the first time since losing her old apartment, she didn't have to worry about where she would sleep that night.

Her shoulders finally relaxed.

She looked down at the keys in her hand.

“Thank you…”

She didn't know who she was thanking.

Maybe the landlord.

Maybe whoever had posted the advertisement.

Maybe just her luck.

She placed the keys on the small table beside the bed.

Then she lay down.

The mattress wasn't perfect.

It wasn't as comfortable as she wanted.

But she didn't care.

She closed her eyes.

For once, she could sleep without wondering what would happen tomorrow.

───

Across the city, Ha-Kim sat at his desk.

His apartment was quiet.

His laptop was open in front of him.

A message appeared on the screen.

Rental application approved.

Ha-Kim looked at it for a few seconds.

Then he closed the notification.

Everything had worked.

She had found the apartment herself.

She had chosen it herself.

And, as far as she knew, she had simply gotten lucky.

That was exactly what he wanted.

He leaned back in his chair.

For some reason, he felt a little less worried than he had the night before.

His phone buzzed.

He glanced at it.

Another company notification.

Ha-Kim sighed and picked it up.

Work again.

He stood from his chair.

Before leaving the room, he looked once more at the closed laptop.

“Good.”

Then he turned off the light.

Ha-eun had a home again.

For now, that was enough.
