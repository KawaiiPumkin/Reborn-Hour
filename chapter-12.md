# Chapter 12 — Seo-jun Knows

A few days had passed since Ha-eun started getting comfortable in the new building.

At first, seeing Ha-Kim in the hallway had been awkward.

Then it became normal.

Seeing him in the elevator was normal.

Running into him after work was normal.

And somehow, eating at his apartment had also started becoming normal.

Ha-eun still didn't understand how that happened.

She just knew that whenever she was hungry, there was a certain door she could knock on.

And lately, Ha-Kim had stopped asking why she was there.

That was probably the strangest part.

---

That evening, Ha-Kim was walking home with a small grocery bag in one hand.

The streets around his apartment were quieter than usual.

As he passed the convenience store, he noticed something beside the building.

A small gray cat was sitting between two cardboard boxes.

Ha-Kim slowed down.

He had seen it before.

The cat stared at him.

Ha-Kim stared back.

“You again.”

The cat blinked.

He continued walking.

Three steps later, he stopped.

Something felt wrong.

There was nothing unusual about the street.

Cars passed normally.

People walked along the sidewalk.

A delivery bike waited at the traffic light.

Everything looked fine.

But Ha-Kim looked back at the cat.

It had moved closer to the road.

He sighed.

“Of course.”

His expression became still.

Then he activated his ability.

The world disappeared.

One hour.

The street moved forward without him.

People came and went.

A worker carried boxes out of the convenience store.

Someone dropped something near the sidewalk.

The sound startled the cat.

It ran.

Straight toward the road.

A delivery bike turned the corner.

Too fast.

Ha-Kim opened his eyes.

The street returned.

The pain came immediately.

A sharp pressure formed behind his eyes.

He lowered his head and pressed two fingers against his temple.

“...Seriously?”

The cat was still there.

Ha-Kim walked toward it.

The cat immediately backed away.

“No.”

He took another step.

The cat hissed.

“I know.”

He crouched down.

“Come here.”

The cat clearly had no intention of listening.

Ha-Kim reached out anyway.

A tiny paw struck his hand.

He pulled back.

Three thin red lines appeared across his skin.

Ha-Kim stared at them.

Then at the cat.

“I just saved you.”

The cat hissed again.

“Ungrateful.”

After a short struggle, he managed to move the cat farther away from the road and placed it beside the wall behind the convenience store.

The cat immediately disappeared between the boxes.

Ha-Kim stood there.

The headache hadn't gone away.

That was strange.

Something this small shouldn't have affected him for this long.

He looked at his hand.

Then toward the apartment building.

Maybe he was just tired.

He picked up his groceries and continued walking.

---

By the time Ha-Kim reached his apartment, his body felt strangely warm.

He took off his jacket and placed it over the back of a chair.

The apartment was quiet.

As usual.

He walked into the kitchen, put the groceries away, and reached for a glass of water.

His hand paused.

Something was wrong.

He touched his forehead.

Warm.

He frowned.

After a moment, he opened a drawer and took out a thermometer.

A few seconds later, the screen beeped.

37.8°C.

Ha-Kim stared at it.

“...A fever?”

It wasn't high.

Nothing serious.

Still, it shouldn't have happened.

He put the thermometer back in the drawer.

Then his phone vibrated.

DING DONG.

The doorbell rang.

Ha-Kim looked toward the door.

Another vibration.

He already knew who it was.

He opened the door.

Seo-jun stood outside holding a plastic bag.

“Surprise.”

Ha-Kim stared at him.

“No.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“Wow. Nice to see you too.”

“What do you want?”

“I was nearby.”

“You live forty minutes away.”

“I was emotionally nearby.”

“You're annoying.”

“Missed you too.”

Ha-Kim sighed and stepped aside.

“Come in.”

Seo-jun walked inside.

Then stopped.

He looked around.

Everything seemed normal.

Until he noticed something on the dining table.

Two plates.

Two glasses.

And a second drink.

Seo-jun slowly turned toward Ha-Kim.

Ha-Kim noticed.

“Don't.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I'm just looking.”

“You're still looking.”

Seo-jun looked back at the table.

Then at Ha-Kim.

Then at the second glass.

Then at the second plate.

His eyebrows slowly rose.

“Are you expecting someone?”

“No.”

Seo-jun pointed at the table.

“Then why are there two?”

Ha-Kim paused.

“...I was hungry.”

“Two plates?”

“I was very hungry.”

“Right.”

Ha-Kim walked toward the kitchen.

Seo-jun followed.

“Something's different.”

“Nothing is different.”

“You have two plates.”

“You've mentioned that.”

“And two glasses.”

“Seo-jun.”

“And—”

The doorbell rang.

Both of them stopped.

Ha-Kim looked at the door.

Seo-jun looked at Ha-Kim.

A tiny smile appeared on his face.

“Oh.”

Ha-Kim's expression immediately changed.

“Don't.”

Seo-jun grinned.

“I didn't even say anything.”

“You're thinking loudly.”

Ha-Kim walked toward the door.

He opened it.

Ha-eun stood there holding a small bag.

She looked at Ha-Kim.

Then at Seo-jun.

Then back at Ha-Kim.

“Oh.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“Hi.”

Ha-eun blinked.

“Hi.”

She looked at Ha-Kim.

“You have a guest.”

“Yes.”

“I can come back later.”

“No.”

Ha-eun looked surprised.

Ha-Kim stepped aside.

“Come in.”

Seo-jun's eyebrows went up.

Ha-eun walked inside.

She took off her shoes without being asked and placed them neatly beside the door.

Seo-jun watched.

Ha-Kim noticed him watching.

“Don't.”

Seo-jun whispered.

“I haven't said anything.”

“You're doing it again.”

Ha-eun looked between them.

“Are you two always like this?”

“Yes,” Ha-Kim said.

“No,” Seo-jun said at the same time.

Ha-eun smiled.

“I see.”

Seo-jun held out his hand.

“I'm Kang Seo-jun.”

Ha-eun shook it.

“Choi Ha-eun.”

“I know.”

She looked at Ha-Kim.

“He knows my name?”

Ha-Kim answered calmly.

“He works here.”

Seo-jun nodded.

“Exactly.”

Ha-eun sat down at the table.

Then she looked at the food.

Her eyes brightened slightly.

“Did you make this?”

Ha-Kim nodded.

“For yourself?”

“Yes.”

She looked at the second plate.

“...Then why is there another one?”

Silence.

Seo-jun slowly turned toward Ha-Kim.

Ha-Kim looked at him.

“Don't.”

Seo-jun covered his mouth.

Ha-eun tilted her head.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Seo-jun sat down.

He was already enjoying this far too much.

Ha-Kim placed another bowl on the table.

Ha-eun immediately picked up her chopsticks.

Seo-jun watched her take the first bite.

Her expression changed.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

Her shoulders relaxed.

Her eyes softened.

A small smile appeared.

“It's good.”

Ha-Kim sat across from her.

“You've eaten it before.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you surprised?”

“Because it's still good.”

Seo-jun looked at Ha-Kim.

Then at Ha-eun.

Then back at Ha-Kim.

“You cook?”

“Yes.”

“You cook for her?”

Ha-Kim's eyes narrowed.

“Apparently, I cook for anyone who asks.”

Ha-eun swallowed.

“I didn't ask today.”

Seo-jun raised an eyebrow.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She smiled innocently.

“I just came.”

“That somehow makes it worse,” Seo-jun said.

Ha-eun looked at him.

“Why?”

“I don't know yet.”

She laughed quietly.

Ha-Kim picked up his chopsticks.

Seo-jun leaned toward Ha-eun.

“So how often do you come here?”

Ha-eun thought about it.

“Not that often.”

Ha-Kim glanced at her.

“Three times this week.”

She looked at him.

“That's not that often.”

Seo-jun stared.

“Three times?”

Ha-eun nodded.

“Sometimes I just get hungry.”

Seo-jun slowly turned toward Ha-Kim.

“You've become a restaurant.”

“Eat your food.”

“I haven't even started.”

“Then start.”

Ha-eun smiled again.

For some reason, Seo-jun found the whole thing funny.

Not because Ha-Kim had a guest.

That wasn't unusual.

It was the fact that Ha-Kim looked completely normal about it.

A few months ago, Seo-jun couldn't imagine Ha-Kim willingly letting someone into his apartment this often.

Now there was a girl sitting at his table, eating his food, drinking his drinks, and apparently knowing where everything was.

And Ha-Kim wasn't pushing her away.

That alone told Seo-jun enough.

---

After dinner, Ha-eun stood and carried her bowl toward the kitchen.

“I'll wash this.”

“You don't have to.”

“I ate it.”

“I made it.”

“So?”

Ha-Kim stared at her.

Ha-eun stared back.

Seo-jun watched the silent argument.

Finally, Ha-Kim handed her another bowl.

“Fine.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“Thanks.”

She walked into the kitchen.

Seo-jun waited.

Then quietly leaned toward Ha-Kim.

“So.”

“No.”

“You don't even know what I'm going to say.”

“Yes, I do.”

“You like having her here.”

Ha-Kim looked at him.

“She eats my food.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It is.”

“It's really not.”

Ha-Kim ignored him.

Seo-jun smiled.

“You're different.”

Ha-Kim's expression barely changed.

“People change.”

“Not you.”

Ha-Kim looked toward the kitchen.

Ha-eun was humming quietly while washing the dishes.

His eyes stayed there for a second longer than necessary.

Seo-jun noticed.

He didn't tease him this time.

Instead, he smiled to himself.

“Yeah.”

Ha-Kim looked back.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

---

A few minutes later, Ha-eun returned.

She sat down again.

Seo-jun was looking at her.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You're staring.”

“I was just wondering something.”

“What?”

“How did you get him to let you come here so often?”

Ha-eun looked at Ha-Kim.

Then back at Seo-jun.

“I don't know.”

“You just show up?”

“Sometimes.”

“And he lets you in?”

“Usually.”

Seo-jun slowly nodded.

“Wow.”

Ha-Kim sighed.

“Are you done?”

“No.”

“Then leave.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“You're embarrassed.”

“I'm tired.”

“Same thing.”

Ha-eun covered her mouth to hide a small laugh.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“You too?”

“Sorry.”

“You're not.”

“No.”

Seo-jun laughed.

For a moment, the apartment felt different.

Not quieter.

Not louder.

Just warmer.

Ha-Kim sat there listening to the two of them talk.

He didn't feel the usual need to end the conversation.

That was new.

Then a sharp pain hit behind his eyes.

He closed them for a moment.

Ha-eun noticed.

“Are you okay?”

Ha-Kim opened his eyes.

“Yeah.”

“You look tired.”

“I'm fine.”

She studied him.

“You're not.”

“I'm fine.”

Seo-jun looked at him.

“You actually do look terrible.”

“Thank you.”

“You're welcome.”

Ha-eun stood.

She walked closer.

“Are you sick?”

“No.”

She placed the back of her hand against his forehead.

Ha-Kim froze.

Seo-jun froze.

Ha-eun didn't notice either of them.

She frowned.

“You're warm.”

Ha-Kim slowly looked at her.

“I know.”

“You have a fever?”

“A small one.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“There was nothing to tell.”

Seo-jun looked between them.

His expression was becoming increasingly amused.

Ha-eun pulled her hand back.

“You should rest.”

“I will.”

“And don't cook tomorrow.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because you're sick.”

“I can cook.”

“You can barely stand properly.”

“I can stand.”

“Barely.”

Seo-jun nodded.

“She has a point.”

Ha-Kim looked at him.

“You're taking her side?”

“I'm taking the side of whoever lets me survive this conversation.”

Ha-eun smiled.

Then she looked at the kitchen.

“I can make something tomorrow.”

Ha-Kim immediately said:

“No.”

She frowned.

“Why?”

“I've seen you cook.”

Seo-jun looked interested.

“What happened?”

Ha-eun's face turned red.

“Nothing.”

Ha-Kim answered at the same time.

“The smoke detector disagreed.”

Seo-jun burst out laughing.

“You almost burned the place down?”

“I didn't almost burn it down!”

“You made the fire alarm go off.”

“It was one time!”

Seo-jun looked at Ha-Kim.

“And you still let her cook?”

“No.”

Ha-eun crossed her arms.

“I can improve.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“You can.”

She blinked.

Then smiled.

“See?”

Seo-jun noticed the tiny smile on Ha-Kim's face.

He didn't say anything.

He just leaned back.

---

Later that evening, Seo-jun finally stood up.

“I should go.”

Ha-eun looked at him.

“Already?”

Seo-jun glanced at Ha-Kim.

Ha-Kim looked relieved.

“Yes. Before he throws me out.”

“I wasn't going to throw you out.”

“You've been trying for an hour.”

“That's because you talk too much.”

Seo-jun put on his shoes.

Ha-eun stood beside the door.

“It was nice meeting you.”

“You too.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“Come over whenever you want.”

Ha-Kim looked at him.

“Don't encourage her.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“I was going to anyway.”

Seo-jun laughed.

“I like her.”

Ha-Kim sighed.

“I noticed.”

Seo-jun opened the door.

Then looked back at Ha-Kim.

For a second, his expression became more serious.

“Take care of yourself.”

Ha-Kim nodded.

“I will.”

Seo-jun left.

The door closed.

Silence returned.

Ha-eun looked at Ha-Kim.

“He's nice.”

“He's annoying.”

“You two are friends.”

“Unfortunately.”

She smiled.

Then picked up her small bag.

“I should go too.”

Ha-Kim nodded.

“Okay.”

She opened the door.

Then looked back.

“Thanks for dinner.”

“You're welcome.”

“And the other dinner.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“And the one before that.”

“You're keeping count?”

“No.”

She smiled.

“Good night, Ha-Kim.”

“Good night.”

She stepped into the hallway.

Then turned around.

“Get some sleep.”

“I will.”

“Promise?”

Ha-Kim paused.

“...Promise.”

She nodded.

“Good.”

The door closed.

Ha-Kim stood there for a few seconds.

The apartment was quiet again.

He walked back to the kitchen.

Two plates were still sitting in the sink.

He looked at them.

Then at the empty chair where Ha-eun had been sitting.

His head started hurting again.

He pressed his fingers against his temple.

The pain was stronger now.

His body felt warmer.

He walked to the drawer and took out the thermometer again.

38.1°C.

Ha-Kim stared at the number.

His expression slowly changed.

The cat.

The vision.

The headache.

The fever.

Something didn't make sense.

He sat down.

For a moment, he considered using his ability again.

Then stopped.

Not tonight.

He closed his eyes.

The apartment became silent.

But unlike before, the silence didn't feel completely empty anymore.

He opened his eyes.

“...Tomorrow.”

He turned off the light.

Outside, Seoul continued moving.

And somewhere in the darkness beyond what he could see, something was already waiting.

Something that would not fit inside one hour.
