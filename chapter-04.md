# Chapter 4 — You’re Worse Than Me

The next morning, Ha-eun stood in front of her mirror for longer than usual.

She looked at herself.

Then at her clothes.

Then back at herself.

“...This is fine.”

It wasn't.

At least, that's what she thought.

Her shirt was clean, but she had worn it before.

Her pants were clean too.

Her shoes were starting to look old.

She quietly pulled down the sleeves of her shirt.

Everyone at Haetae Cyber looked so professional.

Clean suits.

Nice shirts.

Expensive-looking shoes.

Even the interns seemed like they had everything figured out.

Ha-eun looked down at herself again.

“Whatever.”

She grabbed her bag and left.

She didn't have time to worry about clothes.

She had a job now.

That was enough.

───

Haetae Cyber was already busy when she arrived.

People walked through the huge entrance while talking on their phones, carrying laptops, or drinking coffee.

Ha-eun walked through the lobby quietly.

She tried not to look at anyone.

A woman passed her wearing a clean black blazer.

Ha-eun glanced at her.

Then at her own shirt.

She quickly looked away.

“Don't think about it.”

She reached the elevator.

When the doors opened, she stepped inside.

A few employees entered after her.

She moved toward the corner.

One of them looked at her ID badge.

“You're new, right?”

Ha-eun nodded.

“Yeah.”

“Intern?”

“Training employee.”

“Oh.”

The woman nodded and went back to her phone.

Ha-eun wasn't sure whether that was good or bad.

She stared at the elevator numbers.

When the doors opened, she quickly stepped out.

She didn't notice the woman looking after her.

───

Ha-Kim was already at his desk.

Same black shirt.

Same glasses.

Same messy hair.

Same expression that made it impossible to tell what he was thinking.

Ha-eun stopped beside his desk.

“Morning.”

He looked up.

“Morning.”

She waited.

He looked back at his screen.

That was it.

Ha-eun blinked.

“...That's all?”

“What else?”

She stared at him.

“Nothing.”

She walked to her desk.

Ha-Kim watched her for a second before returning to his computer.

She was wearing the same shoes as yesterday.

He noticed.

He didn't say anything.

───

The morning started normally.

Ha-Kim gave her a few tasks.

Nothing complicated.

This time, Ha-eun managed most of them without asking for help.

She was still slow, but she was improving.

At one point, she stopped typing.

“Ha-Kim.”

“What?”

“Can I ask something?”

“Sure.”

“How long did it take you to understand all this?”

He looked at the screen.

“Not long.”

She stared at him.

“That's not helpful.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun sighed.

“You really aren't good at answering questions.”

“I answer them.”

“You technically answer them.”

He looked at her.

She looked back.

A few seconds passed.

Then Ha-eun quietly smiled.

“Never mind.”

She continued working.

Ha-Kim turned back to his screen.

The corner of his mouth moved slightly.

Barely.

───

Around eleven, Ha-eun walked toward the printer.

Two employees were standing nearby.

She waited for them to finish.

One of them glanced at her.

“You're the new girl, right?”

Ha-eun nodded.

“Yeah.”

“You're working with Ha-Kim?”

“Yeah.”

The employee looked surprised.

“Good luck.”

Ha-eun frowned.

“Why?”

The other employee laughed.

“He's difficult.”

Ha-eun looked toward Ha-Kim's desk.

He was staring at his monitor like the entire office didn't exist.

“I noticed.”

They laughed.

Ha-eun smiled slightly.

Then one of them asked casually,

“Which university are you from?”

Ha-eun froze.

“University?”

“Yeah.”

The question was completely normal.

But she didn't answer.

The employee noticed the hesitation.

“Oh. Sorry.”

“It's fine.”

Ha-eun took the papers from the printer.

She looked down.

“I didn't go.”

The two employees went quiet.

“Ah.”

Ha-eun forced a small smile.

“It's okay.”

She walked away before they could say anything else.

Her grip tightened around the papers.

She hated that question.

Not because there was anything wrong with not going to university.

She just didn't know what to say afterward.

Everyone had a story.

University.

High school.

Qualifications.

Plans.

She didn't have any of that.

She had survived.

That was different.

She returned to her desk.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

He didn't ask what happened.

He simply looked at the papers in her hands.

Then at her.

“Everything okay?”

Ha-eun nodded.

“Yeah.”

He stared for another second.

“Okay.”

Then he went back to work.

For some reason, that made her feel better.

He didn't push.

He didn't ask questions she didn't want to answer.

───

At lunch, Ha-eun sat alone.

She opened her wallet.

There wasn't much inside.

She counted the money.

Once.

Twice.

Then closed it.

She looked toward the cafeteria.

The smell of food reached her.

Her stomach quietly growled.

She ignored it.

“Not today.”

She took out a small piece of bread from her bag.

It was the last thing she had brought from home.

She started eating.

A shadow stopped beside her.

Ha-eun looked up.

Ha-Kim.

He looked at the bread.

Then at her.

“You call that lunch?”

Ha-eun looked down.

“It's food.”

“Technically.”

She frowned.

“What's that supposed to mean?”

“Nothing.”

He sat down across from her.

She looked surprised.

“You eat here?”

“Sometimes.”

She looked at his tray.

He had barely touched his food.

“You're not eating either.”

“I'm eating.”

“You've been staring at it for five minutes.”

He looked at his food.

Then took a bite.

Ha-eun almost smiled.

“Happy?”

“No.”

“Then why did you do it?”

“Because you were staring.”

She looked away, trying to hide her smile.

“You’re weird.”

“So I've heard.”

She looked at his tray again.

“You know, you're worse than me.”

Ha-Kim looked up.

“At what?”

“Eating.”

He stared at her.

“You're eating bread.”

“And you're arguing with someone eating bread while your actual food gets cold.”

Ha-Kim looked down at his tray.

Then back at her.

“Fair.”

Ha-eun smiled.

They ate quietly.

For once, Ha-eun didn't feel like she had to fill the silence.

───

After lunch, Ha-eun returned to her desk.

She noticed something.

A small security report on the screen.

She looked at it.

Then again.

Something didn't look right.

She wasn't sure why.

She compared two lines.

Then three.

“Ha-Kim.”

“What?”

“I think this number is wrong.”

He turned around.

She pointed at the screen.

“This one.”

He stood up.

Looked at it.

Then at her.

“You're right.”

Ha-eun blinked.

“Really?”

“Yeah.”

She smiled.

“I found something.”

“You did.”

She looked strangely proud of herself.

Ha-Kim returned to his desk.

“Good.”

She stared at the screen.

Then smiled again.

Maybe she wasn't completely useless.

───

A few minutes later, someone walked past Ha-Kim's desk.

“Hey.”

Ha-Kim looked up.

A man placed a folder on his desk.

“Manager wants this checked before four.”

Ha-Kim looked at the folder.

“Okay.”

The man walked away.

Ha-eun glanced at him.

“You have a lot of work.”

“Yes.”

“And you're still training me.”

“Unfortunately.”

She looked offended.

“Unfortunately?”

“That was a joke.”

She stared at him.

“You make terrible jokes.”

“I know.”

She quietly laughed.

“At least you're aware.”

Ha-Kim returned to his screen.

Then his eyes moved toward the folder.

Something caught his attention.

He opened it.

Read the first page.

Then the second.

He stopped.

“There's a mistake.”

Ha-eun looked over.

“Where?”

He pointed.

She looked.

Nothing seemed wrong.

“What mistake?”

“This number.”

She checked it.

Then checked the original file.

Her eyes widened.

“How did you see that?”

Ha-Kim closed the folder.

“It was obvious.”

“It wasn't.”

“It was.”

She looked at him suspiciously.

“You're secretly a robot.”

“No.”

“Are you sure?”

“Pretty sure.”

She smiled.

“That's exactly what a robot would say.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“You're getting worse.”

“Worse at what?”

“Talking.”

She looked away, hiding another smile.

───

Later that afternoon, someone dropped a stack of papers near the entrance.

Several sheets scattered across the floor.

Ha-eun immediately stood up to help.

Before she could reach them, Ha-Kim was already there.

He caught one sheet before it landed beneath a desk.

Ha-eun stared.

“That was fast.”

He looked at the paper.

“Was it?”

“Yeah.”

He handed it over.

“Lucky.”

She narrowed her eyes.

“Sure.”

Ha-Kim walked away.

She watched him for a moment.

Something about him was strange.

Not bad strange.

Just...

She couldn't explain it.

He noticed things too quickly.

He rarely spoke.

He never seemed surprised.

And sometimes she felt like he already knew what was going to happen.

She shook the thought away.

“He's just observant.”

Probably.

───

Near the end of the day, the elevator doors opened.

A familiar voice came from behind Ha-Kim.

“You're still here?”

Ha-Kim didn't turn around.

“Unfortunately.”

A man walked beside him.

He had a completely different energy from Ha-Kim.

He smiled easily.

“You're really bad at greetings.”

Ha-Kim looked at him.

“So are you.”

The man laughed.

Ha-eun recognized him from around the building.

She hadn't talked to him before.

He noticed her.

“Oh.”

He looked at Ha-Kim.

Then at Ha-eun.

Then back at Ha-Kim.

“So this is her.”

Ha-Kim's expression didn't change.

“Apparently.”

Ha-eun looked between them.

“Do you two know each other?”

The man smiled.

“Yeah.”

Ha-Kim answered at the same time.

“No.”

They both stopped.

Ha-eun blinked.

The man laughed.

“Wow.”

Ha-Kim sighed.

“We work together.”

“That's not what you said.”

“I changed my answer.”

Ha-eun looked at the man.

“What's your name?”

“Kang Seo-jun.”

He smiled.

“Nice to meet you.”

“Choi Ha-eun.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun looked at Ha-Kim.

“He said that too.”

Seo-jun looked at Ha-Kim.

“You say that to everyone?”

“Apparently.”

Seo-jun laughed.

Ha-Kim didn't.

But there was something about the way they spoke.

Like they had been doing this for years.

Ha-eun couldn't figure it out.

Seo-jun looked at her again.

“How's the training?”

Ha-eun sighed.

“Hard.”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“She’s improving.”

Seo-jun raised an eyebrow.

“That's pretty good coming from you.”

Ha-Kim stared at him.

Seo-jun smiled.

“Nothing.”

He didn't explain.

Ha-eun noticed.

Something was going on between them.

She just didn't know what.

───

When the workday finally ended, Ha-eun packed her things.

She checked her phone.

No messages.

No missed calls.

Nothing.

She put it away.

As she walked toward the elevator, she passed a large glass wall.

Her reflection appeared in it.

She stopped.

Same shirt.

Same shoes.

Same tired face.

She adjusted her sleeves.

Then looked away.

“Tomorrow.”

She would figure something out tomorrow.

For now, she had a job.

That mattered.

The elevator opened.

She stepped inside.

The doors began closing.

At the last second, Ha-Kim's hand stopped them.

He stepped inside.

Neither spoke.

The elevator descended.

Ha-eun looked at the floor.

Then at him.

“Hey.”

“Hm?”

“Thanks.”

“For what?”

She thought about it.

“Training me.”

Ha-Kim looked forward.

“You don't need to thank me.”

“Still.”

He nodded.

“Okay.”

The elevator continued downward.

Ha-eun smiled quietly.

Ha-Kim watched the numbers above the door.

His expression remained unreadable.

But for a brief moment, his eyes shifted toward her reflection in the elevator wall.

Same clothes.

Old shoes.

No lunch.

No university.

He noticed all of it.

He didn't ask.

The elevator doors opened.

Ha-eun walked out.

“See you tomorrow.”

“See you.”

She left.

Ha-Kim stayed inside for another second.

The doors closed.

His expression returned to normal.

Quiet.

Unreadable.

Almost impossible to understand.

Then his phone vibrated.

He looked down.

CEO CHANNEL: PRIORITY MESSAGE.

Ha-Kim stared at the screen.

His eyes narrowed slightly.

Then the screen went dark.
