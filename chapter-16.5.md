# Chapter 16.5 — Something She Couldn’t Ignore

The days after Min-seo appeared looked normal.

Ha-eun still went to work.
She still spent her breaks with Ji-woo, complained about difficult tasks, and occasionally found herself pulled into conversations she barely understood.

After work, she still visited Ha-Kim.

But something had changed.

Not between them.

At least, she didn't think so.

It was inside her.

───

That evening, Ha-eun knocked on Ha-Kim's door with a small plastic bag in her hand.

The door opened.

"You're early."

Ha-eun blinked.

"I live across the hall."

"I know."

"Then why are you saying I'm early?"

"You usually come later."

She stared at him.

"...You notice that?"

"You knock loudly."

"I do not."

"You do."

Ha-eun frowned.

"Whatever."

She stepped inside and took off her shoes.

Ha-Kim glanced at the bag.

"What did you bring?"

"Nothing."

He looked at the bag again.

She slowly moved it behind her back.

"Okay. Maybe something."

"What?"

"Food."

"You said nothing."

"I changed my mind."

He stared at her.

She stared back.

"I'm hungry."

"That explains it."

She smiled.

"See? You understand me."

Ha-Kim turned toward the kitchen.

"Sit down."

Ha-eun followed him.

She sat at the table while he started preparing dinner.

For a while, everything felt normal.

The sound of plates.
The quiet movement around the kitchen.
The occasional short conversation.

Nothing special.

And somehow, Ha-eun liked it.

She rested her cheek against her hand and watched him.

His sleeves were rolled up slightly.
His glasses had slipped lower on his nose.
His hair was messier than usual after a long day.

He looked tired.

But comfortable.

Ha-eun smiled.

"You're staring."

Her eyes widened.

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"No."

"Yes."

She looked away.

"Just cook."

"You're strange."

"You're worse."

The corner of his mouth moved slightly.

Ha-eun noticed.

That tiny smile still made her happy.

She didn't know when she had become this comfortable around him.

Maybe it was the food.
Maybe it was the quiet.
Maybe it was simply because Ha-Kim never made her feel like she had to be someone else.

She could come over hungry.
She could be tired.
She could complain.
She could sit in silence.

And he never made her feel like she was bothering him.

She liked that.

More than she wanted to admit.

Then Ha-Kim's phone lit up on the counter.

Ha-eun's eyes moved toward it.

A name appeared on the screen.

Min-seo.

Her smile faded.

Only for a moment.

Ha-Kim noticed.

He picked up the phone and checked the message.

Ha-eun looked back at the table.

She didn't ask.

She already knew who it was.

───

Dinner continued.

Ha-eun tried to act normal.

"These vegetables are too healthy."

"You said you wanted to eat better."

"I changed my mind."

"You do that often."

She poked a vegetable with her chopsticks.

"I'm only eating it because you made it."

"That sounds like a threat."

She smiled.

But this time, it was smaller.

Ha-Kim noticed.

He didn't say anything.

After dinner, Ha-eun helped him clean the dishes.

She stood beside him at the sink while he dried them.

Their shoulders bumped.

"Sorry."

"You don't have to apologize."

"I know."

A few seconds passed.

Then Ha-eun spoke.

"Ha-Kim."

"Hm?"

"Can I ask you something?"

"Yes."

She hesitated.

"Never mind."

"You already asked."

"I know."

"So ask."

She looked down at the dish in her hands.

"Min-seo…"

She stopped.

Ha-Kim waited.

"She's someone important to you, right?"

He was quiet for a moment.

"She was."

Ha-eun's fingers tightened slightly around the dish.

"Was?"

"Yes."

She nodded.

"Oh."

She rinsed the dish and handed it to him.

Ha-Kim watched her.

"You're thinking too much."

She looked at him.

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"You've been quieter."

"I've always been quiet."

"Not like this."

She didn't answer.

He didn't push.

When they finished cleaning, Ha-eun sat on the couch.

Ha-Kim sat across from her.

Usually, she would have complained about the distance and moved closer.

Tonight, she didn't.

She looked around the apartment.

It had become familiar to her.

Almost too familiar.

She knew where he kept the cups.
She knew which cabinet held the snacks.
She knew which chair he preferred.
She knew what his apartment sounded like when neither of them spoke.

It felt comfortable.

Almost like home.

And that was what scared her.

She liked being here.

She liked him.

A lot.

Ha-eun looked down at her hands.

"Can I tell you something stupid?"

Ha-Kim nodded.

"I sometimes think you're too good to be around someone like me."

He didn't answer immediately.

Ha-eun quickly smiled.

"See? Stupid."

"Why do you think that?"

She shrugged.

"I don't know."

She did know.

She just didn't know how to explain it.

"I don't have much."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"I didn't go to university."

He stayed quiet.

"I don't know a lot of things."

"You're learning."

"I mess up."

"You're getting better."

She looked down.

"My clothes aren't exactly…"

She glanced at herself.

Ha-Kim followed her gaze.

"They're clothes."

She gave him a weak smile.

"You make everything sound simple."

"Most things are."

"Not this."

He didn't argue.

For a moment, Ha-eun almost told him everything.

That seeing Min-seo had made her feel small.
That Min-seo seemed so comfortable around him.
That she knew things about him Ha-eun didn't.
That sometimes Ha-eun wondered what someone like him could possibly see in someone like her.

But the words stayed in her throat.

So she stood.

"I should go."

Ha-Kim glanced at the clock.

"It's not late."

"I know."

"You can stay."

Her hand stopped on the door handle.

She looked back at him.

"You don't have to say that just because I'm leaving."

"I didn't."

She stared at him.

He stared back.

Then she smiled.

A real one this time.

"Good night, Ha-Kim."

"Good night."

She opened the door.

Before leaving, she looked back once more.

"Tomorrow?"

He understood.

"Tomorrow."

She nodded and left.

The door closed.

Ha-Kim remained where he was.

The apartment became quiet again.

But this time, the silence didn't feel normal.

Something was bothering her.

He just didn't know what.

───

Ha-eun walked across the hallway to her own apartment.

She stopped outside her door.

For a few seconds, she just stood there.

"Why am I thinking like this?"

She knew the answer.

She just didn't want to say it.

She remembered Min-seo standing in Ha-Kim's doorway.

The way she spoke to him.
The way she seemed comfortable around him.
The little things she knew about him.

Seven years.

That was a long time.

Ha-eun had only known Ha-Kim for months.

She suddenly wondered how much of his life existed before she ever met him.

And for the first time, a thought she didn't want entered her mind.

Maybe Ha-Kim had simply been kind to her.

Maybe she had mistaken that kindness for something more.

She shook her head.

"No…"

She didn't believe that.

Not completely.

Because Ha-Kim remembered things about her too.

He noticed when she was tired.
He noticed when she hadn't eaten.
He cooked for her.
He waited for her.
He let her into his quiet world without asking her to earn a place in it.

Those things meant something.

Didn't they?

Ha-eun entered her apartment and closed the door.

She leaned against it and covered her face.

"I'm thinking too much again."

She stood there for a few seconds.

Then she smiled quietly.

Because despite everything going through her head...

She still wanted to see him tomorrow.

───

Across the hall, Ha-Kim stood near his window.

His phone was still in his hand.

Another message had arrived.

From Min-seo.

He didn't open it.

Instead, he looked toward the apartment across the hall.

A light had turned on.

Ha-eun was home.

He watched it for a moment.

Then his expression softened.

Whatever was bothering her, he would figure it out.

But he wouldn't force her to talk.

If she wanted to tell him, he would listen.

Until then, he would simply be there.

Like he always had.
