Chapter 23 — Living Together
It happened without either of them planning it.
For the past few weeks, Ha-eun had been spending more nights at Ha-Kim's apartment than her own.
At first, it was because she was tired.
Then because she wanted to eat with him.
Then because she liked sleeping beside him.
Eventually, she stopped making excuses.
She would wake up beside him, get ready for work, and leave his apartment in the morning.
Her own apartment across the hall slowly became a place she only visited to pick something up.
One evening, Ha-Kim was putting dishes away when Ha-eun stood in the kitchen doorway.
“Ha-Kim.”
“Hm?”
“Can I ask you something?”
He looked over.
“What?”
She hesitated.
Then—
“Why don't we just live together?”
Ha-Kim stopped.
The plate in his hand remained halfway inside the cabinet.
Ha-eun immediately became nervous.
“I mean... we're basically doing it already.”
He looked around the apartment.
Her mug was on the counter.
Her slippers were beside the couch.
Her charger was plugged beside his bed.
There were clothes of hers in his closet.
She had even left a bottle of shampoo in his bathroom.
He looked back at her.
“You're not wrong.”
Her shoulders relaxed.
“So?”
“So...”
He closed the cabinet.
“Okay.”
Ha-eun blinked.
“That's it?”
“What else should I say?”
“I don't know. Something.”
“Like what?”
She thought.
“Maybe... ‘Ha-eun, let's live together.’”
Ha-Kim stared at her.
Then, completely seriously, said,
“Ha-eun, let's live together.”
She smiled.
“Better.”
───
The next day, they started moving her things.
Ha-eun stood in her old apartment surrounded by boxes.
She looked around.
It was strange seeing the place so empty.
She had lived here alone.
There had been nights when the apartment felt too quiet.
Nights when she had eaten alone.
Nights when she wondered how long she could keep living like this.
Now she looked at the same rooms and felt something completely different.
She was leaving.
Not because she had nowhere to go.
Because she had somewhere to go.
Ha-Kim appeared at the doorway.
“Ready?”
Ha-eun nodded.
“Yeah.”
He picked up one of the boxes.
She reached for another.
“I can carry that.”
“I know.”
“You always say that.”
“And you always try to carry everything yourself.”
She smiled.
“Habit.”
He took the box from her anyway.
She followed him across the hallway.
───
By the afternoon, most of her belongings were in Ha-Kim's apartment.
Clothes.
Books.
Kitchen supplies.
A few old photographs.
Her favorite mug.
Even the small things she hadn't realized she owned until she started packing them.
Ha-eun stood in the middle of the living room.
She looked around.
Then frowned.
“It's still too gloomy.”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“We just moved your entire apartment in here.”
“I know.”
“And you're already complaining.”
“I'm not complaining.”
She pointed at the curtains.
“I'm observing.”
“They're curtains.”
“They're dark.”
“They block sunlight.”
“Exactly.”
Ha-Kim sighed.
“What do you want to do?”
Ha-eun smiled.
“I want to go shopping.”
“For what?”
“Everything.”
“No.”
“Please.”
“Ha-Kim.”
He looked at her.
She smiled.
He already knew he had lost.
“Fine.”
Her smile widened.
“Let's go.”
───
The decoration store was a disaster.
Not because anything went wrong.
Because Ha-eun wanted everything.
She picked up cushions.
Then lamps.
Then plants.
Then small decorations.
Then picture frames.
Then another plant.
Ha-Kim followed behind her with a basket.
“You already chose three.”
“This one is different.”
“They're all plants.”
“They're different plants.”
“They're green.”
“That doesn't mean they're the same.”
She placed another one into the basket.
Ha-Kim looked at it.
“Where are you putting that?”
“Near the window.”
“There are already two there.”
“Now there'll be three.”
“Why?”
“Because three looks better.”
He stared at her.
She stared back.
“Don't judge me.”
“I am.”
She walked away.
A few minutes later, she held up two cushions.
“This one?”
“Left.”
“And this?”
“Right.”
“So you like both?”
“No.”
“Then why did you tell me where they were?”
“You asked me which hand.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
Then laughed quietly.
“You're impossible.”
They continued through the store.
She picked warm-colored curtains.
A soft rug.
A few small lamps.
Some framed pictures.
A little clock.
A vase.
A couple of plants.
And, after several minutes of arguing, a small ceramic cat.
Ha-Kim looked at it.
“No.”
“It's cute.”
“It looks stupid.”
“It has a cute face.”
“It has no face.”
“It does.”
“That's a blank circle.”
Ha-eun put it in the basket.
“It's coming home.”
Ha-Kim sighed.
“Fine.”
She smiled.
───
They spent the next day rearranging the apartment.
The dark curtains came down.
The new ones went up.
The lighting changed.
Plants appeared beside the windows.
The empty shelves gained decorations.
The couch gained new cushions.
The rug became softer.
A few pictures went on the walls.
The apartment slowly became brighter.
More colorful.
More alive.
Ha-Kim stood in the middle of the living room while Ha-eun moved one of the plants three centimeters to the left.
“Done.”
He looked around.
The apartment was still his.
But it didn't feel like the same place.
There was more light.
More color.
More signs that someone lived here.
Someone cared about the place.
Ha-eun looked at him.
“What do you think?”
He took another look.
“It's good.”
“Just good?”
“Very good.”
Her eyes widened.
“That sounded almost enthusiastic.”
“Don't push it.”
She smiled.
“I knew you'd like it.”
He didn't answer.
But he did.
More than he expected.
───
Living together turned out to be different from simply spending the night together.
There were mornings when Ha-eun refused to get out of bed.
“Five more minutes.”
“You said that ten minutes ago.”
“I changed my mind.”
“We're going to be late.”
“Then call in sick.”
“No.”
“Why?”
“Because we're not children.”
She buried her face in the pillow.
“You're no fun.”
“You knew that.”
Eventually, she got up.
Barely.
Other mornings, Ha-Kim woke first and made breakfast.
Ha-eun would stumble into the kitchen with messy hair and sit at the table.
“What is it?”
“Eggs.”
“Again?”
“You like eggs.”
“I do.”
She started eating.
After a few bites, she smiled.
“It's good.”
Ha-Kim glanced at her.
“Obviously.”
She kicked his foot under the table.
───
They argued too.
Usually about stupid things.
“You ate the last snack.”
“You said you didn't want it.”
“I said I wasn't hungry.”
“Same thing.”
“No, it isn't.”
“It is.”
“You're impossible.”
“You're the one arguing over snacks.”
“Because they were mine.”
“They were in the shared cabinet.”
“That's theft.”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“You're dramatic.”
She crossed her arms.
“You're annoying.”
Five minutes later, she was sitting beside him on the couch.
Neither mentioned the argument.
───
Working from home was worse.
Ha-eun would sometimes sit at the table with her laptop.
Ha-Kim would work nearby.
For a while, everything would be quiet.
Then—
“Ha-Kim.”
“Yes?”
“Can I ask something?”
“What?”
“Do you think this is right?”
He looked over at her screen.
He didn't give her the answer immediately.
Instead, he pointed at one section.
“What do you think?”
She frowned.
“I think... this part is wrong.”
“Why?”
She explained.
He listened.
Then nodded.
“Good.”
Her eyes brightened slightly.
“I got it?”
“You got it.”
She smiled and went back to work.
Ha-Kim returned to his own screen.
A few minutes later, he glanced at her again.
She was concentrating hard, biting the inside of her cheek while reading.
He looked back at his work.
Then, without realizing it, smiled.
───
Some nights, they cooked together.
Well—
Ha-eun tried.
“Don't touch that.”
“I know what I'm doing.”
“You're holding the knife wrong.”
“No, I'm not.”
“You are.”
She looked down.
Then changed how she was holding it.
“...Maybe.”
Ha-Kim took the knife from her.
“Give it to me.”
“You don't trust me.”
“I trust you.”
“You clearly don't.”
“I trust you not to intentionally destroy dinner.”
She stared at him.
“That's not the same thing.”
“No.”
She laughed.
He almost smiled.
───
Other nights, neither of them cooked.
They ordered food.
Sat on the floor.
Watched movies.
Talked about work.
Talked about nothing.
Sometimes Ha-eun fell asleep halfway through.
Her head would slowly drop onto his shoulder.
Ha-Kim would lower the volume.
Then stay exactly where he was.
He never moved her.
Not unless she was uncomfortable.
Eventually, he would carry her to bed.
Sometimes she woke halfway.
“I'm awake.”
“You're asleep.”
“No.”
Her eyes were closed.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“Right.”
He carried her anyway.
───
A few weeks passed.
The apartment changed even more.
Not because they bought more things.
Because they were actually living there.
Two pairs of shoes by the door.
Two mugs on the counter.
Two toothbrushes.
Two sets of clothes.
Two people coming home at the end of the day.
Ha-Kim started noticing things he had never noticed before.
He liked hearing Ha-eun moving around in the kitchen.
He liked seeing the lights on when he came home.
He liked that someone would ask if he had eaten.
He liked having someone to tell about the stupid things that happened at work.
He liked that the apartment wasn't silent anymore.
Sometimes he would come home exhausted.
Open the door.
And hear—
“Welcome home.”
Something inside him would loosen.
He never mentioned it.
He didn't need to.
───
One night, Ha-Kim came home later than usual.
The apartment was warm.
The lights were dim.
Ha-eun was asleep on the couch.
A blanket covered her.
The television played quietly.
There was a half-finished cup of tea on the table.
Ha-Kim stood by the door for a moment.
He looked around.
The apartment he had once kept so dark and empty was now full of little things.
Plants.
Pictures.
Warm lights.
Her slippers.
Her books.
Her mug.
Her presence.
He walked over and turned off the television.
Ha-eun stirred.
“Ha-Kim...?”
“Go back to sleep.”
“You're home.”
“Yes.”
She smiled sleepily.
“You're late.”
“I know.”
“Did you eat?”
“No.”
“There’s food.”
“I'll eat.”
She reached for his hand.
He sat beside her.
Her fingers wrapped around his.
She leaned against his shoulder.
Neither spoke.
Ha-Kim looked around the room again.
Then down at her.
He remembered being eighteen.
He remembered how empty everything had felt.
How he had looked at the future without knowing whether he wanted to be part of it.
Back then, an hour had felt like enough.
One hour was all he needed to know.
Now he looked around his apartment.
Their apartment.
And realized he didn't feel like waiting for the next hour anymore.
He just wanted to stay here.
Ha-eun slowly fell asleep against him.
Ha-Kim pulled the blanket higher around her.
A small smile appeared on his face.
He didn't know when his life had become this.
He didn't know exactly when things had changed.
He only knew that they had.
And for once, he wasn't trying to figure out what came next.
He was happy with what was already here.
───
Later that night, they fell asleep together.
Ha-eun stole most of the blanket.
Ha-Kim pulled some back.
She pulled it away.
He sighed.
“Again?”
She didn't answer.
She was already asleep.
He looked at her for a moment.
Then quietly pulled the blanket over both of them.
His eyes closed.
Tomorrow would come.
Work would come.
Problems would come.
Whatever waited ahead would come.
But tonight was simple.
They were together.
They were home.
And that was enough.
