# Chapter 22 — One Hour Ahead
Ha-Kim woke before the alarm.
For a few seconds, he didn't move.
Something warm was pressed against him.
He looked down.
Ha-eun.
She was asleep beside him, one hand loosely holding the front of his shirt.
Her hair was messy.
Half of the blanket had somehow ended up on her side.
Ha-Kim looked at the clock.
6:12 AM.
Then at her.
She had her own apartment.
Six steps away.
Yet somehow she had slept here again.
Ha-Kim carefully moved her hand.
Ha-eun immediately grabbed his shirt again.
He stopped.
"...Seriously?"
She didn't wake.
He slowly freed himself a second time.
This time, she let him go.
Ha-Kim stood.
He pulled the blanket higher over her shoulder before walking toward the bathroom.
───
The light was too bright.
Ha-Kim squinted.
He turned on the water and washed his face.
Cold water ran down his cheeks.
He reached for a towel.
Then looked up.
His reflection stared back.
Messy hair.
Tired face.
Glasses sitting beside the sink.
Without them, the mirror wasn't completely clear.
It hadn't been for a long time.
Ha-Kim leaned closer.
His dark green eyes stared back at him.
He covered one.
Then the other.
The difference was getting worse.
Slowly.
But definitely.
Ha-Kim lowered his hand.
He knew why.
He had always known.
His fingers touched the edge of the sink.
For a moment, he simply stared.
Then he lifted his shirt.
Old scars crossed parts of his skin.
Some faint.
Some easier to see.
Ha-Kim looked at them without expression.
His fingers brushed one.
And suddenly—
he remembered.
───
The first time hadn't felt like a gift.
He was eighteen.
Everything had gone quiet.
Not peaceful.
Just quiet.
The kind of quiet that came after something inside a person had already broken.
Ha-Kim remembered sitting alone.
He remembered closing his eyes.
He remembered thinking he didn't care what happened tomorrow.
Then he opened them.
And the world was wrong.
He was standing somewhere else.
Except he wasn't.
People moved around him.
Someone spoke.
A cup fell from a table.
It shattered.
A man answered his phone.
Someone outside nearly walked into a cyclist.
Ha-Kim couldn't move.
Couldn't speak.
Couldn't touch anything.
He could only watch.
Then—
everything disappeared.
Ha-Kim opened his eyes.
He was back.
Same chair.
Same room.
Same clock.
He stared at it.
Then waited.
At first, nothing happened.
Five minutes.
Ten.
Twenty.
Then the man answered his phone.
The same words.
The same movement.
Ha-Kim stood.
His heart began beating faster.
Later, the cup fell.
It shattered in exactly the same place.
Outside, someone nearly walked into a cyclist.
Exactly as he'd seen.
Ha-Kim checked the time.
One hour.
Exactly.
───
The second time, he did it on purpose.
He didn't know how.
He simply remembered the feeling.
Focused.
And the world disappeared again.
One hour.
Nothing more.
Nothing less.
At first, he thought he was losing his mind.
So he tested it.
Small things.
Which elevator would arrive first.
Whether it would rain.
Who would call.
What someone would say.
Every time—
the future arrived exactly as he'd seen it.
Unless he changed something.
That was when he discovered the second rule.
───
A glass was going to fall.
Ha-Kim had seen it.
So when the moment arrived, he caught it.
Nothing happened.
No shattered glass.
No problem.
For several seconds, Ha-Kim simply stared at the glass in his hand.
He had changed it.
The future wasn't fixed.
That realization should have frightened him.
Instead—
for the first time in a long time, something interested him.
───
Small changes were easy.
Move something before it fell.
Avoid the wrong street.
Leave five minutes earlier.
Say something differently.
He began using it more.
Once every few days.
Then more often.
It felt harmless.
Until the headaches started.
At first, they were small.
Pressure behind his eyes.
Nothing more.
Ha-Kim ignored them.
Then came the exhaustion.
He would sleep and wake up tired.
His hands sometimes felt weak.
His eyes burned after visions.
Still, he continued.
Because knowing was useful.
Knowing meant control.
And control was something Ha-Kim had spent most of his life without.
───
Then came the first time he changed something that actually mattered.
He remembered the future.
Someone getting hurt.
He remembered deciding he could prevent it.
So he did.
The future changed.
And afterward—
Ha-Kim collapsed.
He remembered waking much later.
His head felt like it had been split open.
His vision wouldn't focus.
For several minutes, he could barely see.
That was when he understood.
Seeing wasn't the real cost.
Changing was.
───
Years passed.
Ha-Kim became careful.
He learned the rules.
He learned what his body could tolerate.
Small changes.
Small consequences.
Larger changes.
Larger consequences.
He stopped treating the future like something he could constantly rewrite.
Most of the time.
───
A gray cat stood beside a road.
Ha-Kim remembered watching it.
Remembered closing his eyes.
One hour ahead.
The cat ran into the street.
A vehicle approached.
Then darkness.
He opened his eyes.
Later, when the moment came, he stopped it.
A tiny change.
Something that should have cost almost nothing.
The cat scratched him.
Ha-Kim had stared at it.
"I just saved you."
The cat hissed.
"Ungrateful."
He almost smiled at the memory.
Then he remembered what came afterward.
37.8°C.
Then 38.1°C.
A headache that wouldn't disappear.
Too much for something that small.
And then—
the shadows.
───
Darkness.
Figures.
A smaller shadow.
Familiar.
Ha-eun.
Something enormous behind her.
No clock.
No hour.
No rules.
Then the second vision.
Rain.
Red light.
Metal scraping against concrete.
A voice.
Found you.
───
Ha-Kim blinked.
The bathroom returned.
His hand was gripping the sink.
Too tightly.
He released it.
For several seconds, he stared at himself.
Then he lowered his shirt.
His glasses remained beside the sink.
Ha-Kim picked them up.
Before putting them on, he looked at his reflection one more time.
Blurry.
More than before.
He knew what that meant.
The ability had always taken something.
He had simply gotten good at pretending the price was small.
Ha-Kim put on his glasses.
The world sharpened.
Not completely.
But enough.
For now.
───
The bathroom door opened.
Ha-eun stood outside.
Her eyes were barely open.
Her hair was a disaster.
She stared at him.
Ha-Kim stared back.
"Why are you awake?"
She frowned.
"You left."
"I went to the bathroom."
"I noticed."
She walked toward him.
Then rested her forehead against his chest.
Ha-Kim looked down.
"Are you awake?"
"No."
"You're standing."
"Unfortunately."
He almost smiled.
Ha-eun wrapped her arms loosely around him.
"Come back."
"I have work."
"So do I."
"Then wake up."
"No."
Ha-eun caught his hand and started pulling him toward the bedroom.
"Five minutes."
"Ten."
"Five."
"Eight."
"Five."
She stopped at the bedroom door.
"You're difficult."
"So I've heard."
Ha-eun turned toward him.
Neither moved.
Her sleepiness had faded slightly.
Ha-Kim noticed her eyes move down.
His shirt hadn't completely fallen back into place after he'd looked at the scars.
A small part of one was still visible.
Ha-eun reached toward him.
Then stopped.
"Can I?"
Ha-Kim looked at her hand.
Then nodded.
Her fingers touched his skin gently.
Ha-Kim flinched.
Ha-eun immediately looked up.
"That hurt?"
"No."
"Then why did you move?"
"Your hands are cold."
She stared at him.
Then a tiny smile appeared.
"Oh."
"Don't."
"You're sensitive."
"I'm not."
She touched him again.
He flinched again.
Ha-eun's smile grew.
"Ha-Kim."
"Your hands are still cold."
"Sure."
He caught her wrist.
Ha-eun looked at his hand around hers.
Then at him.
They were standing much closer now.
She reached up with her other hand and kissed him.
Ha-Kim froze for only a second before kissing her back.
It was supposed to be brief.
It wasn't.
Ha-eun's hand rested against his chest.
His grip around her wrist loosened.
She moved closer.
Another kiss.
Then another.
Ha-Kim pulled away slightly.
"We're going to be late."
"I know."
"You were the one complaining about being late last week."
"I know."
"And now you're—"
Ha-eun kissed him again.
That ended the argument.
Ha-Kim's phone vibrated somewhere behind them.
Neither checked it.
For once, Ha-Kim didn't care.
Ha-eun pulled back.
Her face was red, but she didn't look away.
Instead, her fingers caught the bottom of his shirt.
She hesitated.
Ha-Kim understood.
He nodded.
She lifted it carefully.
His body tensed automatically.
The scars were easier to see now.
Ha-eun didn't ask where they came from.
Not yet.
She simply looked at him.
At the old marks.
At the body he'd spent years hiding beneath dark clothes.
Her fingers brushed lightly over his side.
Ha-Kim looked away.
Ha-eun noticed.
"What?"
"Nothing."
"You're doing that thing."
"What thing?"
"Where you pretend you don't care about something."
Ha-Kim was quiet.
Ha-eun looked at him again.
Then smiled.
"You look good."
His eyes returned to hers.
"What?"
"You heard me."
"Ha-eun."
"You do."
His ears slowly turned red.
Ha-eun stared.
Then her eyes widened.
"You're embarrassed."
"No."
"You are."
"I'm not."
She smiled.
"You're cute."
"Stop."
"No."
Ha-Kim reached for his shirt.
Ha-eun caught it first.
"No."
He stared at her.
She stepped closer.
This time, when she touched his skin, he didn't flinch.
Her expression softened.
"You don't have to hide from me."
The teasing disappeared from his face.
For a moment, neither spoke.
Ha-Kim had spent years hiding things.
His eyes.
His scars.
His ability.
His name behind a screen.
Parts of himself nobody was allowed to touch.
But Ha-eun stood in front of him without asking him to explain any of it.
She simply stayed.
Ha-Kim touched her cheek.
She leaned into his hand.
Then kissed his palm.
Something inside him gave way.
He kissed her.
Ha-eun wrapped her arms around him, and this time there was no joke waiting afterward.
No awkward excuse.
No discussion about work.
Just warmth.
Trust.
And the quiet understanding that neither of them wanted to move away.
Ha-eun gently pushed him backward.
Ha-Kim looked behind himself.
The bed.
Then back at her.
She was bright red now.
Still, she didn't retreat.
"Are you sure?"
She nodded.
"Yeah."
Ha-Kim searched her face.
Ha-eun smiled nervously.
Then took his hand.
"I'm sure."
He let her pull him closer.
Outside, Seoul was already waking.
Cars moved through the streets.
Phones vibrated.
Work waited.
The future waited.
For once, Ha-Kim let all of it go.
The room became quiet.
And everything else faded away.
───
Later, sunlight had moved farther across the bedroom floor.
Ha-eun lay close beside him.
Neither had said anything for a while.
Her fingers slowly traced his hand.
Ha-Kim stared at the ceiling.
The strange thing was—
his mind was quiet.
No calculations.
No visions.
No thoughts about what would happen one hour from now.
Ha-eun moved closer.
"Ha-Kim?"
"Yeah?"
"We're definitely late."
He looked at the clock.
Then closed his eyes again.
"I know."
Ha-eun buried her face against him.
A quiet laugh escaped her.
Ha-Kim's arm settled around her.
Work could wait another minute.
The future could too.
For once, he had exactly what he'd spent years needing without knowing how to ask for it.
Someone close enough that he didn't have to see tomorrow to feel safe.
