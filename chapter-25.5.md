Chapter 25.5 — What We Were
Some memories didn't hurt because they were bad.
They hurt because they had once been good.
For Ha-Kim, Min-seo was one of those memories.
Before the arguments.
Before the distance.
Before they learned how badly two people could love each other and still fail to understand one another.
There had been a time when seeing her meant only one thing.
Home.
───
Ha-Kim was five when he first met Min-seo.
Their parents had been friends for years, so family visits weren't unusual.
That afternoon, Ha-Kim sat on the living-room floor while the adults talked around the dining table.
He had been given a small toy car.
He wasn't interested in it.
He was watching the girl sitting across the room.
She was a little younger than him, sitting beside her mother while holding a juice box with both hands.
Their eyes met.
She looked away.
Then looked back.
Ha-Kim tilted his head.
She tilted hers too.
Neither said anything.
Eventually, Min-seo pointed at the toy car.
"Is that yours?"
Ha-Kim looked down.
"Yeah."
"Can I see it?"
He pushed it toward her.
She took it.
Then immediately drove it across the floor.
Ha-Kim stared.
"You didn't ask if you could play with it."
She stopped.
"Oh."
She thought for a second.
"Can I play with it?"
He nodded.
"Okay."
That was it.
No dramatic moment.
No sudden realization.
Just two children sitting on the floor while their parents talked nearby.
By the end of the afternoon, they were playing together.
───
The families kept visiting each other.
Sometimes Ha-Kim's family went to Min-seo's house.
Sometimes Min-seo came to his.
The adults would talk for hours.
The children would disappear into another room.
They played games.
Fought over snacks.
Made up five minutes later.
Once, Min-seo stole the blanket Ha-Kim was using.
He stared at her.
"Give it back."
"No."
"Why?"
"It's mine now."
"You didn't bring it."
She smiled.
"Still mine."
Ha-Kim tried pulling it back.
She held on.
They ended up falling onto the floor, both laughing while their parents shouted from the other room asking what happened.
"Nothing!" Min-seo yelled.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"You started it."
She whispered.
"Shh."
He laughed.
Those visits became something Ha-Kim looked forward to.
His life outside those afternoons wasn't always easy.
Home could be loud.
Arguments happened.
Some days he learned to stay quiet before anyone told him to.
But whenever Min-seo arrived with her parents, something inside him loosened.
He smiled more.
He played.
He forgot to watch the adults.
He forgot to listen for the next argument.
He just became a child.
And Min-seo never had to know why.
She only knew that Ha-Kim was more fun when they were together.
───
Years passed.
They grew up together without realizing how much those small meetings were shaping them.
At seven, they played video games.
At nine, they started arguing about who was better at them.
At eleven, they spent entire afternoons outside while their parents talked.
At twelve, Min-seo started changing.
She became more confident.
She cared more about school.
Made more friends.
Started having her own plans.
Ha-Kim noticed.
He didn't say anything.
He was used to people changing.
Still, whenever she came around, he waited for her.
Sometimes she would sit beside him without saying anything.
Sometimes she would steal his food.
Sometimes they would talk until their parents told them it was time to go home.
Those moments were enough.
Or at least they used to be.
───
Ha-Kim was fourteen when he realized what he felt for her wasn't the same anymore.
They were sitting outside after one of their family visits.
The evening air was cool.
Min-seo was looking at her phone.
Ha-Kim kept glancing at her.
She noticed.
"What?"
"Nothing."
"You keep looking at me."
"I wasn't."
"You were."
He looked away.
She smiled.
"You’re weird."
He didn't answer.
His heart was beating too fast.
That night, after they returned home, he couldn't sleep.
He kept thinking about her.
The next day, he sent her a message.
Can I ask you something?
She replied almost immediately.
What?
He stared at the screen for several minutes.
Then typed:
Do you like me?
Three dots appeared.
Disappeared.
Appeared again.
Then:
Like how?
He swallowed.
You know how.
The reply took longer this time.
Maybe.
Ha-Kim smiled.
Maybe?
Maybe I like you.
He looked at the message for a long time.
Then typed:
I like you too.
She replied:
I know.
He blinked.
How?
You're obvious.
He dropped his phone onto the bed.
His face felt hot.
For the first time in a long time, he smiled without forcing it.
───
They started dating quietly.
Min-seo was twelve and a half.
Ha-Kim was fourteen.
Neither of them really knew what they were doing.
They texted constantly.
They played games together.
They met whenever they could.
Sometimes they sat in a park for hours and talked about nothing.
Sometimes they didn't talk at all.
One afternoon, they found a stray cat behind a convenience store.
It was small and gray.
Min-seo crouched immediately.
"Look."
Ha-Kim looked.
The cat stared back.
"It's skinny."
"I know."
She opened the snack she had bought.
Ha-Kim stopped her.
"Don't give it that."
"Why?"
"It's not good for cats."
She looked at him.
"Then what should we give it?"
He thought for a second.
"I'll find something."
She smiled.
"You care too much."
"You were going to feed it chips."
"I was trying to help."
"Badly."
She lightly shoved his shoulder.
He smiled.
They started coming back with proper food.
The cat eventually recognized them.
Whenever they approached, it would walk toward them.
Min-seo would crouch down.
Ha-Kim would sit beside her.
Neither of them ever gave the cat a name.
They just called it theirs.
For a while, life felt simple.
───
Then Ha-Kim moved to another city.
It wasn't because of Min-seo.
It was because his life was changing.
The distance was manageable at first.
They texted every day.
Called whenever they could.
But eventually, reality started getting in the way.
Min-seo's parents were strict.
They didn't know about the relationship.
She had to hide her phone sometimes.
School became harder.
Her friends became more important.
Her life kept expanding.
Ha-Kim's life became smaller.
He waited for her messages.
She answered when she could.
He wanted to talk.
She was tired.
He wanted reassurance.
She wanted space.
Neither was completely wrong.
Neither was completely right.
One night, Ha-Kim stared at his phone.
You didn't answer me all day.
She replied later.
I was busy.
You could've told me.
I couldn't.
He stared at the screen.
Do you still want this?
There was a long pause.
Then:
Why do you keep asking me that?
He didn't know how to explain it.
Because he was afraid.
Because every unanswered message felt bigger than it should.
Because he needed to hear that she still wanted him.
Because somewhere inside him, he was terrified that people always left.
He typed:
Because I don't know if you do.
She replied:
I'm here, aren't I?
It should have been enough.
For her, it probably was.
For him, it wasn't.
That difference slowly became the center of everything.
───
Their arguments became more frequent.
Ha-Kim would ask for more time.
Min-seo would feel pressured.
Min-seo would pull back.
Ha-Kim would become more anxious.
The more he reached for her, the more she felt trapped.
The more she pulled away, the more abandoned he felt.
Neither knew how to stop it.
Eventually, Min-seo said it.
"I think we should break up."
Ha-Kim stared at the message.
He read it again.
Then again.
He didn't answer for several minutes.
Finally:
Okay.
He put the phone down.
He didn't cry immediately.
He just sat there.
The room was quiet.
Too quiet.
───
A year passed.
Ha-Kim didn't see her.
Then his family moved back to their old town.
And one afternoon, their parents visited each other again.
Ha-Kim knew Min-seo would be there.
He almost stayed in his room.
Instead, he walked downstairs.
She was standing near the window.
Older.
Different.
Still her.
She turned.
Their eyes met.
Neither spoke.
"Hi," she said.
"Hi."
That was all.
The adults continued talking.
Neither of them listened.
Later, they went outside.
They sat several feet apart.
Min-seo looked down at her hands.
"I didn't know you'd come."
"My parents came."
"I know."
Silence.
Then she said quietly:
"I'm sorry."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"For what?"
"For how things ended."
He looked away.
"I wasn't easy either."
She shook her head.
"You were trying."
"So were you."
She looked at him.
"I missed you."
His chest tightened.
"I missed you too."
Neither of them planned it.
Neither of them said they should try again.
It simply happened slowly.
They started talking.
Then texting.
Then meeting.
Eventually, they were together again.
This time, Ha-Kim tried to do things differently.
He listened more.
He stopped demanding answers immediately.
When Min-seo wanted time with her friends, he tried not to take it personally.
When she needed space, he gave it.
When she couldn't express something, he tried to understand instead of forcing it out of her.
For a while, it worked.
They were happy again.
They laughed.
They went out.
They played games.
They found the gray cat again one afternoon.
It had grown.
Min-seo crouched beside it.
"It remembers us."
Ha-Kim sat beside her.
"Maybe it remembers the food."
She smiled.
"You're ruining the moment."
"I'm being realistic."
"You always do that."
She leaned against his shoulder.
He smiled.
For a little while, it felt like they had gone back in time.
───
But the old wound hadn't disappeared.
Ha-Kim still wanted affection.
Reassurance.
Closeness.
He wanted to feel chosen.
Min-seo loved him.
She really did.
But love wasn't something she naturally expressed the way he needed.
She had friends.
School.
Her own interests.
Her own world.
Ha-Kim tried to become comfortable with that world.
But groups made him nervous.
When they went somewhere with her friends, he often stayed quiet.
He would sit beside her and listen.
Sometimes he would smile.
Sometimes he would barely speak.
Min-seo noticed.
"You don't have to come every time."
He looked at her.
"I know."
"You don't like being around them."
"It's not that."
"It is."
He looked down.
"I just don't know what to say."
She sighed.
"You don't have to say anything."
But he heard something else.
You don't fit here.
He knew she hadn't said that.
Still, the thought stayed.
───
Slowly, the distance returned.
Not all at once.
There was no single moment where everything broke.
It was smaller than that.
A missed call.
A canceled plan.
A short reply.
Another evening with friends.
Another time Ha-Kim swallowed what he wanted to say.
He told himself it was fine.
He told himself she was busy.
He told himself he was asking for too much.
He apologized more often.
Sometimes even when he wasn't sure what he had done.
Min-seo noticed.
"Why are you apologizing?"
"I don't know."
"Then stop."
He smiled faintly.
"Okay."
But he didn't know how.
───
Then came the final argument.
It started over something small.
It always did.
Min-seo had canceled their plans again.
Ha-Kim said it was fine.
She knew it wasn't.
"Just say what you're thinking."
"There's nothing."
"Ha-Kim."
He looked at her.
For years, he had swallowed everything.
This time, he couldn't.
"I feel like I'm always waiting for you."
She went quiet.
He continued.
"I don't need you every minute."
His voice shook.
"I don't need you to stop seeing your friends."
"I know."
"I don't need you to give up your life for me."
He looked down.
"I just want to know that you want me in it."
Min-seo's eyes changed.
He had never said it like that before.
Ha-Kim wiped his face.
"I kept thinking if I asked for less, you'd stay."
Her eyes filled with tears.
"Why didn't you tell me?"
"Because every time I tried, it felt like I was making things harder for you."
"You should have told me."
"I was scared."
"Of me?"
"Of losing you."
She cried.
Ha-Kim looked away because seeing her cry made it harder to continue.
"I loved you," he whispered.
"I know."
"No."
He shook his head.
"You don't."
She covered her mouth.
"I loved you so much that I kept trying to become someone who didn't need anything."
His voice broke.
"And I couldn't."
Min-seo cried harder.
"I loved you too."
"I know."
"I really did."
"I know."
They stood there, both crying.
For once, there was no misunderstanding.
She understood.
He understood.
That was what made it hurt even more.
Because understanding couldn't undo everything that had happened.
They stayed together for a little longer after that.
But something had already changed.
Neither of them could pretend otherwise.
───
Ha-Kim's eighteenth birthday came quietly.
There was no celebration.
No excitement.
He sat alone for a while, looking at his phone.
There were messages.
People wishing him happy birthday.
He didn't answer.
He thought about the years before.
His parents.
The arguments.
The lonely nights.
Min-seo.
The good memories.
The bad ones.
Everything.
He wasn't angry anymore.
He wasn't even sure he was sad.
He was simply tired.
Tired of hoping tomorrow would finally feel different.
That night, Ha-Kim disappeared.
He left everything behind.
The relationship.
The town.
The people who knew him.
The version of himself that had spent years waiting for someone else to make life feel worth living.
He walked until the streets became unfamiliar.
Until there was nobody around.
Until the world became quiet.
And somewhere in that silence, something inside him finally broke.
Or perhaps—
something woke up.
───
He would survive that night.
He would wake in a hospital.
He would eventually leave everything behind.
He would build a life no one from his past knew about.
He would become Ha-Kim.
The quiet man behind the glasses.
The mysterious CEO.
The man who could see one hour into the future.
But even years later, when he thought of Min-seo, he never remembered only the ending.
He remembered a little girl stealing his toy car.
A gray cat behind a convenience store.
Late-night messages.
Her head against his shoulder.
The way she used to say his name.
The afternoons when their parents talked while the two of them disappeared into another room.
He remembered that they had been real.
That they had loved each other.
That they had also hurt each other.
And that neither of them had been a monster.
They had simply been two people who wanted different things from the same love.
For a long time, Ha-Kim thought that meant their relationship had been a mistake.
Now, he knew better.
Some things could be real and still end.
Some people could matter and still become part of the past.
And sometimes letting go didn't mean pretending you never loved them.
It meant accepting that the life you once wanted with them was no longer the life you wanted now.
