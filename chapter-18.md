# Chapter 18 — What Happened to Me

The next morning felt strange.
Ha-eun opened her apartment door.
At almost the exact same moment, the door across the hall opened too.
She froze.
Ha-Kim froze.
Neither moved.
Yesterday, this would've been normal.
Today...
Ha-eun looked at him.
Her boyfriend.
Her face immediately became warm.
Ha-Kim adjusted his glasses.
“Morning.”
“Morning.”
Silence.
Ha-eun looked at the elevator.
Ha-Kim looked at the elevator.
Then they started walking toward it together.
Their hands brushed.
Ha-eun immediately pulled hers away.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“I didn't do anything.”
“I know.”
“Then why did you move?”
“I don't know.”
The elevator doors opened.
They stepped inside.
Ha-eun stared straight ahead.
Last night flashed through her mind.
His hand against her cheek.
His face close to hers.
His lips—
She squeezed her eyes shut.
Ha-Kim looked over.
“Are you okay?”
“Yes.”
“You look uncomfortable.”
“I'm perfectly comfortable.”
“You're standing in the corner.”
She looked down.
She was.
“It's a nice corner.”
Ha-Kim stared at her.
“Don't.”
“I didn't say anything.”
“I know what you're thinking.”
“I doubt that.”
The elevator doors opened.
Ha-eun walked out first.
Behind her, she heard the tiny laugh he tried to hide.
───
For most of the day, Ha-eun was happy.
Embarrassingly happy.
Every time she saw Ha-Kim, something inside her reacted differently.
Nothing about him had changed.
Same expression.
Same glasses.
Same quiet voice.
Same habit of looking at his screen like it had personally offended him.
But now...
He was her boyfriend.
Even thinking the word made her smile.
“Why are you smiling?”
Ha-eun immediately looked at Ji-woo.
“I'm not.”
“You are.”
“No.”
Ji-woo narrowed her eyes.
Ha-eun returned to her computer.
“Work.”
“I am working.”
“Then work harder.”
Ji-woo stared at her.
“You're suspicious.”
Ha-eun ignored her.
But the smile returned.
───
It should've been one of the happiest days she'd had in a long time.
And it was.
Until her phone vibrated.
Ha-eun glanced down.
Unknown number.
Her smile disappeared.
She didn't open the message immediately.
Her fingers stopped above the screen.
Another vibration.
Her chest tightened.
She turned the phone facedown.
“Ha-eun?”
She looked up.
Ji-woo was watching her.
“What?”
“You okay?”
“Yeah.”
The answer came too quickly.
Ji-woo hesitated.
Ha-eun smiled.
“Just spam.”
Ji-woo eventually turned back toward her computer.
Ha-eun didn't touch her phone again.
But she already knew.
It wasn't spam.
───
That evening, Ha-eun stood outside Ha-Kim's apartment.
Usually she knocked without thinking anymore.
Tonight, her hand stayed in the air.
She could go home.
Pretend she hadn't received anything.
Pretend the past was still far away.
Pretend she was just Ha-eun from Haetae.
The girl who complained about vegetables.
The girl who ate Ha-Kim's food.
The girl who had somehow become his girlfriend.
Not the other Ha-eun.
Not the child she had spent years trying to leave behind.
Her hand lowered.
Then the door opened.
Ha-eun jumped.
Ha-Kim stood there.
She stared at him.
“How did you know I was here?”
“I heard you.”
“I didn't knock.”
“You walk loudly too.”
Her mouth fell open.
“I do not.”
“You do.”
She almost argued.
But Ha-Kim's expression changed.
He looked at her properly.
“What happened?”
There it was.
Ha-eun looked away.
“Nothing.”
“You're doing it again.”
“What?”
“Lying badly.”
Normally she would've complained.
Tonight she didn't.
Ha-Kim stepped aside.
“Come in.”
Ha-eun hesitated.
Then entered.
───
She sat on his couch.
Ha-Kim brought her water.
She held the glass between both hands without drinking.
He sat nearby.
Not too close.
Not too far.
For a while, he didn't ask anything.
Ha-eun appreciated that.
She watched the water move slightly inside the glass.
Eventually, she spoke.
“Do you remember when you asked why I needed the job so badly?”
“Yes.”
“I didn't tell you everything.”
“I know.”
Ha-eun looked at him.
“You knew?”
“I knew there was more.”
“Why didn't you ask?”
“You didn't want to tell me.”
That simple answer made her chest hurt.
Ha-eun looked back at the glass.
“What if I tell you something bad?”
Ha-Kim stayed quiet.
“Something really bad.”
“Then tell me.”
“What if you think differently about me?”
“I'll decide that after you tell me.”
Ha-eun frowned.
“That isn't reassuring.”
“Would you rather I lie?”
She looked at him.
Then a tiny smile appeared.
“No.”
Silence returned.
Ha-eun took a breath.
Then another.
“My parents died when I was five.”
Ha-Kim didn't move.
She kept her eyes on the glass.
“I don't remember everything about them anymore.”
Her voice became quieter.
“Sometimes I remember little things. A voice. Someone holding my hand. A smell.”
She swallowed.
“But their faces are getting harder to remember.”
Ha-Kim listened.
Ha-eun's fingers tightened around the glass.
“There wasn't anyone else.”
“No relatives?”
She shook her head.
“Not anyone who came for me.”
Her throat tightened.
“So I ended up outside.”
Ha-Kim's expression changed slightly.
Ha-eun noticed.
She immediately looked down.
“I know.”
“Know what?”
“How it sounds.”
“I didn't say anything.”
“You looked at me.”
“I'm listening.”
She went quiet.
Then continued.
“I was little.”
Her voice shook slightly.
“I didn't understand anything. I just knew I was hungry all the time.”
Images she had buried for years began returning.
Cold streets.
Empty stomach.
Dirty hands.
Rain.
Bread.
Running.
“Sometimes I stole food.”
Ha-eun waited.
Ha-Kim said nothing.
“I stole money too.”
Still nothing.
“Whatever I needed.”
Her fingers trembled.
“I wasn't a good kid.”
Ha-Kim finally spoke.
“You were five.”
Ha-eun looked at him.
“You were trying to survive.”
Her lips pressed together.
She looked away again.
“Later, I met people.”
Ha-Kim's eyes sharpened slightly.
“What people?”
Ha-eun hesitated.
“People I shouldn't have met.”
She didn't say gang.
Not yet.
The word itself felt too heavy.
“At first, I thought they were helping me.”
She gave a small, empty laugh.
“They gave me food.”
Her thumb rubbed against the side of the glass.
“They gave me somewhere to sleep sometimes.”
Ha-Kim stayed completely still.
“And then they started asking me to do things.”
“What things?”
“Carry things.”
She swallowed.
“Take things.”
Another pause.
“Go places.”
Her breathing became uneven.
“If I said no…”
She stopped.
Ha-Kim didn't ask her to finish.
That somehow made it easier.
“They made sure I understood that saying no wasn't really an option.”
Ha-Kim's jaw tightened.
Ha-eun saw it.
She immediately became nervous.
“You're angry.”
“Yes.”
Her stomach dropped.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“Not at you.”
She froze.
His voice stayed calm.
“At them.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
Something inside her loosened slightly.
She looked back down.
“I stayed around them for years.”
She paused.
“Too long.”
“You were a child.”
“I still did things.”
“You were a child.”
“You don't even know what I did.”
“I know enough.”
Ha-eun shook her head.
“No, you don't.”
Her voice rose slightly.
“I lied. I stole. I helped people who hurt other people. Sometimes I knew something bad would happen and I still did what they told me because I was scared.”
Her eyes burned.
“I could've said no.”
“And then what?”
She stopped.
Ha-Kim's voice remained quiet.
“What would've happened if you said no?”
Ha-eun couldn't answer.
Because she knew.
Ha-Kim did too.
She looked down.
“I don't know.”
“Yes, you do.”
Her eyes filled.
Ha-eun blinked quickly.
“I hate talking about this.”
“Then we can stop.”
She looked at him.
There was no disappointment in his face.
No disgust.
No fear.
Just Ha-Kim.
Waiting.
Ha-eun shook her head.
“No.”
She wiped beneath her eye.
“I want you to know.”
That made him quiet.
Ha-eun took another breath.
“There was someone.”
Ha-Kim waited.
“A girl.”
Her voice almost disappeared.
“Her name was Sa-ram.”
The name felt strange after so many years.
Ha-eun rarely said it aloud.
“She helped me.”
A tear slipped down before she could stop it.
“She shouldn't have.”
Ha-Kim didn't interrupt.
“She saw me being chased once.”
Rain.
Bread clutched against her chest.
Dogs barking.
A gunshot.
Ha-eun's breathing stopped for a second.
“She protected me.”
Her hands started trembling.
“And she died because of it.”
Ha-Kim's eyes softened.
Ha-eun wiped her face again.
“I ran.”
Her voice cracked.
“I just ran.”
The room became blurry.
“I didn't help her.”
“You were a child.”
“I left her there.”
“You were a child, Ha-eun.”
“I didn't even look back.”
She covered her mouth.
For years, that moment had followed her.
The sound.
Her name.
The gunshot.
Running until her legs hurt.
Ha-Kim moved closer.
Slowly.
Giving her time to move away.
She didn't.
He took the glass from her shaking hands and placed it on the table.
Ha-eun stared at her empty hands.
“I still dream about it sometimes.”
Ha-Kim sat beside her.
“I still hear it.”
She pressed her hands against her face.
“And sometimes I think…”
Her voice broke.
“What if I'm still that person?”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“What person?”
“The girl who runs.”
Silence.
“The girl who steals.”
She wiped her face.
“The girl who does whatever she has to because she's scared.”
Ha-Kim's voice was quiet.
“You survived.”
Ha-eun shook her head.
“That doesn't make everything okay.”
“No.”
She looked at him.
“It doesn't.”
His answer surprised her.
Ha-Kim continued.
“But surviving something doesn't mean you have to spend the rest of your life punishing yourself for surviving it.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
Her eyes filled again.
“I got away.”
She looked down.
“I thought that was the end.”
Ha-Kim's expression became more serious.
“It wasn't?”
Ha-eun hesitated.
This was the part she feared most.
“They found me again.”
Ha-Kim became completely still.
Ha-eun looked at her phone on the table.
“That was why I lost my apartment.”
He followed her gaze.
“They wanted money?”
She nodded.
“They knew I had a job.”
“How?”
“I don't know.”
Her breathing became quicker.
“They started calling again. Messaging me. Asking for money.”
“How much?”
“I don't know anymore.”
“Ha-eun.”
“I kept sending it.”
She looked at him.
“I thought if I gave them enough, they'd leave me alone.”
Ha-Kim's expression hardened.
“They didn't.”
“No.”
“So your rent…”
“I couldn't pay it.”
Ha-Kim finally understood.
The cheap lunches.
The worn shoes.
The empty apartment.
The bags.
The lie about the landlord selling it.
Everything.
Ha-eun looked ashamed.
“I didn't want anyone to know.”
“Why?”
“Because…”
She struggled to say it.
“Because I finally had something normal.”
Her voice became smaller.
“A job.”
She looked around his apartment.
“A home.”
Then at him.
“People.”
Her eyes lowered.
“You.”
Ha-Kim didn't speak.
“I was scared that if anyone knew where I came from…”
She swallowed.
“They'd look at me and only see that.”
Her eyes met his.
“And now I'm scared they're going to take everything back.”
“Who?”
“The people from before.”
Her hands tightened.
“I don't want to go back.”
“You won't.”
“You don't know that.”
“No.”
Ha-Kim's voice remained calm.
“I don't.”
Ha-eun looked at him.
“But you don't have to face them alone.”
Something in her expression broke.
She quickly looked away.
“You shouldn't say things like that.”
“Why?”
“Because I'll believe you.”
“You should.”
Her lips trembled.
“Ha-Kim…”
He turned toward her.
“Look at me.”
She did.
“You were a child.”
Ha-eun stayed silent.
“You were hungry. You were alone. Adults used that.”
His expression remained controlled, but she could see the anger underneath it.
“What happened to you wasn't something you chose.”
“But the things I did—”
“Are not the only things you've done.”
She stopped.
“You work.”
Ha-eun blinked.
“You learn.”
He continued.
“You help Ji-woo even when she creates problems for herself.”
A tiny laugh escaped through Ha-eun's tears.
“She really does.”
“You worry about everyone.”
Ha-Kim looked at her.
“You brought me soup when you barely knew how to cook.”
“That soup was good.”
“It was acceptable.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
“Seriously?”
A tiny smile appeared on his face.
She wiped her cheek.
Then his expression softened again.
“You're not that little girl anymore.”
Ha-eun looked down.
“But she's still me.”
“Yes.”
Ha-Kim didn't deny it.
“She is.”
Ha-eun slowly looked back at him.
“And she deserves kindness too.”
Her breath caught.
Ha-Kim continued.
“Your past explains what happened to you.”
His voice was quiet.
“It doesn't decide what you deserve now.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
For years, she had imagined this conversation.
Not specifically with Ha-Kim.
With anyone.
In every version, something changed afterward.
They became uncomfortable.
They asked questions she couldn't answer.
They looked at her differently.
They left.
Ha-Kim didn't.
He was still sitting beside her.
Still looking at her exactly the same way.
Maybe even softer.
“Do you regret saying yes yesterday?”
Ha-Kim frowned.
“No.”
“You didn't even think about it.”
“I don't need to.”
“But now you know.”
“Yes.”
“And?”
“And you're still my girlfriend.”
Ha-eun's face crumpled.
She tried to stop it.
Failed.
Ha-Kim opened his arms slightly.
He didn't pull her toward him.
He waited.
Ha-eun stared at him for half a second.
Then moved.
She buried her face against his chest.
Ha-Kim wrapped his arms around her.
Ha-eun held onto his shirt.
For once, she didn't apologize for crying.
She didn't try to hide it.
She simply stayed there.
And Ha-Kim let her.
His hand moved slowly over her hair.
No questions.
No judgment.
No promises that everything would magically become easy.
He was just there.
After a while, Ha-eun's breathing calmed.
Her voice came muffled against his chest.
“I'm getting your shirt wet.”
“I have others.”
She laughed quietly.
“You're terrible at comforting people.”
“You stopped crying.”
“That's not because you're good at it.”
“Still worked.”
She lifted her head.
Her eyes were red.
Ha-Kim gently wiped beneath one of them with his thumb.
Ha-eun went quiet.
“Thank you.”
“For what?”
“For not looking at me differently.”
Ha-Kim looked at her for a moment.
“I do.”
Her expression fell slightly.
Then he continued.
“I understand you better now.”
Ha-eun stared at him.
His hand remained against her cheek.
“That's different.”
Her eyes filled again.
“Don't make me cry again.”
“I wasn't trying to.”
“You're bad at this.”
“I know.”
Ha-eun smiled.
A real one.
Small.
Tired.
But real.
Neither of them moved.
Ha-Kim's hand was still against her cheek.
Ha-eun looked at him.
Then at his lips.
She remembered last night.
How nervous she'd been.
How she had hidden against his shoulder after their first kiss.
This time felt different.
There was still embarrassment.
But beneath it was something stronger.
She wanted to be close to him.
Not because she was afraid he'd leave.
Not because she needed to prove anything.
She simply wanted him.
Ha-eun moved first.
She leaned forward and kissed him.
Ha-Kim froze for only a second before kissing her back.
His hand slipped gently behind her neck.
Ha-eun held the front of his shirt.
The kiss lasted longer than the ones from the night before.
There was no nervous question beforehand.
No immediate retreat.
Just warmth.
When Ha-Kim slowly pulled away, Ha-eun followed him without thinking.
Their lips met again.
Ha-Kim paused.
Ha-eun opened her eyes.
“What?”
“I thought we were done.”
She stared at him.
“Who decided that?”
Ha-Kim blinked.
“I assumed.”
“Don't assume.”
For once, Ha-Kim had absolutely nothing to say.
Ha-eun realized what she'd just said.
Her confidence disappeared immediately.
She lowered her face.
“Actually, forget I said that.”
“No.”
Her eyes snapped back up.
The smallest smile appeared on his face.
“You said not to assume.”
Ha-eun's face burned.
“I hate you.”
“No, you don't.”
That shut her up.
Ha-Kim leaned toward her again.
This time Ha-eun met him halfway.
The kiss was slow.
Comfortable.
She didn't feel the need to hide afterward.
When they separated, she stayed close enough that their foreheads touched.
Ha-Kim brushed a loose strand of hair away from her face.
Ha-eun closed her eyes.
For the first time that evening, her chest didn't feel heavy.
She leaned into him.
Ha-Kim shifted back against the couch, and Ha-eun rested against his chest.
His arm settled around her.
Neither spoke.
They didn't need to.
Minutes passed.
Ha-eun listened to his heartbeat.
Her fingers rested loosely against his shirt.
“Ha-Kim.”
“Hm?”
“You're warm.”
“So are you.”
“I know.”
A few more minutes passed.
Her eyes became heavier.
Ha-Kim noticed.
“You're falling asleep.”
“No.”
“You are.”
“I'm resting my eyes.”
“You said that ten minutes ago.”
Ha-eun opened one eye.
“You've been counting?”
“No.”
“Liar.”
She closed it again.
Ha-Kim looked down at her.
“Your apartment is across the hall.”
“I know.”
“You should sleep there.”
Ha-eun tightened her arm around him.
“No.”
“Ha-eun.”
“I'm comfortable.”
“You complained about this couch before.”
“I changed my mind.”
“You do that often.”
She smiled against his shirt.
“Be quiet.”
Ha-Kim didn't argue again.
His hand moved slowly through her hair.
Ha-eun's breathing gradually became softer.
Then slower.
Until she stopped answering entirely.
Ha-Kim looked down.
She was asleep.
Her face was still slightly red from crying.
But she looked peaceful.
He didn't wake her.
Outside, Seoul continued glowing beyond the window.
Nothing about Ha-eun's past had changed.
Sa-ram was still gone.
The years she spent afraid still existed.
The people she escaped were still somewhere out there.
But tonight, someone knew.
Not everything.
But enough.
Enough to understand why she was afraid.
Enough to understand why she ran.
Enough to understand why an ordinary home, ordinary food, and ordinary happiness meant so much to her.
And he was still there.
Ha-eun shifted slightly in her sleep and moved closer to him.
Ha-Kim looked down at her.
His expression softened.
For the first time in years, Ha-eun had told someone what happened to her.
And afterward...
She had felt safe enough to fall asleep.
