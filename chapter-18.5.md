# Chapter 18.5 — The Girl at the Café

A few weeks before Seo-jun realized he had a problem, there was a café near Haetae Cyber that he visited almost every day.

It wasn't anything special.

A small place on the corner of a busy street.

A few wooden tables. Warm lights. A display case filled with pastries. Quiet music that nobody really paid attention to.

Seo-jun liked it because it was close to his studio.

And because the coffee was decent.

That was all.

At least, that's what he told himself.

───

The first time Eun-bi really noticed him, he walked in while talking on the phone.

"Yeah, send me the file."

He held his phone between his ear and shoulder while opening the door.

"No, not that one. The other—"

He stopped when he reached the counter.

Eun-bi looked up.

"Welcome."

Seo-jun quickly finished his call.

"Sorry. One second."

He put his phone away.

"Americano, iced."

"Medium?"

"Yeah."

Eun-bi nodded and prepared the drink.

Seo-jun glanced around.

"You work here often?"

She looked slightly confused.

"I... work here every day."

He laughed.

"Right. That was a stupid question."

A small smile appeared on her face.

"A little."

Seo-jun raised an eyebrow.

"Oh. You're honest."

"Sorry."

"No, no. I like it."

She handed him his drink.

"Have a good day."

"You too."

He walked away.

Neither of them thought much about it.

At least, not yet.

───

A few days later, Seo-jun walked into the café again.

He barely made it to the counter before Eun-bi looked up.

"Americano, iced?"

He stopped.

"...You remember?"

"You order it every time."

"Do I?"

She nodded.

"Every time."

Seo-jun looked at her for a moment.

Then smiled.

"That's actually impressive."

Eun-bi looked down as she started preparing the drink.

"It's not that hard."

"Still."

He leaned against the counter.

"You remember everyone's orders?"

"Not everyone's."

She immediately realized what she'd said.

Her hands paused for half a second.

Seo-jun didn't notice.

"That's good. I'd hate to be just another customer."

Eun-bi quietly smiled.

"You aren't."

He didn't catch the meaning behind it.

He just took his drink and walked toward a table.

───

After that, it became normal.

Seo-jun would stop by.

Eun-bi would recognize him.

They'd talk for a few minutes.

Sometimes about coffee.

Sometimes about the weather.

Sometimes about nothing.

Seo-jun was good at talking to people.

He could start a conversation with almost anyone.

Eun-bi was the opposite.

She usually needed time before becoming comfortable with someone.

But with him, it happened faster.

Maybe because he never made her feel pressured.

Maybe because he filled the silence when she didn't know what to say.

Or maybe because she already liked him.

Either way, she slowly became more comfortable.

One afternoon, Seo-jun walked in looking exhausted.

Eun-bi noticed immediately.

"Long day?"

Seo-jun stopped.

"That obvious?"

"A little."

He laughed.

"Everyone's been asking me for changes today."

He dropped into a chair near the counter.

"I changed the same website six times."

Eun-bi placed his usual drink in front of him.

"Then maybe you should rest."

He looked at the drink.

"I didn't even order yet."

"You were going to."

He smiled.

"You're getting scary."

She smiled back.

"Sorry."

"Stop apologizing."

She blinked.

Then nodded.

"Okay."

Seo-jun took a sip.

"Thanks."

"You're welcome."

It was a small conversation.

But Eun-bi remembered it for the rest of the day.

And Seo-jun remembered that she'd noticed he was tired.

He didn't know why that mattered.

───

Another afternoon, Seo-jun wasn't alone.

Ha-eun walked beside him.

She had originally planned to go home after work, but Seo-jun had convinced her to stop for something to drink.

"I'm telling you, this place is good."

"You say that about every place."

"Because I have excellent taste."

Ha-eun looked at him.

"You once recommended instant noodles as a restaurant."

"That was different."

"How?"

"It was a very good instant noodle place."

She stared at him.

Seo-jun opened the café door.

"See? You're judging me again."

"I am."

They walked inside.

Eun-bi looked up.

Her expression brightened slightly when she recognized Seo-jun.

"Welcome."

Then she noticed Ha-eun.

"Oh."

Ha-eun smiled politely.

"Hi."

"You came before, right?"

Ha-eun blinked.

"You remember me?"

Eun-bi nodded.

"You came with him before."

Ha-eun glanced at Seo-jun.

"Did I?"

Seo-jun pointed at himself.

"See? I'm memorable."

"No one said that."

Eun-bi laughed quietly.

Seo-jun looked at her.

It was brief.

But he noticed it.

He smiled without thinking.

Ha-eun noticed that.

She didn't say anything.

They ordered their drinks and sat near the window.

Eun-bi brought them over.

Ha-eun took hers.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Ha-eun took a sip.

Then looked at Seo-jun.

"She seems really nice."

Seo-jun shrugged.

"Yeah."

Ha-eun glanced toward the counter.

Eun-bi was preparing another customer's drink.

"You come here a lot?"

Seo-jun looked at her.

"Sometimes."

Ha-eun raised an eyebrow.

"Sometimes?"

"Okay. Often."

"How often?"

"I don't keep track."

"You definitely keep track."

"I don't."

She smiled.

"Sure."

Seo-jun ignored her.

Across the room, Eun-bi glanced over.

She saw Ha-eun smiling.

She felt a little nervous.

But when Seo-jun looked back at her, she quickly looked away.

Ha-eun noticed that too.

She didn't say anything.

───

A few days later, Seo-jun came into the café alone.

Eun-bi was carrying a box of supplies.

She tried to move it behind the counter.

It was heavier than it looked.

Seo-jun noticed.

"Need help?"

"No, it's okay."

He took the box anyway.

"I've got it."

"But—"

"Where?"

Eun-bi pointed toward the back.

He carried it inside.

When he returned, she was standing there waiting.

"Thank you."

"No problem."

She looked at him for a moment.

"You always help people."

Seo-jun shrugged.

"Do I?"

"Yeah."

He smiled.

"I guess."

Eun-bi looked down.

"That's..."

She hesitated.

"That's nice."

Seo-jun gave her a confused look.

"What's nice?"

She shook her head.

"Nothing."

He laughed.

"You're weird."

She smiled.

"So are you."

He pointed at her.

"See? You're getting comfortable."

Eun-bi immediately looked embarrassed.

"Maybe."

He smiled and walked toward his usual table.

Neither of them noticed how natural the conversation had become.

───

Another day, Seo-jun arrived later than usual.

Eun-bi looked up from behind the counter.

"You're late."

He stopped.

"Late?"

She realized what she'd said.

"I mean..."

She looked down.

"You're usually here earlier."

Seo-jun stared at her for a second.

Then grinned.

"Were you waiting for me?"

Her face turned slightly red.

"No."

"You're sure?"

"Yes."

"Because it sounded like you were."

"I wasn't."

He laughed.

"Okay, okay."

He ordered his drink and sat down.

Eun-bi went back to work.

But every few minutes, she glanced toward his table.

And every time she did, Seo-jun happened to be looking somewhere else.

───

The encounters kept happening.

Nothing dramatic.

Nothing that would have made either of them stop and think, This is important.

Just small things.

A remembered order.

A short conversation.

A shared laugh.

A few minutes spent talking after the café had gotten quieter.

Seo-jun telling her about his web-design studio.

Eun-bi asking what kind of websites he made.

Eun-bi telling him about a difficult customer.

Seo-jun making her laugh.

Sometimes he'd stay longer than he planned.

Sometimes he'd leave quickly because he had work.

Sometimes Eun-bi would see him through the window before he even entered.

And every time the door opened, she knew who it was.

───

Then one evening, Seo-jun stopped by after work.

The café was quieter than usual.

Eun-bi was wiping down the counter.

She looked up.

"You're here."

Seo-jun smiled.

"That sounded disappointed."

"No."

"Relieved?"

"No."

"Excited?"

She paused.

"...Maybe."

Seo-jun laughed.

Eun-bi looked embarrassed.

"I didn't mean—"

"I know."

He sat down.

She prepared his drink without asking.

When she brought it over, he looked at it.

"You really know my order now."

"You always get the same thing."

"Maybe I should change it."

"Why?"

He looked at her.

She looked back.

"To surprise you."

Eun-bi smiled.

"I'd still remember."

Something about that made him quiet.

Not uncomfortable.

Just quiet.

He smiled.

"Then maybe I'll keep it."

She nodded.

"Good."

Neither of them said anything after that.

But the silence wasn't awkward.

It was comfortable.

And that was probably the first time Seo-jun started enjoying being there for a reason other than the coffee.

He just didn't know it yet.

───

Now.

Seo-jun sat across from Ha-Kim.

Ha-Kim was working on his laptop.

Seo-jun had been staring at the same spot on the table for almost five minutes.

Ha-Kim finally looked up.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

Ha-Kim waited.

Seo-jun sighed.

"Okay. Something."

"What?"

Seo-jun leaned back.

"I think I have a problem."

Ha-Kim closed his laptop.

"What kind?"

Seo-jun hesitated.

Then said it.

"I think I like someone."

Ha-Kim stared at him.

There was a brief silence.

"Who?"

Seo-jun looked away.

"Eun-bi."

Ha-Kim's expression didn't change.

"The café employee?"

"Yeah."

Another pause.

"How long have you known her?"

"A few weeks."

"How often do you see her?"

Seo-jun thought.

"Pretty often."

"Why?"

Seo-jun frowned.

"What do you mean, why?"

"Why do you keep going there?"

"I like the coffee."

Ha-Kim stared at him.

Seo-jun stared back.

"...Okay, maybe not just the coffee."

Ha-Kim nodded once.

Seo-jun looked down at the table.

"I don't know when it happened."

Ha-Kim opened his laptop again.

Seo-jun frowned.

"What are you doing?"

"Research."

"Research?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Ha-Kim looked at him.

"You're emotionally involved. Your judgment is compromised."

Seo-jun stared at him.

"Bro."

Ha-Kim continued typing.

"I need to know what kind of person she is."

"Why?"

"So you don't get hurt."

Seo-jun's mouth fell open.

"I came here for relationship advice."

"You are receiving it."

"You're researching my crush!"

Ha-Kim didn't look up.

"Correct."

Seo-jun leaned forward.

"You're insane."

Ha-Kim calmly typed something else.

"I've been told."

Seo-jun covered his face with both hands.

"I should've talked to someone normal."

From the kitchen, Ha-eun's voice suddenly came out.

"You definitely should have."

Seo-jun slowly looked toward her.

She was holding two cups.

Ha-Kim looked up.

"When did you get here?"

"About ten minutes ago."

Seo-jun pointed at Ha-Kim.

"He's investigating Eun-bi."

Ha-eun blinked.

"Why?"

"Because I like her."

Ha-eun smiled.

"Oh."

Seo-jun waited.

"That's it?"

She nodded.

"That's good."

Seo-jun looked at Ha-Kim.

"See? That's normal."

Ha-Kim continued typing.

Ha-eun walked over and looked at his screen.

She stared.

Then looked at him.

"Did you actually search her?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I explained."

Ha-eun looked at Seo-jun.

Then back at Ha-Kim.

"You're taking this way too seriously."

Ha-Kim closed the laptop.

"No."

Seo-jun pointed at him.

"Yes."

Ha-Kim looked at him.

"You're my friend."

Seo-jun stopped.

His expression softened slightly.

Then he smiled.

"...Thanks."

Ha-Kim nodded.

A second later, he added:

"That doesn't change the fact that I need more information."

Seo-jun's smile disappeared.

"Forget it."

Ha-eun quietly laughed.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She sat beside him.

Seo-jun looked between them.

Then sighed.

"I already regret telling you."

Ha-Kim opened his laptop again.

"You shouldn't."

"I absolutely should."

And somewhere between the café, the conversations, the remembered orders, and all the small moments he hadn't thought much about...

Seo-jun had finally realized something.

He didn't just like going to the café.

He liked seeing Eun-bi.

And now that he'd admitted it out loud, there was no going back.
