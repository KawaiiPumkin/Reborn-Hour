# The Apartment

A few weeks passed.

Things at Haetae Cyber had started to feel normal.

Ha-eun knew where everything was now.

She knew which elevator was usually the fastest.

She knew which printer was always broken.

She knew Ji-woo's favorite lunch.

And she knew that Ha-Kim drank coffee like it was the only thing keeping him alive.

She had also gotten better at her work.

Not perfect.

But better.

She still made mistakes.

Just fewer of them.

For the first time in a long time, Ha-eun felt like her life was moving somewhere.

Then her phone started ringing.

───

She was walking home when it happened.

Her phone vibrated in her pocket.

She looked at the screen.

Unknown number.

She stopped walking.

The street around her was busy.

Cars passed.

People walked by.

Nobody seemed to notice her.

The phone kept ringing.

Ha-eun stared at it.

Then answered.

She didn't say anything.

Neither did the person on the other end.

A few seconds passed.

Then a familiar voice spoke.

“You've been hard to reach.”

Ha-eun's face changed.

She looked around.

“Don't call me.”

“You know that's not how this works.”

Her fingers tightened around the phone.

“I don't have anything.”

“You have a job now.”

Ha-eun said nothing.

“You're making money.”

“Not enough.”

“You'll figure it out.”

The call ended.

Ha-eun stood there.

Her hand slowly lowered.

For a few seconds, she didn't move.

Then she put her phone away and continued walking.

She didn't look back.

───

That night, Ha-eun sat on the floor of her apartment.

A small table was in front of her.

Her salary had arrived.

She looked at the number on her phone.

Then opened another screen.

A payment.

She stared at it for a long time.

Her finger hovered over the button.

She didn't want to press it.

But she knew what would happen if she didn't.

She sent the money.

The number in her account dropped.

Ha-eun stared at the screen.

“...Great.”

She opened her wallet.

A few bills.

That was it.

She looked toward the kitchen.

There was almost nothing there.

She opened the cabinet.

Instant noodles.

Again.

She sighed.

“I'm going to turn into noodles.”

She took one anyway.

───

The next morning, Ha-eun arrived at work with a cup of convenience-store coffee.

Ji-woo looked at it.

“You stopped buying cafeteria coffee?”

Ha-eun nodded.

“It's expensive.”

“You used to buy it.”

“I've changed.”

Ji-woo stared at her.

“You sound like someone's grandmother.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“Maybe I am.”

Ji-woo laughed.

Ha-eun laughed too.

For a moment, everything felt normal.

Then her phone vibrated.

Her smile disappeared.

She looked at the screen.

Another message.

Don't forget.

She locked the phone.

Ha-Kim looked up from his desk.

He had seen her expression change.

He didn't say anything.

Ha-eun put the phone away.

“Everything okay?”

Ha-Kim asked.

She looked at him.

“Yeah.”

He stared at her for a second.

Then nodded.

“Okay.”

He returned to his screen.

Ha-eun looked at him.

She had expected another question.

He didn't ask one.

For some reason, that made it easier to breathe.

───

Over the next few weeks, little things changed.

Ha-eun stopped eating lunch in the cafeteria.

She started bringing cheap food from home.

Sometimes bread.

Sometimes rice.

Sometimes nothing.

When Ji-woo asked, she always had an excuse.

“I ate earlier.”

“I'm not hungry.”

“I'll eat later.”

It became normal.

At least, that's what she wanted everyone to think.

She also stopped looking at clothes when she walked past stores.

Not because she didn't like them.

She simply didn't have money for them anymore.

Her shoes started wearing down.

The soles were getting thin.

One morning, she looked at them before leaving her apartment.

She sighed.

“Just a little longer.”

She put them on.

───

The gang called again.

Then again.

Then again.

Each time, Ha-eun tried to keep the conversation short.

She never told them where she worked.

She never told them who she worked with.

She never mentioned Haetae.

She didn't want anyone from her old life anywhere near her new one.

But they knew enough.

Enough to keep finding her.

Enough to remind her that running away hadn't erased anything.

One evening, she received another message.

You still owe us.

Ha-eun stared at it.

She typed a reply.

Deleted it.

Typed again.

Deleted it again.

Finally, she locked her phone.

Her hands were shaking.

She put the phone face down on the table.

Then sat there in silence.

She didn't want to go back.

She couldn't.

Whatever happened next, she had to deal with it herself.

───

Her rent was due.

Ha-eun opened her banking app.

She stared at the balance.

Then at the rent amount.

Then back at the balance.

She calculated it again.

It didn't change.

She didn't have enough.

“Maybe next month.”

She said it quietly.

As if saying it would make it true.

A few days later, she received a message from her landlord.

She read it.

Then read it again.

Rent is overdue. Please make the payment as soon as possible.

Ha-eun swallowed.

She replied that she would pay.

She promised it wouldn't happen again.

She closed her phone.

Then put her head down on the table.

“I'm sorry.”

She didn't even know who she was apologizing to.

───

At work, she acted normal.

She laughed with Ji-woo.

She worked with Ha-Kim.

She complained when he gave her difficult tasks.

She smiled when she finished them.

Nobody knew.

Or at least, that's what she thought.

Ha-Kim had started noticing things.

He noticed that she checked her phone more often.

He noticed that she never ate properly anymore.

He noticed she sometimes stared at nothing for several seconds.

He noticed the tiredness around her eyes.

And he noticed that she kept saying she was fine.

Too often.

───

One afternoon, Ha-Kim walked past her desk.

“Have you eaten?”

Ha-eun looked up.

“Yeah.”

“What?”

She froze.

“Uh...”

He waited.

“Something.”

“What?”

“Food.”

He stared at her.

She stared back.

Then smiled awkwardly.

“Why are you interrogating me?”

“I'm asking.”

“Then yes.”

He looked at her.

She looked back.

Neither said anything.

Finally, Ha-Kim nodded.

“Okay.”

He walked away.

Ha-eun watched him.

She quietly sighed.

“He notices too much.”

───

A week later, the landlord gave her another warning.

This time, there was no extra time.

Ha-eun stood in the doorway of her apartment after reading the message.

Her hand stayed on the door handle.

She looked around.

The small room.

The old bed.

The cracked table.

The clothes hanging near the wall.

It wasn't much.

But it was hers.

She had spent nights here alone.

She had eaten here.

Cried here.

Laughed here.

She had imagined that maybe this place could become the beginning of a normal life.

Now she had to leave.

She sat on the bed.

Her phone was in her hand.

She opened her contacts.

Ji-woo.

She stared at the name.

Then Ha-Kim.

She stared at that one too.

Her thumb hovered over his name.

She could call him.

He would probably help.

That was the problem.

She didn't want his help.

She didn't want anyone looking at her like she was helpless.

She locked the phone.

“No.”

She shook her head.

“I'll figure it out.”

───

Two days later, Ha-eun came home after work.

There were boxes outside her apartment.

She stopped.

Her heart dropped.

Her belongings had been packed.

A few bags.

A box of clothes.

Her old shoes.

A small kitchen bag.

That was almost everything she owned.

She stood there silently.

The hallway felt colder than usual.

She unlocked the door.

The apartment was nearly empty.

Ha-eun stepped inside.

She looked around.

Nothing had changed.

And somehow everything had.

She slowly sat on the floor.

Her back rested against the wall.

She stared at the empty room.

“What am I supposed to do now?”

No answer.

Of course.

She wiped her eyes quickly.

She didn't want to cry.

Not now.

She reached for her phone again.

Ji-woo.

Ha-Kim.

She looked at the names.

Then locked the screen.

She couldn't tell them.

Not yet.

Maybe tomorrow.

She picked up one of the bags.

Then another.

She left the apartment.

The door closed behind her.

───

That night, Ha-Kim couldn't sleep.

He had gone to bed earlier than usual.

But something felt wrong.

He didn't know why.

He closed his eyes.

The room disappeared.

Rain.

That was the first thing he noticed.

Rain falling somewhere nearby.

He stood in the middle of an unfamiliar street.

It was dark.

The buildings around him were impossible to recognize.

He looked ahead.

Someone was standing in the distance.

A small figure.

He couldn't see their face.

Ha-Kim took a step forward.

The figure didn't move.

He took another.

“Who are you?”

No answer.

The rain became heavier.

Then he heard a voice.

Quiet.

Almost too quiet to hear.

“Don't leave me.”

Ha-Kim froze.

Something about the voice felt familiar.

He tried to move again.

The figure suddenly disappeared.

Ha-Kim looked around.

Nothing.

Then he woke up.

───

Ha-Kim sat upright in bed.

His breathing was heavier than usual.

The room was dark.

He looked around.

Nothing.

He checked the clock.

3:17 AM.

He rubbed his face.

“What the hell...”

He rarely remembered his dreams.

He almost never woke up like this.

Ha-Kim got out of bed.

He walked to the kitchen and poured himself a glass of water.

He stood there for a moment.

Then looked toward the window.

Rain wasn't falling outside.

The streets were dry.

He frowned.

“Just a dream.”

He went back to bed.

Closed his eyes.

For a moment, everything was quiet.

Then he saw it again.

The same figure.

Only for a second.

Ha-Kim opened his eyes.

Nothing.

He sat there silently.

He didn't sleep again.

───

The next morning, Ha-eun arrived at Haetae Cyber.

She looked normal.

At least she tried to.

She smiled at Ji-woo.

“Morning.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“Morning!”

Ha-eun walked toward her desk.

Ha-Kim looked up.

Something was different.

He didn't know what.

He watched her sit down.

She placed a small bag beside her chair.

Not her usual bag.

He looked at it.

Then at her.

“Where are you going after work?”

Ha-eun stopped.

“What?”

“After work.”

“Home.”

Ha-Kim stared at her.

“Your apartment?”

There was a pause.

Ha-eun smiled.

“Yeah.”

It was a small smile.

Too quick.

“Okay.”

Ha-Kim turned back to his computer.

Ha-eun looked at him.

Her fingers tightened around the edge of her desk.

She had lied.

He knew.

She knew he knew.

Neither said anything.

───

At the end of the day, Ha-eun packed her things.

She looked around the office.

Ji-woo was talking to another employee.

People were leaving.

The building was still busy.

Everything looked normal.

Ha-eun put her bag over her shoulder.

“See you tomorrow.”

Ji-woo waved.

“See you!”

Ha-eun smiled.

Then walked toward the elevator.

Ha-Kim watched her.

She pressed the button.

The doors opened.

She stepped inside.

The doors began closing.

Ha-Kim stood up.

He wasn't sure why.

He walked toward the elevator.

The doors had already closed.

He stopped.

His eyes moved toward the glass entrance.

Through it, he could see Ha-eun walking away from the building.

She wasn't heading toward the bus stop she normally used.

She was carrying more than usual.

Two bags.

And she kept looking behind her.

Ha-Kim stood there.

Something about it bothered him.

He didn't know what.

He only knew that she wasn't going home.

And for the first time...

Ha-Kim wondered where she actually had to go.

Outside, Ha-eun kept walking.

She had nowhere to go.

But she kept walking anyway.

Behind her, the glass doors reflected the lights of Haetae Cyber.

And for a moment, Ha-Kim's reflection stood there alone.

Watching.

Unmoving.

Then his phone vibrated.

CEO CHANNEL: PRIORITY MESSAGE.

He ignored it.

His eyes stayed on the doors.
