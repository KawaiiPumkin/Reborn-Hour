Chapter 24 — The People Around Us
Monday morning came with the usual noise of Haetae Cyber.
Phones rang.
Keyboards clicked.
People rushed between desks with coffee in their hands.
Ji-woo dropped into her chair and immediately looked toward Ha-eun.
“You're smiling.”
Ha-eun paused while turning on her computer.
“No, I'm not.”
“You are.”
“I just got here.”
“Exactly. Something happened before you got here.”
Ha-eun looked away.
Ji-woo leaned closer.
“Was it Ha-Kim?”
Ha-eun's ears turned slightly red.
“Why do you always know?”
“Because you're terrible at hiding it.”
Ha-eun quietly opened her first file.
“He made breakfast.”
Ji-woo stared at her.
“He made breakfast?”
“Yeah.”
“And?”
“He packed some for me.”
Ji-woo put a hand over her chest.
“That is disgustingly domestic.”
Ha-eun tried not to smile.
“He just made too much.”
“Sure.”
“He did.”
“Sure.”
Ha-eun looked at her.
“You're annoying.”
“You're happy.”
That made Ha-eun stop.
For a moment, she didn't answer.
Then she gave a small smile.
“Yeah.”
───
Across the office, Seo-jun was having a completely different problem.
He had been staring at his phone for almost a minute.
A message sat on the screen.
Eun-bi:Are you free after work?
Seo-jun smiled.
He typed back.
Seo-jun:For you? Always.
Three dots appeared.
Then disappeared.
Then appeared again.
Eun-bi:That's cheesy.
Seo-jun:But you smiled.
There was a long pause.
Eun-bi:Maybe.
Seo-jun leaned back in his chair.
He was still smiling when Ha-Kim walked past.
“You look stupid.”
Seo-jun looked up.
“Good morning to you too.”
“You've been staring at your phone for five minutes.”
“I'm texting my girlfriend.”
Ha-Kim stopped.
Seo-jun looked at him.
Ha-Kim looked back.
“Girlfriend?”
Seo-jun grinned.
“Yeah.”
Ha-Kim nodded once.
“Congratulations.”
“That's it?”
“What do you want?”
“I don't know. Something emotional.”
“You're dating Eun-bi. You're not dying.”
Seo-jun laughed.
“You really don't know how to celebrate anything.”
“I bought you coffee.”
“You did?”
Ha-Kim pointed toward Seo-jun's desk.
A coffee sat there.
Seo-jun picked it up.
“You remembered my order.”
“I remember everything.”
Seo-jun stared at him.
“That's actually a little scary.”
Ha-Kim walked away.
Seo-jun smiled again.
His phone buzzed.
Eun-bi:See you later.
He typed back.
Seo-jun:I'll be waiting.
This time, he didn't hesitate before sending it.
───
After work, Seo-jun stood outside the café.
Eun-bi came out a few minutes later.
She was still wearing her work clothes.
“You waited long?”
“No.”
“You're lying.”
“Maybe a little.”
She smiled.
Seo-jun held out his hand.
She looked at it.
Then at him.
“You're getting bold.”
“I have a girlfriend now.”
“And?”
“I think I'm allowed.”
Eun-bi quietly took his hand.
“Maybe.”
They started walking.
Nothing dramatic happened.
They talked about work.
They argued about what to eat.
Seo-jun wanted something spicy.
Eun-bi wanted something simple.
In the end, they went somewhere in between.
It was ordinary.
And neither of them wanted anything else.
───
Meanwhile, on the fourth floor, Han Ji-hoon stood in front of the conference room.
Another meeting with the CEO had just ended.
The screen had gone dark.
Ji-hoon remained seated.
Something still bothered him.
He opened the meeting record on his tablet.
The CEO had known about a server issue before the department responsible for it had officially reported the problem.
It could have been coincidence.
Or someone could have passed the information along.
But there was something else.
Ji-hoon checked the access logs.
The CEO's account had accessed the report remotely.
Nothing strange there.
Except the timestamp.
Ji-hoon frowned.
The account had accessed the file from a location that wasn't listed among the company's registered executive offices.
He checked it again.
Then again.
His expression didn't change.
But his eyes narrowed.
“Where are you working from?”
He leaned back.
He wasn't going to confront anyone.
Not yet.
He needed more.
One inconsistency could be explained.
Two could be coincidence.
Three...
He closed the file.
He would keep watching.
───
The following afternoon, a department meeting was called.
Min-hyuk entered first.
“Everyone, this is Park Seo-yeon.”
A woman followed him into the room.
She was thirty, dressed neatly, carrying a thin folder under one arm.
Her expression was calm.
Not friendly.
Not unfriendly.
Just focused.
“This is her first time working directly with this department,” Min-hyuk continued. “She's leading the security response team.”
Seo-yeon gave a short nod.
“Nice to meet you.”
Several employees greeted her.
She looked around the room.
Her eyes stopped on the presentation displayed on the screen.
“This report is wrong.”
The room went quiet.
Min-hyuk looked at the screen.
“What part?”
Seo-yeon walked closer.
“The risk estimate.”
She pointed at one section.
“You've calculated the expected loss using the current failure rate.”
“Yes.”
“That rate won't stay current if the vulnerability is exploited.”
Min-hyuk crossed his arms.
“You're assuming exploitation.”
“I'm preparing for it.”
“You're doubling the estimated risk.”
“Because you're underestimating the people trying to break into the system.”
Min-hyuk looked at her.
Seo-yeon looked back.
Neither seemed willing to move.
One of the employees quietly looked down at their notes.
This was going to be a long meeting.
Min-hyuk finally said, “Show me your numbers.”
Seo-yeon opened her folder.
“I already prepared them.”
A faint smile appeared on Min-hyuk's face.
“Of course you did.”
She noticed.
“Something funny?”
“No.”
“Good.”
The meeting continued.
They disagreed on almost everything.
Timeline.
Resources.
Risk.
Priorities.
But neither argument was pointless.
Every time Min-hyuk challenged her, Seo-yeon had an answer.
Every time Seo-yeon pushed back, Min-hyuk found another weakness in her proposal.
By the end, the entire room was exhausted.
Except them.
Seo-yeon closed her folder.
“You're difficult.”
Min-hyuk stood.
“So are you.”
She gave him a small, almost amused look.
“Good.”
“Why?”
“Because I'd hate to be the only difficult person here.”
Min-hyuk almost smiled.
Almost.
“Tomorrow. Nine.”
“I'll be there.”
“Don't be late.”
“I don't.”
They walked out in opposite directions.
Neither noticed that both were thinking about the same thing.
Finally, someone who can keep up.
───
That evening, Ha-eun arrived home before Ha-Kim.
She dropped her bag beside the sofa and looked around.
The apartment still felt different.
There were more of her things now.
A blanket she liked.
Books on the shelf.
Hair ties near the bathroom.
A little ceramic cat she'd insisted on buying.
She smiled.
Then her phone buzzed.
Ha-Kim:I'm leaving work.
She replied.
Ha-eun:Okay.
A few seconds later:
Ha-Kim:Do you want anything?
She thought about it.
Ha-eun:Strawberries.
The reply came almost immediately.
Ha-Kim:That's specific.
Ha-eun:I want strawberries.
Ha-Kim:Fine.
She smiled at her phone.
When he came home twenty minutes later, he was carrying a small bag.
Ha-eun immediately looked inside.
“Strawberries.”
“You asked for them.”
She picked one up.
“You're useful.”
Ha-Kim stared at her.
“That's what I get?”
She smiled.
“Thank you.”
He nodded.
She ate one.
Her expression softened.
“Good.”
“You like them?”
“I love them.”
He watched her for a second.
Then took off his coat.
She walked toward the kitchen.
“I'll make dinner.”
“You're tired.”
“So are you.”
“I can do it.”
“I know.”
She looked back at him.
“But I want to.”
Ha-Kim didn't argue.
He simply followed her into the kitchen.
───
Later that night, Seo-jun sent Eun-bi a message.
Seo-jun:Did you get home?
Eun-bi:Yes.
Seo-jun:Good.
A moment later:
Eun-bi:Thank you for today.
Seo-jun smiled.
Seo-jun:Anytime.
Then another message appeared.
Eun-bi:Goodnight, boyfriend.
Seo-jun stared at the screen.
His face slowly turned red.
He covered his mouth with one hand.
“...Wow.”
Across the city, Eun-bi sat on her bed, staring at her phone with an equally embarrassed smile.
Neither of them had expected a simple word to make them this happy.
Boyfriend.
Girlfriend.
It was real now.
───
At Haetae, Ji-hoon sat alone at his desk.
He opened the CEO records again.
The same strange access time.
The same unusual location.
He stared at it.
Then closed the file.
He wasn't ready to accuse anyone.
But he wasn't going to forget it either.
Somewhere in the building, Ha-Kim walked past an empty hallway.
He stopped for a moment.
His eyes rested on the dark office windows.
Then he continued walking.
He didn't know Ji-hoon had noticed anything.
And Ji-hoon didn't know how close he actually was.
For now, both continued with their own lives.
───
By the end of the week, small things had changed.
Seo-jun and Eun-bi were no longer two people wondering if they liked each other.
They were a couple.
Ji-hoon's suspicion had grown by another small degree.
Min-hyuk had found someone who could actually challenge him.
Seo-yeon had found someone she didn't want to lose to.
And Ha-eun had slowly stopped thinking of Ha-Kim's apartment as somewhere she stayed.
It was home.
Their home.
She sat beside him on the sofa that night, her head resting against his shoulder.
“What are you thinking about?”
Ha-Kim looked down at her.
“Nothing.”
She smiled.
“That's rare.”
“It happens.”
She shifted closer.
“Good.”
He didn't ask what she meant.
She didn't explain.
Outside, Seoul continued moving.
People went to work.
People fell in love.
People chased secrets.
People met someone who challenged them.
Everyone had something ahead of them.
And for once, Ha-Kim didn't feel the need to know what it was.
He had enough of today.
