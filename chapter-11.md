# Chapter 11 — Don't Let Me Cook

A few days passed.

Ha-eun was slowly getting used to her new apartment.

She knew which elevator was slower.

She knew the shower took a few seconds to become warm.

She knew someone upstairs had a strange habit of moving furniture late at night.

And, unfortunately, she was starting to recognize Ha-Kim's schedule.

Not because she was watching him.

They just kept running into each other.

Sometimes in the elevator.

Sometimes outside the building.

Sometimes on their way to work.

After a while, neither of them found it particularly strange anymore.

That evening, Ha-eun came home hungry.

She dropped her bag beside the door and looked toward the kitchen.

Her stomach growled.

She opened the cabinet.

Instant noodles.

She stared at them.

"No."

She closed the cabinet.

"I eat these too much."

She opened the refrigerator.

There wasn't much inside.

A few eggs.

Some vegetables.

A small package of meat she had bought because it was discounted.

Ha-eun stared at everything.

She could make something with that.

Probably.

She pulled out her phone.

"How hard can this be?"

---

Twenty minutes later, she regretted saying that.

"Why is it sticking?"

Ha-eun stared into the pan.

The food was definitely sticking.

She poked it with a spatula.

Nothing.

She pushed harder.

Half of it came loose.

The other half remained attached to the pan.

She checked the recipe.

Then looked back at the pan.

"This doesn't look like the picture."

She added a little oil.

Then a little more.

Probably too much.

"That's fine."

She reached for the vegetables.

One slipped from her hand.

She caught it.

Another fell onto the floor.

She stared at it.

"Five-second rule."

She picked it up.

Paused.

Then threw it away.

"Actually, no."

The pan hissed behind her.

Ha-eun turned around.

"Oh."

Smoke was starting to rise.

She lowered the heat.

At least, she thought she did.

More smoke appeared.

"No, no, no."

She quickly turned the stove off.

The smoke kept rising.

Then the smoke detector started screaming.

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

Ha-eun looked up.

"No."

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

She grabbed a towel and started waving it around.

"Stop!"

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

"I know!"

She opened the window.

Then another.

She ran back toward the detector.

The smoke was getting worse.

Someone knocked on the door.

Ha-eun froze.

Another knock.

She looked around her apartment.

Smoke.

A destroyed pan.

The smoke detector screaming.

She slowly walked to the door.

"Who is it?"

"It's me."

Ha-eun immediately recognized the voice.

She opened the door.

Ha-Kim stood outside.

He looked at her.

Then at the smoke.

Then back at her.

"Are you on fire?"

"No."

A cloud of smoke drifted past her.

Ha-Kim watched it.

"Your apartment disagrees."

"It's under control."

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

Ha-Kim looked up.

Then back at her.

"Move."

"I have it."

"You're fighting a smoke detector."

"It started it."

He stepped inside.

Ha-eun moved aside.

Ha-Kim went straight to the kitchen.

He checked the stove.

Off.

Windows.

Open.

He moved the pan somewhere safe and checked that nothing else was burning.

A few minutes later, most of the smoke had disappeared.

Ha-eun stood in the middle of the kitchen holding the towel.

Ha-Kim looked at the pan.

He stared at it for several seconds.

Ha-eun crossed her arms.

"Don't."

"What was this?"

"Dinner."

He looked again.

"Before that."

"Food."

"Are you sure?"

"Get out."

Ha-Kim almost smiled.

Almost.

Instead, he looked around the kitchen.

There wasn't much food.

He opened the refrigerator.

Ha-eun immediately became defensive.

"Don't judge my refrigerator."

"I'm not."

"You're looking at it."

"I'm checking what you have."

"That's worse."

He closed the refrigerator.

"You were going to cook this?"

"Yes."

"You've never cooked before."

"I have."

Ha-Kim looked at the pan.

Ha-eun sighed.

"Not successfully."

He looked at the remaining ingredients.

Then rolled up his sleeves.

Ha-eun blinked.

"What are you doing?"

"Cooking."

"You can cook?"

"Yes."

"Since when?"

"A long time."

She watched him take the ingredients.

"Who taught you?"

"Myself."

There was a small pause.

Ha-eun noticed it.

She didn't ask anything else.

Instead, she leaned against the counter.

"Can I watch?"

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"Why?"

"I want to learn."

"You should probably start by not burning things."

"That's not an answer."

"Move."

She moved.

He started cooking.

Ha-eun watched carefully.

He didn't use a recipe.

He didn't measure everything.

He just seemed to know what he was doing.

"You're actually good at this."

"I know."

She looked at him.

"That sounded arrogant."

"It wasn't."

"It definitely was."

He ignored her.

A few minutes later, the smell filling the apartment was completely different.

Ha-eun looked at the food.

"That's unfair."

"What?"

"You made it look easy."

"It is easy."

She frowned.

"I hate you."

"No, you don't."

The words came out naturally.

Both of them went quiet for a second.

Ha-Kim continued cooking.

Ha-eun looked away.

"...Maybe a little."

---

They sat across from each other at the small table.

Ha-eun looked at the food.

Then took a bite.

She stopped.

Ha-Kim noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You don't like it?"

"No."

He looked at her.

She quickly corrected herself.

"No, I mean... I do."

"Then why did you say no?"

"I don't know."

She took another bite.

"This is really good."

Ha-Kim nodded.

"Thanks."

Ha-eun looked toward the kitchen.

The destroyed pan was still sitting there.

She sighed.

"Now I feel worse."

"Why?"

"Because mine was terrible."

"It was."

She stared at him.

"You could've lied."

"You would've known."

She laughed quietly.

For a while, they ate without talking.

The silence didn't feel uncomfortable.

It was different from the silence they used to share at work.

There was no keyboard clicking.

No coworkers nearby.

No manager.

No reason to hurry.

Just two people eating dinner.

When they finished, Ha-eun looked at the clock.

"I should clean."

"I'll do it."

"No, you cooked."

"You nearly burned the building down."

"I did not."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

She sighed.

"Fine."

They cleaned the kitchen together.

It took longer than it should have because Ha-eun kept getting in the way.

Eventually, Ha-Kim handed her the towel.

"You're making it worse."

"I am helping."

"You're moving the same plate three times."

"I'm organizing."

He looked at her.

"Right."

She smiled.

---

The next evening, nothing happened.

Ha-eun went home.

Ha-Kim went home.

They didn't see each other.

And neither of them thought much about it.

At least, that's what Ha-eun told herself.

The following evening, they ran into each other in the elevator.

"Hey."

"Hi."

"How was work?"

"Fine."

"Yours?"

"Fine."

The elevator reached their floor.

They walked out.

"Good night."

"Good night."

They went into their apartments.

That was it.

No dinner.

No conversation.

Nothing unusual.

And somehow, Ha-eun found herself looking at the instant noodles in her cabinet.

She sighed.

"Tomorrow."

---

A few days later, someone knocked on Ha-Kim's door.

He opened it.

Ha-eun stood there holding two drinks.

"I brought these."

Ha-Kim looked at them.

"Why?"

"You cooked for me."

"That was several days ago."

"I was busy."

He looked at the drinks.

"You bought them because they were on sale."

She froze.

"That is completely unrelated."

Ha-Kim stepped aside.

She smiled and walked in.

This time, she didn't stand awkwardly near the door.

She sat down on the couch.

Ha-Kim noticed.

He didn't say anything.

She looked around.

"So what are you doing?"

"Working."

"Of course."

She opened her drink.

He went back to his laptop.

For a while, neither spoke.

Ha-eun looked at her phone.

Ha-Kim worked.

The silence was comfortable.

Eventually, Ha-eun looked up.

"You're not annoyed?"

"About what?"

"Me being here."

"No."

"Oh."

She smiled.

"Good."

---

A few days later, Ha-eun came home from work and realized something terrible.

She couldn't find her keys.

She checked her pockets.

Her bag.

Her jacket.

Nothing.

She stood outside her apartment and stared at the door.

"No."

She checked her bag again.

Still nothing.

She sighed and sat down against the wall.

She called the building manager.

He answered after a few rings.

"Hello?"

"Hi. I think I locked myself out."

There was a short pause.

"I can come up with the spare key, but I'm still outside the building. It'll take about twenty minutes."

Ha-eun looked at the hallway.

"Okay."

She ended the call.

A few minutes later, Ha-Kim walked down the hallway.

He stopped when he saw her sitting on the floor.

He looked at her.

Then at the locked door.

Then back at her.

"What happened?"

"I lost my keys."

"You lost them?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I don't know."

He nodded.

She pointed at him.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"My face isn't doing anything."

"It is extremely judgmental."

Ha-Kim unlocked his apartment.

"You can wait inside."

Ha-eun looked at his door.

Then at him.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

She hesitated.

"Okay."

She stood up and followed him inside.

This was the first time she had been in his apartment properly.

She looked around.

Everything was clean.

Not overly decorated.

Not empty either.

Just quiet.

She looked at him.

"I expected something darker."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

She looked around again.

"I thought you'd have one chair and a computer."

"I have two chairs."

She laughed.

Ha-Kim went to the kitchen.

"Want something to drink?"

"Water is fine."

He handed her a glass.

She sat on the couch.

At first, she sat straight.

Then she relaxed.

Then she pulled her legs up slightly.

Ha-Kim noticed but said nothing.

Twenty minutes later, the building manager knocked.

Ha-eun stood up.

"That was fast."

Ha-Kim opened the door.

The manager handed Ha-eun the spare key.

"Here you go."

"Thank you."

She turned toward Ha-Kim.

"Thanks for letting me stay."

"No problem."

She walked toward her apartment.

Then stopped.

She looked back.

"Good night."

"Good night."

She entered her apartment.

Ha-Kim closed his door.

---

After that, things changed slowly.

Not all at once.

Ha-eun started stopping by more often.

Sometimes she brought a drink.

Sometimes she brought food.

Sometimes she had no reason at all.

One evening, Ha-Kim came home with groceries.

Ha-eun happened to be leaving her apartment.

She looked at the bags.

"Are you cooking?"

"Yes."

"For yourself?"

"Yes."

She looked at him.

Then at the groceries.

Then back at him.

"Can I eat too?"

Ha-Kim sighed.

"You have food."

"Not good food."

"You can cook."

She gave him a look.

They both remembered the smoke detector.

Ha-Kim unlocked his door.

"Come in."

She smiled.

"Thank you."

Another evening, she knocked on his door.

Ha-Kim opened it.

"I'm hungry."

"Hello to you too."

"Hi."

He stared at her.

She smiled.

"I'm still hungry."

He sighed.

"Come in."

She walked inside.

It was becoming normal.

Too normal.

One night, Ha-eun was sitting at his table while he worked.

She wasn't talking.

He wasn't talking.

They were both doing their own thing.

After a while, she looked up.

"Are we boring?"

"Yes."

She frowned.

"Oh."

A few seconds passed.

"I kind of like it."

Ha-Kim's fingers paused over the keyboard.

"Me too."

She smiled and returned to her phone.

Neither of them noticed how natural it had become.

---

One evening, Ha-eun stood at Ha-Kim's door after dinner.

"I should go."

"Yes."

She frowned.

"You could at least pretend you don't want me to leave."

"You said you should go."

"I know."

"So go."

She stared at him.

Then smiled.

"Good night, Ha-Kim."

"Good night."

She stepped into the hallway.

Then turned around.

"Oh."

Ha-Kim looked at her.

"Tomorrow I'm cooking."

His expression immediately changed.

"No."

Ha-eun laughed.

The door closed behind her.

Her footsteps slowly disappeared down the hallway.

Ha-Kim returned to his desk.

He sat down.

His laptop was exactly where he had left it.

His coffee was beside it.

Everything was the same.

Quiet.

For years, silence had never bothered him.

Most of the time, he preferred it.

Ha-Kim looked toward the door.

The apartment felt strangely empty.

He stayed still for a moment.

Then looked back at his laptop.

Tonight, the silence felt a little different.
