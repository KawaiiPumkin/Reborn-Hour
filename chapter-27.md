# Chapter 27 — Something Is Coming
The rain didn't stop.
It wasn't heavy anymore, but the streets stayed wet, reflecting the lights from buildings and cars.
Ha-Kim stood by the window, watching the city.
Behind him, Ha-eun was still asleep.
He had barely slept after the vision.
Every time he closed his eyes, he saw it again.
The black figure.
The rain.
That smaller shadow.
Her.
And those two words.
Too late.
Ha-Kim looked down at his hand.
It was steady now.
His mind wasn't.
He had seen strange things before.
The first shadow.
The darkness.
The red light.
The voice.
But this time felt different.
It wasn't just something waiting in the distance.
It felt close.
Too close.
A soft sound came from behind him.
"You're awake?"
Ha-Kim turned.
Ha-eun was sitting up, her hair messy from sleep.
"What time is it?"
"Seven."
"Why are you awake at seven?"
"I woke up."
"That's not an answer."
"It is technically an answer."
She stared at him.
He stared back.
She sighed and dropped back onto the pillow.
"You're annoying in the morning."
"I've been told."
"You've been told by me."
"Exactly."
A tiny smile appeared on her face.
She reached one hand toward him.
"Come here."
Ha-Kim hesitated.
"Why?"
"Just come here."
He walked over.
Ha-eun grabbed his wrist and pulled him down beside her.
She rested her head against his shoulder.
For a few seconds, neither spoke.
Then she quietly said,
"You had that dream again, didn't you?"
Ha-Kim's eyes shifted toward her.
"How do you know?"
"You looked scared."
"I wasn't scared."
"You were."
He didn't answer.
Ha-eun looked up at him.
"You don't have to tell me if you don't want to."
A pause.
"But don't lie to me either."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"I'll tell you when I know what it means."
She studied his face.
"Okay."
No questions.
No pressure.
Just okay.
That somehow made it worse.
Because he knew something she didn't.
And he didn't know how long he could keep it that way.
───
By the time they left for work, the rain had stopped.
Ha-eun walked beside him with an umbrella tucked under her arm.
"You know," she said, "we could just stay home."
"We have work."
"You could take the day off."
"I could."
"But you won't."
"No."
She sighed.
"You work too much."
"So do you."
"I'm still new. I have an excuse."
"That's not how excuses work."
She looked at him.
"...You're annoying."
"I've been told."
She smiled.
They reached the subway station.
Ha-eun suddenly stopped.
Ha-Kim took two more steps before noticing.
He turned.
"What?"
She was staring across the street.
A man stood near a convenience store.
Dark jacket.
Black cap.
He wasn't doing anything.
Just standing there.
Ha-eun's expression changed.
Not fear.
Not exactly.
Something quieter.
Recognition.
Ha-Kim followed her gaze.
The man looked in their direction.
Then turned away.
Ha-eun lowered her eyes.
"Do you know him?"
"No."
The answer came too quickly.
Ha-Kim noticed.
"You sure?"
"Yeah."
She started walking again.
Ha-Kim followed.
He didn't ask again.
But he looked back.
The man was gone.
───
At Haetae, Ha-eun tried to act normal.
She answered emails.
Worked through reports.
Made two mistakes.
Corrected both.
Ji-woo leaned over the divider.
"You're distracted."
"I'm not."
"You've been staring at the same screen for five minutes."
Ha-eun blinked.
"Oh."
Ji-woo narrowed her eyes.
"Something happen?"
"No."
"Boyfriend?"
Ha-eun immediately looked down.
"...No."
Ji-woo smiled.
"That was definitely a yes."
Ha-eun quietly pushed her shoulder.
"Go work."
"I'm working."
"You're bothering me."
"I'm multitasking."
Ha-eun couldn't stop the small smile.
For a moment, everything felt normal again.
Then her phone vibrated.
She looked down.
Unknown number.
One message.
You disappeared without paying what you owe.
Her smile disappeared.
She stared at the screen.
Another message arrived.
We know where you are.
Her fingers went cold.
She locked the phone.
Ji-woo noticed.
"You okay?"
Ha-eun nodded.
"Yeah."
But her voice was different.
Ji-woo didn't push.
Ha-eun looked toward the office windows.
Across the floor, Ha-Kim was speaking with someone.
Calm.
Normal.
Like nothing had happened.
She looked back at her phone.
Her old life had found her.
Again.
───
Ha-Kim noticed her change before lunch.
She was quieter.
Not unusually quiet.
Just enough.
He waited until they were alone near the elevator.
"What's wrong?"
"Nothing."
"You've said that twice today."
"Because nothing's wrong."
The elevator doors opened.
They stepped inside.
Ha-Kim looked at her reflection in the metal doors.
"You're lying."
Ha-eun looked at him.
He wasn't angry.
He was simply watching her.
She looked down.
"My past."
He waited.
She swallowed.
"Someone might have found me."
The elevator continued downward.
"Who?"
"I don't know."
"Did someone contact you?"
She hesitated.
Then nodded.
"Messages."
Ha-eun pulled out her phone.
She showed him.
He read both messages.
His eyes stayed on the screen for a few seconds.
Then he looked at her.
"Why didn't you tell me?"
"I didn't want to ruin your day."
"Ha-eun."
She looked up.
"You don't ruin my day."
Her eyes softened.
"I know."
"Then tell me when something happens."
"I was going to."
"When?"
She thought about it.
"...Eventually."
"That's not a time."
She almost smiled.
Almost.
Then the elevator doors opened.
They stepped out.
Ha-Kim handed her phone back.
"Don't respond."
"I wasn't going to."
"Don't go anywhere alone."
"I can handle myself."
"I know."
He paused.
"That's not the point."
Ha-eun looked at him.
For a moment, neither moved.
Then she quietly nodded.
"Okay."
───
That evening, Ha-Kim stayed at the office.
The building was almost empty.
He sat alone in front of his computer.
The messages were still on his mind.
He pulled up the company's security system.
Cameras.
Entry records.
Parking access.
External footage.
He searched the last three days.
Nothing.
Then he widened the search.
One week.
Nothing.
Two weeks.
Still nothing.
He leaned back.
The black figure appeared in his mind again.
Rain.
Ha-eun.
Too late.
He closed his eyes.
Then opened them.
"Not again."
He started another search.
This time, he checked the cameras around Ha-eun's apartment building.
Someone had been there.
Three nights ago.
A dark vehicle had stopped across the street for six minutes.
The license plate was partially blocked.
Ha-Kim enlarged the image.
The driver's face was hidden.
But the passenger stepped out.
Ha-Kim froze.
The man's face was visible for less than two seconds.
He didn't recognize him.
But something about him felt familiar.
Not personally.
From somewhere else.
Ha-Kim opened another file.
Old records.
Archived information.
He searched the little he knew about Ha-eun's past.
Most of it had been difficult to trace.
Then one result appeared.
A name.
A gang.
A case from years ago.
Ha-Kim's eyes narrowed.
The same organization connected to the people who had once controlled Ha-eun.
He looked at the timestamp.
The file had been accessed recently.
Very recently.
Someone else had been searching too.
Ha-Kim stared at the screen.
His phone vibrated.
Ha-eun.
Are you coming home?
He looked at the message for a long moment.
Then typed.
Yeah.
Another message arrived.
Can you bring food? I'm hungry.
For the first time all day, his expression softened.
What do you want?
Three dots appeared.
Then disappeared.
Then appeared again.
Anything. But preferably something good.
He almost smiled.
That's not specific.
Then surprise me.
Ha-Kim stood.
He shut down the computer.
The file remained in the back of his mind.
Someone was looking for Ha-eun.
Someone had already found her.
And whatever he had seen in that vision...
It wasn't far away anymore.
───
When Ha-Kim got home, Ha-eun was waiting by the door.
She looked tired.
But when she saw the food in his hands, her expression immediately brightened.
"You actually brought food."
"You asked."
"I didn't think you'd remember."
"I remember most things."
She took the bag from him.
The small smile on her face made something in his chest loosen.
She turned toward the kitchen.
Then stopped.
"Ha-Kim?"
"Yeah?"
She looked back.
"Thank you."
He nodded.
"You're welcome."
She smiled again.
Then walked into the kitchen.
For a few seconds, Ha-Kim simply watched her.
The apartment was warm.
Quiet.
Normal.
Exactly what he had wanted.
He looked toward the window.
Outside, the street was empty.
But somewhere beyond the glass...
Something was moving.
And this time, Ha-Kim knew it wasn't just a vision.
Something was coming.
