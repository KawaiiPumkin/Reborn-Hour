# The Smile

A few days passed.

Nothing dramatic happened.

Ha-eun still made mistakes.

Ha-Kim still corrected her.

She still complained about how terrible he was at explaining things.

And he still told her she talked too much.

But something had changed.

She wasn't as nervous anymore.

───

On Saturday afternoon, Ha-eun stood in front of a clothing store.

She stared through the glass.

A simple black jacket hung near the entrance.

She liked it.

It wasn't anything special.

Just a jacket.

She walked inside.

The store was warm and smelled faintly like perfume.

Music played quietly from the speakers.

Ha-eun walked between the clothes racks, touching a few shirts as she passed.

She found the jacket again.

She picked it up.

Then looked at the price.

“...Oh.”

She immediately put it back.

She looked around.

There were cheaper clothes.

She found a plain shirt.

Checked the price.

Still expensive.

She sighed.

“Maybe next month.”

She walked toward the exit.

Before leaving, she looked back at the jacket.

Then left.

───

Her apartment was quiet when she returned.

Too quiet.

Ha-eun dropped her bag beside the bed.

The room was small.

A bed.

A tiny table.

A chair.

A few clothes hanging near the wall.

That was basically everything.

She opened the refrigerator.

There wasn't much inside.

She stared for a few seconds.

“Yeah.”

She closed it.

She opened the cabinet instead.

Instant noodles.

Again.

She looked at them.

Then at the empty refrigerator.

“I'm getting sick of these.”

She took one anyway.

A few minutes later, she sat on the floor with the bowl in her hands.

Steam rose into her face.

She took a bite.

Then another.

She looked around her apartment.

It wasn't nice.

The walls were old.

The paint near the window had started peeling.

The table had a small crack running across one side.

But it was hers.

For now.

Ha-eun looked at the noodles.

“I'm eating something better next week.”

She paused.

“Definitely.”

She continued eating.

───

Across Seoul, Ha-Kim's apartment was almost completely silent.

He sat at his desk.

His laptop was open.

Several reports were displayed across the screen.

He had been working for almost two hours.

A cup of coffee sat beside him.

Untouched.

He finally leaned back.

His eyes felt tired.

He closed the laptop.

The room became quiet.

No television.

No music.

Nothing.

Ha-Kim stood up and walked toward the window.

Seoul stretched out beneath him.

Thousands of lights.

Cars moving through the streets.

People walking.

Everyone going somewhere.

He watched them for a while.

Then he looked away.

He had work tomorrow.

He always had work tomorrow.

Ha-Kim returned to his desk.

He sat down.

For a moment, he stared at his hands.

Then closed his eyes.

The room remained silent.

He focused.

For a brief moment, everything around him disappeared.

Then he opened his eyes.

Nothing had changed.

He looked at the clock.

One hour.

That was all he needed.

Sometimes it was enough.

Sometimes it wasn't.

He rubbed his eyes.

A dull pain had already started behind them.

He ignored it.

Then opened his laptop again.

───

Monday came quickly.

Ha-eun entered Haetae Cyber with a different feeling than before.

She still felt nervous.

But not as much.

She knew where the elevators were now.

She knew where the cafeteria was.

She knew which printer always got stuck.

And, unfortunately, she knew where Ha-Kim's desk was.

“Morning.”

Ha-Kim looked up.

“Morning.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“You sound excited.”

“I am.”

“You don't look excited.”

“I don't look excited often.”

“That sounds depressing.”

“It isn't.”

She laughed quietly.

Then walked toward her desk.

Ha-Kim watched her for a second.

She was different.

A little.

She didn't walk around like she was afraid of getting something wrong anymore.

She still made mistakes.

But now she asked questions instead of panicking.

That was progress.

───

Around ten, a woman walked over to Ha-eun's desk.

“Hey.”

Ha-eun looked up.

“Hi.”

“You're Ha-eun, right?”

“Yeah.”

The woman smiled.

“I'm Ji-woo.”

Ha-eun smiled back.

“Nice to meet you.”

“I've seen you around.”

“Really?”

“Yeah. You're always with Ha-Kim.”

Ha-eun looked toward him.

He was working.

“Unfortunately.”

Ji-woo laughed.

“Is he really that bad?”

“He's weird.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun smiled.

“You know?”

“Everyone knows.”

Ha-eun tried not to laugh.

She failed.

A small laugh escaped.

Ha-Kim looked over.

“What?”

Ha-eun immediately shook her head.

“Nothing.”

Ji-woo covered her mouth.

“Nothing.”

Ha-Kim stared at both of them.

“Okay.”

He went back to work.

The second he looked away, Ji-woo whispered,

“He definitely knows.”

Ha-eun whispered back,

“Probably.”

They both smiled.

───

By lunch, Ha-eun and Ji-woo were sitting together.

Ji-woo talked more than Ha-eun expected.

She talked about the company.

The managers.

The cafeteria.

People Ha-eun hadn't even met yet.

Ha-eun mostly listened.

“So,” Ji-woo said, “how long have you been training with Ha-Kim?”

“Almost a week.”

“And you're still alive?”

Ha-eun laughed.

“Barely.”

Ji-woo smiled.

“He's scary at first.”

“He still is.”

“He once stared at me for ten seconds because I moved his documents.”

“What did you do?”

“I moved them back.”

Ha-eun laughed.

“You didn't apologize?”

“No.”

“Brave.”

“I was scared.”

They both laughed.

Across the cafeteria, Ha-Kim looked over.

He saw Ha-eun laughing.

Not the small smile she usually gave.

A real laugh.

He looked away.

A few seconds later, the corner of his mouth lifted.

Barely.

───

After lunch, something happened.

Ji-woo accidentally printed fifty copies of the same document.

The printer kept going.

One.

Two.

Three.

Ten.

Twenty.

“Wait.”

Ji-woo stared at it.

“Why is it still printing?”

Ha-eun walked over.

“Did you tell it to print fifty?”

Ji-woo froze.

“...Maybe.”

Ha-eun looked at the printer.

Then at Ji-woo.

“You're worse than me.”

“I know.”

Ha-eun laughed.

Ji-woo quickly tried to stop the printer.

It didn't stop.

Ha-eun pressed another button.

Nothing.

“Why isn't it stopping?”

“I don't know!”

They both stared at it.

Ha-Kim walked over.

He looked at the printer.

Then at them.

Then at the stack of papers.

“What did you do?”

Ji-woo pointed at Ha-eun.

“She did it.”

Ha-eun's mouth dropped open.

“What?!”

“You were standing next to it.”

“That doesn't mean I did it!”

Ha-Kim looked between them.

Neither said anything.

Then he pressed one button.

The printer stopped.

Silence.

Ji-woo looked embarrassed.

Ha-eun looked at her.

“You're buying lunch tomorrow.”

“Why?”

“Because you almost killed the printer.”

Ha-Kim looked at them.

“You can't kill a printer.”

Ji-woo pointed at him.

“See? Even he thinks you're wrong.”

Ha-eun stared at Ha-Kim.

“You're supposed to be on my side.”

“I didn't take a side.”

“You absolutely did.”

Ha-Kim's expression remained serious.

Then a quiet laugh escaped him.

Both girls stopped.

Ha-Kim seemed to realize it too.

He looked away.

Ji-woo stared.

Ha-eun stared.

Then Ji-woo slowly smiled.

“Did he just laugh?”

Ha-Kim looked at her.

“No.”

“You did.”

“No.”

Ha-eun covered her mouth.

She started laughing again.

Ha-Kim looked at her.

And this time, he smiled too.

───

Later that afternoon, Seo-jun walked into the office.

He stopped when he saw Ha-Kim.

Then looked at Ha-eun.

Then Ji-woo.

They were talking about something.

Ha-Kim was listening.

Not just pretending to listen.

Actually listening.

Seo-jun stood there for a moment.

Ha-Kim noticed him.

“What?”

Seo-jun smiled.

“Nothing.”

Ha-Kim narrowed his eyes.

“Then why are you staring?”

“I wasn't.”

“You were.”

Seo-jun walked closer.

“Since when do you laugh?”

Ha-Kim's expression immediately returned to normal.

“I don't.”

“You just did.”

“No.”

“I heard you.”

“You're imagining things.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“Sure.”

Ha-Kim returned to his computer.

Seo-jun looked toward Ha-eun.

She was talking to Ji-woo again.

She noticed him looking.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Ha-eun looked confused.

Seo-jun smiled.

“Keep doing whatever you're doing.”

She blinked.

“...What?”

But Seo-jun had already walked away.

Ha-Kim watched him leave.

He knew exactly what Seo-jun meant.

───

Near the end of the day, Ha-eun sat at her desk.

She looked at her work.

Then at Ji-woo.

Then at Ha-Kim.

She smiled.

A few weeks ago, she had walked into this building terrified.

She thought everyone would notice how inexperienced she was.

How little she knew.

How different she was.

But now...

She had someone to talk to.

Someone who taught her.

People who knew her name.

It wasn't much.

But it was something.

And for once, something felt normal.

───

The office slowly became quieter.

People packed their things and left.

Ha-eun stood up.

“See you tomorrow.”

Ji-woo waved.

“See you.”

Ha-eun looked at Ha-Kim.

“Bye.”

He looked up.

“See you.”

She smiled and left.

Ha-Kim watched the elevator doors close.

Then returned to his screen.

Seo-jun was still standing nearby.

“You're different.”

Ha-Kim didn't look at him.

“No.”

“You are.”

“No.”

Seo-jun smiled.

“You used to hate when people stayed around you.”

“I still do.”

“Then why don't you?”

Ha-Kim finally looked at him.

There was a pause.

“I don't know.”

Seo-jun's smile faded slightly.

He looked toward the elevator.

Then back at Ha-Kim.

For years, he had known Ha-Kim as someone who kept everything inside.

Someone who rarely smiled.

Someone who seemed like he had forgotten what it was like to simply enjoy being around people.

But lately...

That was changing.

Slowly.

Almost too slowly to notice.

Seo-jun smiled again.

“It's good.”

Ha-Kim looked back at his screen.

“What's good?”

Seo-jun didn't answer.

He just walked away.

Ha-Kim watched him leave.

Then looked toward the elevator again.

For a moment, he remembered Ha-eun laughing.

He didn't know why.

But he smiled.

A real one.

Small.

Almost impossible to notice.

But it was there.
