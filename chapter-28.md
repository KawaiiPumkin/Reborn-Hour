Chapter 28 — The Storm
The rain started before noon.
At first, it was light.
By three, the windows of Haetae were covered in small streams of water.
Ha-eun sat at her desk, staring at her phone.
One new message.
Unknown number.
She had gotten used to those.
Different numbers.
Same people.
Usually threats.
Usually demands.
This one was different.
One last payment.
Ha-eun frowned.
Another message appeared.
Bring it tonight and we're done.
She stared at the words.
Then—
You won't hear from us again.
Her chest tightened.
For several seconds, she didn't move.
Done.
She read the messages again.
Once.
Twice.
Three times.
Her first instinct was not to believe them.
She knew these people.
She knew what promises from them were worth.
But another message arrived.
An amount.
Smaller than before.
Then an address.
Come alone. Pay what you owe. Then you're free.
Ha-eun swallowed.
She should tell Ha-Kim.
The thought came immediately.
He had asked her to tell him if they contacted her again.
And normally, she would.
But she looked across the office.
Ha-Kim sat at his desk, glasses slightly lower on his nose as he stared at his monitor.
He looked exhausted.
He had barely been sleeping.
She noticed every time he thought she didn't.
The headaches.
The moments when he stopped talking and pressed two fingers against his temple.
The way he sometimes stared through the window like he was waiting for something.
And lately, he'd been worrying about her constantly.
Walking closer to her outside.
Looking behind them.
Checking whether she had gotten home even though they lived in the same apartment now.
Ha-eun looked back at the message.
One last payment.
Maybe this really could be the end.
One final payment.
Then no more strange numbers.
No more looking behind her.
No more bringing her old life into the one she had now.
Her phone vibrated.
Tonight.
Ha-eun slowly locked the screen.
She wouldn't tell him.
Not yet.
She would finish it herself.
Then she'd come home.
And tell him it was finally over.
───
At lunch, Ha-Kim stole from her plate.
Ha-eun stared at his chopsticks.
"Did you seriously just take that?"
"You weren't eating it."
"I was saving it."
"For what?"
"The end."
"Why?"
"Because it was the best one."
Ha-Kim looked at the piece between his chopsticks.
Then at her.
Ha-eun narrowed her eyes.
"Don't."
He ate it.
Her mouth fell open.
"Ha-Kim."
"It was good."
"I hate you."
"No, you don't."
She paused.
Her face warmed slightly.
"...That's not the point."
Ha-Kim almost smiled.
Ha-eun immediately stole something from his plate.
"Then I'm taking this."
"Okay."
She frowned.
"You're supposed to complain."
"Why?"
"Because I stole it."
"You wanted it."
"You're ruining this."
"Sorry."
"You don't sound sorry."
"I'm not."
Ha-eun ate it anyway.
A small smile stayed on her face.
For a few minutes, she forgot about the message.
Forgot about the money.
Forgot about tonight.
It was just lunch.
Just Ha-Kim.
Just another ordinary day.
She wanted more days like this.
That was why she had to end it.
───
Near the end of work, Ha-eun checked her bank account.
The amount would hurt.
A lot.
But she could manage.
Barely.
She transferred some money between accounts and stared at what remained.
Not much.
Her phone vibrated.
8:00. Don't be late.
She locked it quickly.
"What's that?"
Ha-eun nearly dropped her phone.
Ha-Kim stood beside her desk.
"Nothing."
"You jumped."
"You appeared out of nowhere."
"I walked here."
"Quietly."
"I wasn't trying to."
She put her phone into her bag.
Ha-Kim watched her.
"You're acting weird."
"I'm always weird."
"Different weird."
Ha-eun looked up at him.
He wasn't suspicious.
Not yet.
Just observant.
Which was worse.
She stood.
"Actually..."
"What?"
"I need to go somewhere after work."
"Where?"
"Somewhere."
"Very informative."
She smiled.
"I'm getting something."
"What?"
"A surprise."
Ha-Kim stared at her.
"For who?"
"You."
His expression immediately became more confused.
"Why?"
Ha-eun laughed softly.
"Why do you sound scared?"
"I don't like surprises."
"You'll like mine."
"What is it?"
"If I tell you, then it isn't a surprise."
"I don't need a surprise."
"I know."
"Then don't get one."
"No."
"Ha-eun."
She zipped her bag.
"No."
Ha-Kim sighed.
"How long?"
"Not long."
"Want me to come?"
"No!"
The answer came a little too quickly.
Ha-Kim raised an eyebrow.
Ha-eun immediately tried to recover.
"Because then you'll see it."
"I can wait outside."
"You'll figure it out."
"I won't."
"You absolutely will."
Ha-Kim considered that.
"...Probably."
"Exactly."
She smiled.
"Go home."
"You're ordering me around now?"
"Yes."
"Interesting."
"Get used to it."
He looked at her for another moment.
Something about her felt off.
But she was smiling.
And if she genuinely wanted to surprise him...
He wasn't going to ruin it.
"Fine."
Ha-eun brightened.
"But take an umbrella."
"I have one."
"The broken one?"
"It's not broken."
"It turned inside out three times."
"Why do you keep remembering that?"
"Because it happened three times."
Ha-eun sighed.
"Fine."
───
They left Haetae together.
Rain covered the streets.
Cars passed through shallow puddles, headlights reflecting across the wet road.
Ha-Kim held the umbrella above both of them.
Ha-eun walked close beside him.
Her hand found his.
Neither mentioned it.
They simply kept walking.
For a while, everything felt normal.
Then they reached the place where they had to separate.
Ha-eun stopped.
"Okay."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"Okay?"
"You go that way."
"And you?"
"That way."
She pointed.
Ha-Kim looked down the street.
"How far?"
"Not far."
"I can walk you."
"No."
"Ha-eun."
"You'll ruin it."
"The mysterious surprise."
"Exactly."
He sighed.
"Fine."
She smiled.
Then Ha-Kim handed her the umbrella.
Ha-eun blinked.
"What about you?"
"I'll buy another one."
"You'll get wet."
"I'll survive."
She looked at the umbrella.
Then at him.
A small warmth entered her expression.
"Thanks."
"Call me if you're going to be late."
"I will."
"And don't wander around for three hours choosing one lamp again."
Her mouth opened.
"That lamp was important."
"It looks like the other one."
"It does not."
"It does."
"You're impossible."
"So I've heard."
Ha-eun took a few steps backward.
Then stopped.
"Ha-Kim."
"What?"
For a second, she just looked at him.
She almost told him.
The messages.
The payment.
Where she was really going.
She almost did.
But tonight was supposed to end all of it.
One last time.
Then she'd never have to drag him into that part of her life again.
Her expression softened.
"Don't eat without me."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"You're coming back for dinner?"
"Obviously."
"What do you want?"
"I'll decide later."
"That's helpful."
"I know."
She smiled.
"I'll see you at home."
"Okay."
Ha-eun turned.
Ha-Kim watched her disappear into the crowd.
He stood there a few seconds longer than necessary.
Then turned toward home.
───
The address was farther than Ha-eun expected.
By the time she arrived, the busy streets were behind her.
The area was quieter.
Older buildings.
Closed shops.
A narrow road shining beneath the rain.
Ha-eun slowed.
Her smile was gone.
She checked the address again.
Correct.
Her phone vibrated.
Keep walking.
Ha-eun looked around.
No one.
She typed.
I'm here.
The response came immediately.
End of the street.
Ha-eun's fingers tightened around her phone.
Something felt wrong.
She almost called Ha-Kim.
Her thumb hovered over his name.
Then she stopped.
One payment.
That was all.
She could do this.
She had survived worse.
Ha-eun put the phone away.
And kept walking.
───
Ha-Kim arrived home alone.
He removed his wet coat and hung it near the entrance.
The apartment was quiet.
Too quiet.
He noticed that more now.
Before Ha-eun, silence had been normal.
Comfortable.
Now it just meant she wasn't there.
Ha-Kim looked around.
A blanket she had left on the sofa.
Her slippers near the bedroom.
A small decoration she'd insisted on buying sitting near the television.
The apartment didn't look like his anymore.
It looked like theirs.
He checked the time.
7:38.
Ha-eun would probably be back soon.
He went into the kitchen.
Opened the refrigerator.
There was almost nothing useful.
He stared at it.
"Great."
He took out his phone.
What do you want for dinner?
Delivered.
No response.
He started preparing something simple anyway.
Five minutes passed.
His phone vibrated.
Ha-Kim checked it.
Not Ha-eun.
Seo-jun.
Are you alive
Ha-Kim typed back.
Unfortunately.
The reply came immediately.
bro 😭
Ha-Kim put his phone down.
Then stopped.
A strange pressure had started behind his eyes.
He touched his temple.
It disappeared.
He frowned.
A few seconds passed.
Then—
Pain.
Sharp.
Sudden.
Ha-Kim grabbed the counter.
His vision blurred.
"Ah..."
He closed his eyes.
For half a second—
Rain.
A dark street.
Then the kitchen returned.
Ha-Kim froze.
His breathing slowed.
"No."
Another flash.
Ha-eun.
Standing beneath a streetlight.
The apartment returned.
Ha-Kim grabbed his phone.
He called her.
Ring.
Ring.
No answer.
"Come on."
Again.
Nothing.
Ha-Kim's heartbeat quickened.
He called a third time.
Still nothing.
Then the pain exploded.
His phone slipped from his hand.
Ha-Kim caught himself against the counter.
The kitchen disappeared.
───
Rain.
Heavy rain.
A narrow street.
Ha-Kim couldn't move.
Couldn't speak.
Couldn't interfere.
He knew this feeling.
A future vision.
Not the strange shadows.
Not the impossible warnings.
His real ability.
Clear.
Precise.
One hour.
Ahead of him stood Ha-eun.
Her back was turned.
A man stood several meters away.
Ha-Kim couldn't see himself.
Only the familiar indistinct shadow where his future body should have been.
Then—
01:00:00
Ha-Kim's breath stopped.
The countdown moved.
00:59:59
The man stepped closer to Ha-eun.
She backed away.
Ha-Kim watched.
Helpless.
The man's hand moved inside his jacket.
Something reflected beneath the streetlight.
Metal.
A blade.
Ha-Kim's blood went cold.
The payment.
The surprise.
She hadn't gone shopping.
She had gone to them.
Alone.
00:59:51
Ha-eun turned to run.
The man grabbed her.
And Ha-Kim finally understood what all those broken visions had been trying to show him.
They weren't warnings about something far away.
They were warnings about tonight.
00:59:46
The blade rose.
Ha-Kim stared at Ha-eun.
And for the first time since his ability appeared...
he didn't care what changing the future would do to him.
