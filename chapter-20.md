Chapter 20 — The Mysterious CEO
Han Ji-hoon had worked at Haetae Cyber long enough to know when something didn't make sense.
Usually, he ignored it.
Every company had strange things.
Strange rules.
Strange employees.
Haetae had more than most.
Especially its CEO.
Nobody had seen him.
Nobody knew where his office was.
Nobody knew his age.
Every important meeting happened through a screen.
Every message came through encrypted channels.
His voice was filtered.
Even senior employees who had worked at Haetae for years knew almost nothing about him.
Most people had stopped questioning it.
Ji-hoon hadn't.
───
"Meeting at three."
Ha-Kim looked up from his laptop.
Ji-hoon stood beside his desk.
"What meeting?"
"Security division."
Ha-Kim glanced at the clock.
2:17 PM.
"Okay."
Ji-hoon turned to leave.
Then stopped.
"The CEO will be attending."
Ha-Kim's fingers paused above the keyboard.
Only for a moment.
"Okay."
Ji-hoon watched him.
Nothing else changed.
Ha-Kim returned to his work.
Ji-hoon walked away.
At 2:46 PM, Ji-hoon passed Ha-Kim's desk again.
Empty.
He slowed down.
Coffee still there.
Laptop locked.
Chair pushed in.
Ji-hoon looked around.
No Ha-Kim.
He continued walking.
At 2:58, employees entered the conference room.
The lights dimmed.
A large screen activated.
Static appeared briefly.
Then the familiar filtered voice filled the room.
"Good afternoon."
Everyone became quiet.
Ji-hoon sat near the front.
The meeting began.
Quarterly security performance.
New infrastructure.
An upcoming internal audit.
Nothing unusual.
Except Ji-hoon found himself thinking about the empty desk outside.
He glanced toward the screen.
A dark figure sat behind heavy distortion.
Impossible to identify.
"Ji-hoon."
His attention snapped back.
"Yes, sir."
"Your report."
Ji-hoon opened the document.
He explained the recent system changes.
The CEO listened.
Then asked two questions.
Both specific.
Very specific.
Questions about weaknesses Ji-hoon had only noticed that morning.
He answered them.
"Understood."
The CEO moved on.
Ji-hoon stared at the screen.
Something bothered him.
He couldn't explain what.
───
The meeting ended at 3:41.
Employees returned to their desks.
Ji-hoon walked through the office.
Ha-Kim was sitting at his desk.
Working.
Ji-hoon stopped.
Ha-Kim looked up.
"What?"
"Where were you?"
"Bathroom."
Ji-hoon stared at him.
"For almost an hour?"
Ha-Kim's expression didn't change.
"No."
"Your desk was empty before the meeting."
"I got coffee."
Ji-hoon glanced toward the cup.
"Then bathroom."
"Yes."
"And then?"
Ha-Kim looked at him.
"Do you need my complete schedule?"
Ji-hoon was silent.
Ha-Kim waited.
"No."
"Okay."
Ji-hoon walked away.
Ha-Kim watched him for several seconds.
Then looked back at his laptop.
A small encrypted notification sat in the corner.
CEO COMMUNICATION CHANNEL — SESSION CLOSED
He deleted it.
───
Ha-eun rolled her chair toward him.
"What was that?"
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"What?"
"Ji-hoon."
"Nothing."
"He looked like he was interrogating you."
"He asks questions."
"He asked where you went to the bathroom."
"Apparently."
Ha-eun tried not to smile.
"Suspicious."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"Of what?"
"I don't know."
She leaned closer.
"Maybe you're secretly hiding something."
Ha-Kim stared at her.
Ha-eun smiled.
"That was a joke."
"I know."
"You looked guilty."
"I didn't."
"You did."
Ha-Kim returned to his laptop.
Ha-eun watched him for another second.
Then rolled back toward her desk.
Ha-Kim adjusted his glasses.
His expression remained calm.
But his attention wasn't on the screen anymore.
Ji-hoon was observant.
That could become a problem.
───
It didn't happen again the next day.
Or the day after.
Ha-Kim made sure of that.
If a CEO meeting was scheduled, he disappeared earlier.
Sometimes he remained at his desk while prerecorded announcements played.
Other times he worked remotely.
He changed small parts of his routine.
Nothing dramatic.
Nothing that would look like he was trying to hide something.
For a while, Ji-hoon stopped asking questions.
Ha-Kim assumed that was enough.
It wasn't.
───
Several evenings later, Ji-hoon walked through Haetae's underground parking garage.
Most employees had already gone home.
His footsteps echoed through the concrete space.
He reached his car.
Then stopped.
Someone was standing several rows away.
Black clothes.
Glasses.
Messy dark hair.
Ha-Kim.
He was alone.
Ji-hoon frowned.
"Ha-Kim?"
Ha-Kim turned.
Neither spoke for a moment.
"You're still here."
Ha-Kim looked at him.
"So are you."
"I had work."
"Same."
Ji-hoon looked around.
There were few cars left.
Ha-Kim wasn't carrying anything.
"Where's your car?"
Ha-Kim paused.
"I don't drive to work."
"Then why are you in the parking garage?"
Silence.
Ha-Kim pointed behind Ji-hoon.
"Elevator."
Ji-hoon turned.
The employee elevator was behind him.
He looked back.
Ha-Kim had already started walking toward it.
Ji-hoon followed.
They entered together.
The doors closed.
Ji-hoon pressed the lobby button.
Ha-Kim said nothing.
Ji-hoon watched the floor numbers change.
"You know a lot about this company."
Ha-Kim glanced at him.
"I work here."
"More than most people who work here."
Ha-Kim adjusted his glasses.
"Is that a problem?"
"No."
The elevator continued upward.
Ji-hoon looked at him.
"Just interesting."
The doors opened.
Ha-Kim stepped out.
"Good night."
Ji-hoon remained inside.
"Good night."
The doors closed.
Ji-hoon stared at his reflection.
Something didn't fit.
He just didn't know what yet.
───
Ha-Kim walked home.
Cold air brushed against his face.
He kept his hands inside his pockets.
Ji-hoon's questions weren't dangerous yet.
Curiosity wasn't evidence.
Still...
He would need to be more careful.
Ha-Kim crossed the street.
His vision blurred.
He stopped.
A sharp pressure appeared behind his eyes.
"No."
He knew that feeling.
But he hadn't activated anything.
The street disappeared.
───
Darkness.
Ha-Kim couldn't feel the ground beneath him.
There was no sky.
No buildings.
No clock.
No sixty-minute limit.
Only shadows.
Dozens of them.
Standing far away.
Motionless.
Ha-Kim tried to move.
Nothing happened.
Then he saw it.
The smaller shadow.
The familiar one.
His chest tightened.
Ha-eun.
Or something shaped like her.
She stood farther away than before.
"Ha-eun?"
No response.
The shadows around her moved.
Not toward her.
Away from something.
Ha-Kim looked past them.
There.
Something enormous stood in the distance.
The same black shape.
But this time...
It was closer.
Ha-Kim tried to focus.
The darkness shifted.
For a fraction of a second, something appeared behind the shadows.
A street.
Rain.
Red light reflected across wet pavement.
Then—
A sound.
Metal scraping against concrete.
Ha-Kim's eyes widened.
The smaller shadow turned.
Not toward the thing behind her.
Toward him.
A hand reached forward.
The darkness moved.
Closer.
Closer.
Closer.
Ha-Kim tried to reach her.
His body wouldn't move.
Then he heard something.
A voice.
Too distorted to recognize.
"...found..."
Ha-Kim froze.
"What?"
The shadow behind Ha-eun moved.
"...you."
Everything shattered.
───
Ha-Kim opened his eyes.
He was standing beside a building.
His hand pressed against the wall.
His breathing was heavier than usual.
Cars moved along the road.
People passed him without noticing.
He checked the time.
8:14 PM.
Then again.
8:14.
No missing hour.
No future.
No explanation.
Ha-Kim removed his glasses and rubbed his eyes.
The headache remained.
"Found you..."
He looked toward the street.
Who?
Him?
Ha-eun?
Someone else?
He didn't know.
That was the problem.
His normal ability gave answers.
This gave him questions.
Ha-Kim put his glasses back on.
Then continued home.
───
Rain started later that night.
At first, quietly.
Then harder.
By eleven, it covered the windows.
Thunder rolled somewhere above Seoul.
Ha-Kim sat on his couch with his laptop.
Ha-eun was beside him.
She had come over for dinner hours earlier.
Neither had noticed how late it had gotten.
A flash illuminated the apartment.
A few seconds later—
BOOM.
Ha-eun jumped.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
She immediately straightened.
"I didn't jump."
"You did."
"No."
Another flash.
Thunder followed.
Ha-eun flinched again.
Ha-Kim stared at her.
"Don't."
"I didn't say anything."
"You were going to."
"I wasn't."
"You were thinking it."
Ha-Kim closed his laptop.
"I can't control thunder."
"I know."
Rain hammered against the window.
Ha-eun looked toward it.
Then toward the clock.
11:26 PM.
"I should probably go."
Ha-Kim nodded.
"Probably."
She didn't move.
Ha-Kim waited.
Ha-eun remained on the couch.
"You aren't leaving."
"I know."
"You live across the hall."
"I know."
"That's about six steps."
Ha-eun looked at him.
"Can I stay here tonight?"
Ha-Kim stopped.
Her voice had become quieter.
Another roll of thunder filled the apartment.
"You want to stay?"
She nodded.
"Yeah."
Ha-Kim looked at her for a moment.
Then nodded.
"Okay."
Her shoulders relaxed.
"Okay."
───
Ha-Kim gave her one of his shirts to sleep in.
It was far too large.
When Ha-eun came out of the bathroom, the sleeves covered part of her hands.
Ha-Kim looked at her.
She looked down at herself.
"Don't."
"I didn't say anything."
"You looked."
"I have eyes."
Ha-eun narrowed hers.
"You're annoying."
"So I've heard."
She walked toward the bed.
Then stopped.
Ha-Kim was still standing.
"Aren't you sleeping?"
"I can use the couch."
Ha-eun stared at him.
"Why?"
He looked at the bed.
Then at her.
Ha-eun's face became warm.
"Oh."
Neither spoke.
Then she moved slightly to one side.
"You can sleep here."
Ha-Kim hesitated.
"Are you sure?"
"Yes."
He nodded.
"Okay."
───
The room went dark.
Rain continued outside.
Ha-eun lay facing away from him.
Ha-Kim stared at the ceiling.
There was enough space between them for another person.
Five minutes passed.
Then ten.
Thunder rolled again.
Ha-eun moved closer.
Ha-Kim looked toward her.
She remained still.
Another few minutes.
She moved closer again.
"Ha-eun."
"What?"
"You're moving."
"No, I'm not."
"You are."
Silence.
Then:
"It's cold."
"You have a blanket."
"It's still cold."
Ha-Kim almost smiled.
Ha-eun turned toward him.
Their faces were close.
Much closer than either expected.
Neither moved.
Ha-eun looked at his eyes.
Then his lips.
Then back at his eyes.
Ha-Kim noticed.
"You're staring."
"So are you."
He couldn't argue with that.
Ha-eun moved closer.
Their lips met.
A small kiss.
Then another.
Ha-Kim's hand gently touched her cheek.
Ha-eun leaned into him.
The rain grew louder.
She kissed him again, more confidently this time.
When he pulled back slightly, she followed him.
Ha-Kim smiled.
"I remember."
Ha-eun blinked.
"What?"
"Don't assume."
Her entire face turned red.
"You remembered that?"
"Yes."
"Forget it."
"No."
She hid her face against his chest.
Ha-Kim quietly laughed.
"Stop."
"I'm not doing anything."
"You're laughing."
"A little."
Ha-eun looked up.
Then kissed him again just to shut him up.
It worked.
For a while.
The kisses became slower.
Longer.
The nervousness between them gradually disappeared.
Eventually Ha-Kim pulled back.
His forehead rested against hers.
"Are you okay?"
Ha-eun nodded.
"Yeah."
He waited.
She understood why.
Her expression softened.
"I want to be close to you."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"Are you sure?"
She nodded again.
"I'm sure."
He kissed her forehead.
Then her lips.
Outside, thunder rolled across the city.
Inside, neither of them hurried.
For once, Ha-Kim wasn't thinking about what would happen an hour from now.
There was only her.
And eventually, the room became quiet.
───
Much later, the storm had weakened.
Rain still tapped softly against the window.
Ha-Kim lay awake.
Ha-eun slept beside him beneath the blanket.
Her hair was messy.
Her face was peaceful.
One hand rested against his chest.
Ha-Kim looked at the ceiling.
The vision returned to his thoughts.
The shadows.
The rain.
The enormous shape.
That voice.
Found you.
He could activate his ability.
Look one hour ahead.
Make sure nothing happened tonight.
The thought remained for several seconds.
Then Ha-eun moved in her sleep.
Her hand slipped from his chest.
Without waking, she reached for him again.
Her fingers found his.
Ha-Kim looked down.
She held his hand loosely.
Still asleep.
His expression softened.
He didn't activate anything.
He didn't look ahead.
He didn't need to.
Not tonight.
Ha-Kim closed his eyes.
Outside, the last thunder faded somewhere beyond Seoul.
Whatever was coming could wait until tomorrow.
Tonight, he stayed in the present.
