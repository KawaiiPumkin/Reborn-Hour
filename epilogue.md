# Epilogue — The Future
Ten years passed.
Faster than anyone expected.
The city changed.
Buildings that once stood for years disappeared and were replaced by something newer.
Technology changed.
People changed.
Some people left.
Some stayed.
Some relationships ended.
Others began.
And somewhere inside a quiet hospital room, Ha-Kim remained asleep.
Until one morning—
his fingers moved.
Just slightly.
The nurse checking the monitors froze.
She looked at his hand.
Then at his face.
His eyelids trembled.
The monitor changed.
"Doctor."
Footsteps quickly approached.
Ha-Kim's eyes slowly opened.
Light filled his vision.
For several seconds, he couldn't understand anything.
The ceiling.
The machines.
The unfamiliar room.
His body felt heavy.
His throat was dry.
He tried to speak.
Nothing came out.
Then someone entered the room.
A woman.
She stopped the moment she saw him awake.
Ha-Kim stared at her.
He didn't recognize her at first.
Then—
he did.
His eyes widened.
"...Ha-eun?"
She covered her mouth.
Tears immediately filled her eyes.
She hadn't changed completely.
But ten years had passed.
Her hair was longer.
Her face was older.
She wasn't the same twenty-two-year-old girl who had sat beside his hospital bed.
But her eyes were the same.
Ha-eun walked toward him slowly.
"You woke up."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
His voice was barely there.
"How long?"
She didn't answer immediately.
She sat beside him.
Held his hand.
Then whispered—
"Ten years."
Ha-Kim stared at her.
Ten years.
He looked toward the window.
The world outside was unfamiliar.
He had slept through an entire decade.
Ha-eun squeezed his hand.
"I waited."
His eyes returned to her.
"You..."
She smiled through her tears.
"I told you I would."
Ha-Kim looked down at their hands.
For some reason, that hurt more than anything.
"I'm sorry."
Ha-eun shook her head.
"No."
"I left you alone."
"You didn't leave."
"I was asleep."
"I know."
She smiled.
"You were still here."
Ha-Kim looked at her for a long time.
Then he asked quietly—
"Why did you stay?"
Ha-eun's expression softened.
"Because I loved you."
He looked away.
She continued.
"And because you told me once that I didn't have to become someone else to deserve a normal life."
Ha-Kim swallowed.
"You remembered that?"
"I remember everything."
Silence settled between them.
A comfortable one.
The kind they used to share at home.
Ha-Kim looked toward the window again.
Then he noticed something.
He could see clearly.
The buildings outside.
The trees.
The rain beginning to fall.
Everything was sharp.
His eyes widened.
"My glasses."
Ha-eun smiled.
"You don't need them."
He stared at her.
"The doctors said your eyesight recovered years ago."
Ha-Kim slowly looked around the room.
Clear.
Everything was clear.
For years, his eyesight had been damaged.
But now—
he could see.
Ha-eun watched him.
"There was one more thing."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"What?"
She hesitated.
"The doctors said something strange happened after you went into the coma."
He waited.
"They couldn't explain it."
"What?"
She looked directly into his eyes.
"Your brain activity changed."
Ha-Kim remained silent.
Something inside him already knew.
He closed his eyes.
For the first time in years, he reached for the familiar feeling.
That strange place behind his eyes.
The place where the future used to wait.
Nothing.
He tried again.
Nothing.
No countdown.
No images.
No shadows.
No distant hour waiting to be seen.
Nothing.
Ha-Kim opened his eyes.
He stared at his hands.
"It's gone."
Ha-eun understood immediately.
"Your ability?"
He nodded.
"It's gone."
She watched him carefully.
"How do you feel?"
Ha-Kim thought about it.
Then—
he smiled.
Small.
Real.
"Free."
Ha-eun's eyes softened.
He didn't have to know anymore.
He didn't have to calculate.
Didn't have to wonder what the next hour would bring.
Didn't have to sacrifice pieces of himself trying to protect tomorrow.
For the first time since he was eighteen,
tomorrow was simply tomorrow.
Ha-eun leaned closer.
"There's something else."
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"What?"
"You still have secrets."
He blinked.
"So do you."
She smiled.
"Then I guess we're even."
Ha-Kim almost laughed.
Almost.
She rested her forehead against his.
"Tell me."
So he did.
He told her about the first time he saw the future.
About the first change.
The headaches.
The fear.
The cost.
The things he had hidden.
The visions he never understood.
The shadows.
The countdown.
The day he saw her die.
And the moment he chose to change it anyway.
Ha-eun listened to everything.
She didn't interrupt.
When he finished, she was quiet for a while.
Then she told him her story too.
Her childhood.
Her parents.
The streets.
Sa-ram.
The gang.
Everything she had been too ashamed to say.
Ha-Kim listened.
Neither of them looked away.
They finally knew everything.
Not just the parts they had been brave enough to share before.
Everything.
After a while, Ha-eun asked softly—
"Do you regret it?"
Ha-Kim knew what she meant.
The future.
The coma.
The ten years.
His ability.
He looked at her.
"No."
She blinked.
"Not even losing ten years?"
Ha-Kim smiled.
"I got you back."
Ha-eun's eyes filled with tears.
She laughed quietly.
"You really know how to make me cry."
"I wasn't trying to."
"I know."
She leaned forward and hugged him.
Ha-Kim wrapped his arms around her.
For ten years, she had waited.
Now she was here.
So was he.
And that was enough.
───
Later that day, Ha-Kim stood by the hospital window.
The city stretched beyond the glass.
Ten years had passed without him.
But the world hadn't stopped.
It had kept moving.
And now—
so would he.
Ha-eun came to stand beside him.
"You ready?"
Ha-Kim looked at her.
"For what?"
She smiled.
"Tomorrow."
He looked outside.
Then back at her.
There was no vision.
No countdown.
No warning.
No guarantee.
Just the person standing beside him.
Ha-Kim took her hand.
"Yeah."
He smiled.
"I'm ready."
And for the first time—
he didn't need to know what came next.
He only needed to live it.
